## Bug found and fixed: the rug detector was really a price detector (commit 02a2935)
- **Cause:** a constant-product pool's $ liquidity moves with sqrt(price). GENO's -77.5% dump alone gives
  sqrt(0.225) = 0.47, i.e. "-53% liquidity", so our 50% "liquidity pulled" rug exit fired with nothing pulled.
- **Evidence:** our exit showed $50,081 at price 0.000217. 34 minutes later the pool was back at $100,335 at 0.000812,
  near our entry. That is impossible after an LP pull.
- **Cost:** we sold at the bottom of the wick. Sold for $47; the same coins were worth about $175 at 11:09.
- **Fix:** rug = liquidity <= 50% of liq0 * sqrt(min(1, price/entry price)), i.e. only liquidity missing beyond what the
  price move explains.
  - Price rises don't raise the bar, because concentrated-liquidity pools don't grow like sqrt; so a pull on a pumping coin is caught exactly as before.
  - A real pull (both reserves drop) still exits at once.
- **Tests:** a GENO-shaped dump now holds; a dump followed by a 60% pull exits; a 5x pump followed by a 60% pull exits. All suites and pyflakes pass.
- **Note:** the rejected-coin follow-up uses the same naive rug metric (liq_min <= 20% of liq0) and a missing pair counts as $0.
  Its "rugged" counts are inflated (QNT counted as a rug). Fix it before trusting rejected_followup rug rates.
