Start with the stakes, because both teams are desperate. “For both programs, it’s a must-win game,” Central Michigan coach Matt Drinkall said this week. His Chippewas are 3-5 and have dropped three straight, the latest a 28-27 gut-punch at home to Miami (Ohio) on a touchdown with 1:41 left. Eastern Michigan, the designated home team in Detroit this year, is 4-5 after losses to Toledo and Ohio. The Eagles’ bowl hopes are fading, and so are Central’s. That’s usually when teams stop playing scared and start taking shots.
Who’s actually going to stop anybody?
This is where the total looks too low to me. Use the opponent-adjusted numbers from CollegeFootballData, and both defenses rank in the bottom quarter of the country in success rate allowed. In other words, both teams let offenses stay on schedule.
Eastern Michigan’s pass defense ranks 134th in EPA allowed per dropback, which is about as generous as it gets in the FBS. That matters more this week than it would have a month ago. Central Michigan used to be a run-first team with Angel Flores, but Flores suffered a season-ending injury, and Jadyn Glasser has brought balance. In his first start, Glasser went 18-of-31 for 221 yards and a touchdown against a good Miami defense, which was a season high for CMU through the air. Drinkall said the two quarterbacks are “as opposite as you could possibly be.”
