‘Bows offense
Since June Jones implemented his version of the four-wide offense in 1999, the slots have been targeted the most.
As part of the evolution, tight ends were added — mostly as check-down receivers and not the hybrid blocker/ receiver most offenses now employ. But in every UH offense the past three decades, there always were speedy wideouts to take the top off the defense.
This season, the Warriors have not developed deep consistency. Last season, Micah Alejado completed 35% of deep passes (airborne for at least 20 yards from the line of scrimmage); this year, the deep connections are down to 18.8%. Departures (Jackson Harris, Brandon White), injuries (Robert Haynes, Tre’ Griffiths, Audric Harris) and umbrella coverages have narrowed the long-ball options. Stanford and Wyoming showed pre-snap man coverages on the wideouts, then sent DBs and nickels into the deep routes.
“There’s a difference when you say you’re playing a man but there are a bunch of (defenders) in the man lanes,” receivers coach Jared Ursua said. “There’s a connectivity that’s needed that we have not established yet.”
In practices this week, wideouts Carson Brown and Devin Alves have worked on creating separation from clingy corners.
“It’s starting to come along,” said Alves, who has been GPS-clocked at 21.6 mph. Alves’ background as a high school quarterback is helping in understanding route concepts.
The Warriors also are tinkering with the pass protection. Dean Briski has moved back from right tackle to the left side, where he played last season. San Jose State transfer Denaris DeRosa has healed from a labrum injury suffered in the spring game to claim the right guard’s spot.
DeRosa spends hours each week studying videos.
“There’s always someone faster than you and stronger than you,” DeRosa said. “I want to control what I can control. Putting in the work with film and getting treatment, those are things in my control.”
Uluaki Taukiuvea brings athleticism as back-side protector for left-handed Alejado.
‘Bows defense
The overall good defensive effort has been hindered by a lack of big plays.
The Warriors have managed two picks and a fumble recovery in 45 drives. Opponents are averaging 7.48 yards on first down (5.90 on rushes), converting 45.28% on third down, and scoring at 85.71% in the red zone.
The Warriors have had to overcome injuries while rebuilding the interior line and secondary. A federal judge did not make it a priority to rule on the eligibility of cornerback Caleb “C-Bo” Brown. But rush end Lester Lagafuaina has recovered from a shoulder injury and was impactful in last week’s game against Wyoming.
After two injury-filled seasons, safety Kona Moore leads with 33 tackles. And dime linebacker Jamih Otis, who was limited to one series last week after suffering a rib ailment, was not listed on the preliminary injury report.
Mike linebacker Wynden Ho‘ohulil and Otis, who traded positions, have excelled in stabilizing the 4-2-5 defense. They also have served as mentors to linebacker understudies Matteus Ioane and Aisiah “Scoob” Paogofie. As Otis’ injury replacement, the 6-2, 225-pound Ioane played 41 snaps last week, exceeding his previous high of nine.
“He’s a guy who said from the start: ‘All I need is an opportunity,” recalled associate head coach Chris Brown, who coaches the linebackers.
Ahead of signing day on Feb. 7, 2024, Brown lobbied to make a gray-shirt offer to Ioane, an unheralded Leilehua linebacker.
“You watch him on film, he’s violent,” Brown recalled. “I said, ‘That’s the kind of guy I want. I want a violent guy.’ I’d rather have a player who plays with too much fire than a guy I’d have to start the fire.”
‘Bows specialists
For all the unique problems on special teams, ball security has not been one of them.
Tama Uiliata has fielded 13 punts — eight fair catches, five returns — without a muff. Titan Lacaden is averaging 41.5 yards on two kickoff returns.
The Warriors should receive a boost in field position with the return to Ching field, where they have figured out the impact of the northeasterly winds, especially in the fourth quarter.
