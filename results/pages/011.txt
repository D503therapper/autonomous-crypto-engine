ARIZONA (-2) vs New Orleans
Strong Opinion – First Half Under (21.5)
· The Cardinals have led for a league-low 3% of their offensive snaps and 49% of their points have come in the fourth quarter. Kliff Kingsbury’s scripted plays have been abominable, and they are consistently dependent on Kyler Murray to bail them out late in games.
· Arizona’s offense is averaging -0.238 EPA/play in the first half (31st) and leading the NFL at +0.118 EPA/play in second halves when Murray ditches Kingsbury’s game plan and starts to freestyle.
· Cardinals WR DeAndre Hopkins will make his season debut after serving his six-game performance-enhancing drug suspension, but the addition will be more than offset by WR Marquise Brown’s foot fracture. Hopkins finished 2nd on the team in yards per route run last season (1.76) to Christian Kirk’s 1.81. Brown was gaining 1.83 yards per route run in the slot this year (8th).
· Arizona’s front office traded for Robbie Anderson, but it was indicated he would be very limited on Thursday night if he is on the field at all. It’s a short week and Anderson was averaging only 1.16 yards per route run this season after gaining just 0.83 last year. He’s not going to make a major impact.
· New Orleans CB Paulson Adebo surrendered eight receptions last Sunday, but he will bounce back across from washed up Cardinals wide receiver AJ Green. The 34-year-old WR is averaging a league-low 0.32 yards per route run which is nearly half of the second worst average in the league (0.63).
· TE Zach Ertz has 35 receptions (3rd) but the Saints are allowing only 5.1 yards per target to tight ends (4th).
· Arizona’s major problem on offense is the interior of the offensive line. Guard Justin Pugh tore his ACL and it looks like center Rodney Hudson will be out another game. Backups Sean Harlow and Max Garcia surrendered nine total pressures in Seattle last Sunday.
· The Saints had nearly as many rushes (34) as dropbacks (37) last week and I think they will continue to be run-heavy with starting wide receivers Michael Thomas and Jarvis Landry sidelined.
· Andy Dalton will get rookie WR Chris Olave back in uniform on Thursday night. Olave is averaging 2.56 yards per route run (9th) but he will be contained by Arizona’s cornerback Marco Wilson, who conceded only three catches on four targets for 15 yards last week.
· Our model favors the Cardinals by 2.9 points, with a predicted total of just 41.2 points.
The first-half Under 21.5 is a Strong Opinion at -120 odds or better but the line has moved out of range at 21.

DALLAS (-7) vs Detroit
Lean – Over (49)
· Dak Prescott is set to return on Sunday in a favorable matchup versus a Lions defense surrendering 0.20 EPA per passing play (31st).
· Detroit’s games this season are averaging 62.0 combined points, the most in the NFL.
· The Lions nickelbacks are surrendering 1.77 yards per cover snap in the slot and Prescott will look on the inside to WR CeeDee Lamb, who is gaining 2.01 yards per route run in the slot (6th).
· Detroit’s rush defense is the worst in the league by our metrics and Dallas should be able to pound the rock if Prescott’s thumb is bothering him.
· The Cowboys lead the NFL with a 32% pressure rate, but the Lions have a solid offensive line and Jared Goff has only 3.6% sack rate (3rd).
· Detroit WR Amon-Ra St. Brown was on a snap count before the bye and was on the field for only 32% of the offensive plays, but he should be much healthier now with the extra week’s rest. St. Brown is gaining 2.30 yards per route run (12th).
· Our model favors the Cowboys by 9.7 points, with a predicted total of 50.1 points, but the Lions apply to a very good 100-32-3 ATS situations and bad teams that are struggling recently tend to play much better coming off their bye week. In fact, teams with a win percentage of 0.250 or worse that have lost 3 or more games in a row are 45-17 ATS after their bye week (6-0 the last two seasons).

CINCINNATI (-6.5) vs Atlanta
· Cincinnati’s defense is 5th in EPA allowed per passing play but Atlanta is running on a league-high 70.2% of first down snaps. The Falcons rank 6th in early down EPA/play.
· The Bengals are surrendering 5.6 yards per rush in the three games since interior defender DJ Reader went down. Reader had an 18.2% run stop percentage while backup Josh Tupou is currently at 2.1%.
· Marcus Mariota is averaging only 20.8 pass attempts per game over the last five weeks.
· Cincinnati’s defense is surrendering 8.0 yards per target to tight ends (25th) and Mariota will look to involve Kyle Pitts.
· Bengals CB Chidobe Awuzie ranks 15th in PFF coverage grade, and he will limit Drake London, who is averaging 2.23 yards per route run (13th).
· Joe Burrow just scored 30 points against a Saints defense with a mediocre pass rush, and he might do it again this week. New Orleans has just a 18% pressure rate (28th) and Atlanta’s defense last in pressure rate (12%).
· Ja’Marr Chase caught seven passes for 132 yards with two touchdowns last week and he will battle with Falcons’ cornerback AJ Terrell, who has 4 pass breakups (12th).
· Cincinnati WR Tee Higgins is gaining 2.13 yards per route run (17th) and he will go against backup CB Darren Hall with starter Casey Hayward likely requiring shoulder surgery.
· Atlanta’s nickelback Isaiah Oliver only allowed three catches for 21 yards into his coverage in his season debut last week and he should limit Tyler Boyd in the slot.
· Our model favors the Bengals by just 4.6 points, with a predicted total of 46.3 points, but the Falcons (6-0 ATS) apply to a 31-80-3 ATS contrary situation.

Lean – BALTIMORE (-6.5) vs Cleveland
· The Browns and Ravens are similar in terms of how they want to move the ball on offense. They establish the run and use tight ends frequently in the passing game. Baltimore’s defense is suited to stop this approach while Cleveland’s has not been.
· The Ravens have the 3rd-rated rush defense while the Browns rush defense ranks 31st according to our metrics.
· David Njoku (308) and Mark Andrews (299) have the most receiving yards among tight ends the last four weeks.
· Cleveland’s defense is surrendering 8.1 yards per target to TEs (26th) and Baltimore’s defense is allowing just 6.1 yards per target to tight ends (9th). However, Browns LB Deion Jones might make his debut this week and could plug the hole versus tight ends. Jones has been inconsistent in pass defense throughout his career but has finished inside the top 10 of PFF coverage grade three of the past five seasons.
· Ravens LT Ronnie Stanley has not conceded a single pressure in his two games, and he should protect Lamar Jackson against edge defender Myles Garrett, who ranks 5th in pass rushing efficiency.
· Baltimore’s passing game has suffered without WR Rashod Bateman, who was gaining 2.59 yards per route run (7th). Devin Duvernay is averaging 1.71 yards per route run and none of Baltimore’s other wide receivers are above 1 yards per route run. Bateman looks like he should be back in uniform this week.
· Ravens rookie cornerback Damarion Williams surrendered 3.64 yards per cover snap last week and a touchdown. Cleveland’s offense uses 11 personnel on only 45% of snaps (4th-fewest) so Williams shouldn’t hurt the Ravens’ secondary as much this week since he won’t be on the field too often.
· Browns Pro Bowl RG Wyatt Teller has been seen in a walking boot this week and he is likely out this Sunday. Teller is one of the most valuable guards in the NFL and is worth nearly a point.
· Our model favors the Ravens by 10.3 points, with a predicted total of 42.4 points, but the Browns apply to a 149-55-5 ATS situation that plays on teams after a blowout loss. I’ll still lean with Baltimore at less than 7 due to the line value, which in this case is worth more than the situation.

Green Bay (-4.5) vs WASHINGTON
Lean – Under (41.5)
· The Packers had 44.5% of their possessions to end in a score last season (7th) but are down to just 27.9% this year (31st).
· Green Bay’s offense ranks 24th in expected points added per play, but I expect them to turn it around at some point. Aaron Rodgers and Matt LaFleur are 10-1 against the spread the week a loss.
· The Packers offensive line will neutralize Washington’s pass rush. Commanders’ interior defender Jonathan Allen has 21 pressures (5th), but C Josh Myers leads the league in pass blocking efficiency and LG Jon Runyan ranks 3rd in pass blocking efficiency.
· Edge defender Montez Sweat ranks 17th in pass rushing efficiency, but LT David Bakhtiari looks nearly back to his All-Pro self and conceded only one hurry last Sunday.
· Green Bay’s wide receiver Randall Cobb likely suffered a high-ankle sprain. Cobb is gaining 2.21 yards per route run in the slot (5th) and is worth 0.4 points by our numbers.
· Carson Wentz will get surgery on his finger and miss at least a month. I don’t think there is a significant drop off from Wentz to backup Taylor Heinicke. Wentz is averaging 5.3 yppp and Heinicke averaged 5.9 yppp last season. However, Washington’s offensive line is much worse in 2022.
· Commander RT Samuel Cosmi ranks 9th-worst in pass blocking efficiency, and he will struggle across from edge defender Rashan Gary, who has 22 pressures (12th).
· Packers’ interior defender Kenny Clark ranks 2nd in pass rushing efficiency and will collapse pockets against backup RG Saahdiq Charles.
· Our model favors the Packers by 5.4 with a predicted total of 38.8.

Lean – DENVER (+1.5) over NY Jets
Note
: Denver was released as a 2-Star Best Bet at -1 and will be graded as such. If you played it then hope for Rypien to play at a decent level. I’d actually still lean with Denver at -1 even with Rypien so there is no need to get out of the play at a worse line.
· Denver has downgraded Russell Wilson to out for this week, which means that Brett Rypien will get the start. Wilson hasn’t played well this season but Rypien is projected to be about 2 points worse than Drew Lock was last season, which would be a 4.5 points adjustment for Denver’s offense this season. That’s in line with the market, as Denver would have been favored by 3 or 3.5 with Wilson and the line has moved to +1.5 and +1.
With that being the case, the model now favored Denver by 0.3 points and the Jets’ still apply to negative 47-124-5 ATS situation based on last week’s upset win over the Packers (and a 277-424-17 ATS contrary angle) and the Broncos are still the side.
· Zach Wilson is 3-0 in his 3 starts but he ranks 27th in EPA/play, 36th in success rate, and 34th in completion percentage over expectation.
· New York’s offensive coordinator Mike LaFleur clearly doesn’t believe in Wilson. The Jets have been 10%, 5%, and 6% below pass rate expectation over the past three games.
· Corey Davis leads New York’s receivers, averaging 1.78 yards per route run, but he will be shut down by Denver cornerback Patrick Surtain, who has allowed just 147 yards on 38 targets this season (3.9 YPT). Surtain blanketed Chargers WR Mike Williams last week, yielding only one reception for four yards.
· Alijah Vera-Tucker is playing out of position at right tackle for the Jets and he will struggle versus Broncos’ edge defender Baron Browning, who ranks 8th in pass rushing efficiency.
· The Jets are in a huge letdown spot this week, as they qualify in a negative 47-124-5 ATS situation based on last week’s upset win over the Packers. Also, the market starts to overreact this time of the season to teams with an ATS win percentage that is 0.333 or more higher than their opponent and the Jets apply to a 277-424-17 ATS situation that is based on that ATS percentage differential. The Broncos have my highest technical analysis rating (a 5) and 5 ‘Tech’ ratings are 6-1 so far this season and 61% over many years.
· Our model favors the Broncos by 0.3 points with Rypien at QB for the Broncos, with a predicted total of 37.9 points, and the situation is still strongly in favor of Denver.

Lean – LA CHARGERS (-5.5) over Seattle
· Justin Herbert posted a 5.9-yard average depth of target last Monday night and the Chargers really don’t have any receivers to stretch the field without Jalen Guyton. However, reinforcements are coming to the short passing game for Herbert, and he should be able to crush Seattle’s defense in that area.
· Star slot receiver Keenan Allen will likely be back on the field for the first game since week 1. Seahawks nickelback Coby Bryant allowed -1 yards on 7 targets against the Cardinals and the rookie could be improving. However, Bryant was surrendering 2.05 yards per cover snap in the slot (4th-worst) before last week.
· The Chargers are the only offense targeting RBs on more than 30% of pass attempts and Seahawks are the only defense surrendering more than 8 yards per target to running backs.
· The Chargers only managed one touchdown last week against Denver. Seattle’s defense has forced a punt on a league-worst 24% of opponent drives and Los Angeles should find the endzone on at least three possessions this week.
· Chargers Pro Bowl cornerback JC Jackson was benched versus the Broncos, and he has the worst PFF coverage grade this season. Backup CB Michael Davis is allowing just 6.9 yards per target, but DK Metcalf will be a challenge. Metcalf is gaining 2.09 yards per route run (19th).
· Geno Smith will also be looking to his tight ends, who have a 26% target share (5th-highest). The Los Angeles defense allows 9.2 yards per target to tight ends (3rd-worst).
· Tyler Lockett is averaging 1.82 yards per slot route run (9th) but he will be shut down by Chargers nickelback Bryce Callahan, who is conceding league-low 0.48 yards per cover snap in the slot.
· Our model favors the Chargers by 7.5 with a predicted total of 48.5.

MIAMI (-7) vs Pittsburgh
· Steelers rookie quarterback Kenny Pickett got a concussion in the third quarter against the Buccaneers and Mitchell Trubisky led Pittsburgh to a victory. It looks like Pickett will be back on Sunday, but there isn’t much separating the two QBs regardless.
· The Steelers beat the Buccaneers despite missing four starters in the secondary and All-Pro edge defender TJ Watt. It looks like S Minkah Fitzpatrick, CB Levi Wallace and CB Cam Sutton will be back for this game which will go a long way into containing Miami’s wide receivers. Tyreek Hill already has three games with 150+ receiving yards with his new team.
· However, Pittsburgh’s nickelback Arthur Maulet is surrendering 12.3 yards per target. Jaylen Waddle is gaining 2.69 yards per route run (5th) and Tua Tagovailoa will look for him on the inside when lined up against Maulet.
· The Dolphins are averaging 7.2 yppp (4th) despite starting three different quarterbacks in six games which speaks to the quality of Mike McDaniel’s scheme.
· Steelers interior defender Cameron Heyward is up to 18 pressures (11th) after abusing Tampa Bay’s rookie LG Luke Goedeke last week. It might be much of the same for Heyward on Sunday night versus Dolphins LG Liam Eichenberg, who ranks 6th-worst in pass blocking efficiency.
· It’s surprising to see Pittsburgh’s offensive line leading the league in pass blocking efficiency and Miami’s defense has just a 15.7% pressure rate this season (28.5% last season).
· Chase Claypool was having a disappointing year through the first five games with just 129 yards, but he gained 96 yards last week and should keep it going on Sunday night. Dolphins nickelback Kader Kohou is allowing 1.89 yards per cover snap in slot (3rd-worst).
· Our model favors the Dolphins by 6.4 points, with a predicted total of 46.8 points. The math leans over but the Steelers are 77-49-2 Under on the road under coach Mike Tomlin (2-1 UN this season). Pittsburgh also applies to a 40-99-4 ATS letdown situation that is based on last week’s upset win over Tampa. I used the Dolphins in my spread pool.
