“That trophy is coveted, certainly by us and the other academies, as much as any tangible piece of hardware or gold that we can get,” Monken said. “It really is the ultimate measuring stick for our program: how we stack up against the other academies.”
The Falcons face complications, having lost quarterback Liam Szarka in the victory over Navy and going into this week with an unknown at the spot. Evan Medders started the first three games after Szarka’s injury, but he missed last week’s victory over UConn with a turf toe injury. There’s a chance he returns. Or that Kemper Hodges, who ran for 200 yards and four touchdowns against UConn, handles the position.
Air Force has not explored the passing game with any of the quarterbacks who have played since Szarka, but a one-dimensional game plan would seem unlikely against an Army squad well versed at stopping the option.
This chess match will take place under the backdrop of the biggest stakes the Falcons — already bowl eligible — are likely to see the rest of the season. There’s still a chance Air Force, with help, could play its way into the Mountain West championship game. But outside of that, there won’t be anything that will come close to carrying this kind of emotional investment.
