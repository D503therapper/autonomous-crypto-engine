The Roadrunners lead the FBS in penalty yardage this season, but they also rank in the top 10 in opponent penalties, showing one reason why the style comes with plenty of benefits on top of the obvious drawbacks.
"We are pushing it. We are living on the edge," Traylor said. "We want to play a physical style of football that people don't want any part of."
No team has been whistled for more yards of penalties than UTSA's 83.3 yards per game this season, and the 8.5 flags per game thrown against UTSA is tied for No. 130 out of 138 FBS teams.
But UTSA also ranks No. 9 in the FBS in opponent penalty yardage at 71.4 per game, sitting second in opponent penalty calls with an average of 9.0.

UTSA has had more penalty yards than its opponent in seven out of 10 games, and the Roadrunners have had more penalties called on them in five out of 10 games, with one tie.
"I know we get some penalties sometimes, I get it," Traylor said. "We just play with so much freaking passion, and it's really fun to watch our kids play so hard."
That being said, Traylor added that his leniency has limits. Certain calls before the play including substation infractions, false starts or offsides are still problematic, as are personal fouls and other situations that arise after the whistle.
Limiting those scenarios is something Traylor said the Roadrunners have "got to do a better job of coaching."
"There are some that are unacceptable," Traylor said. "You cannot jump offsides on defense or offense. Pre-snap penalties and post-snap penalties, no bueno. It just can't happen."
