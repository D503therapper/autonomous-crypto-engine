Nobody predicted that Weber State would be tied for the top spot in the Big Sky going into Week 8. That's exactly where the Wildcats find themselves after a 4-1 start in conference play under head coach Eric Kjar. The Wildcats are on a three-game winning streak, which includes wins over Idaho State and Idaho. This will be their biggest test of the season, but we've seen some insane upsets this year. Can the Wildcats find a way to stun the Bobcats in Bozeman?
On the other side, Montana State has started to get healthy, which could be a major issue for the rest of the FCS. The Bobcats looked dominant in a win over Eastern Washington last weekend, moving to 6-1 overall with a 36-point win on the road in Cheney. With the return of Justin Lamson, Adam Jones, and Caden Dowler, this team has a real chance to remind the country exactly who they are over the next few games. We'll see if Taco Dowler makes a return this weekend, but regardless, the Bobcats have plenty of dangerous weapons for Lamson to utilize offensively.
This game will be determined by Weber State's ability to get Montana State's offense off the field. That's much easier said than done, but the Wildcats are not going to be able to win a shootout on the road against this team. Weber State's defense has been average most of the year, allowing over 34 points per game and nearly 6.0 yards per play.
As expected, Montana State has been elite at running the football, surpassing the 220-yard mark in three consecutive games. Despite missing some time due to injury, Adam Jones still leads the Bobcats with 408 rushing yards and 8 TDs, while Jared White and Colson Coon have combined for 476 yards and 4 TDs behind him. Coon was unavailable last week, but should be able to return this weekend.
My biggest concern for Weber State is the lack of consistency in the passing game, which has been held to under 175 yards in each of the past two games. I don't think the Wildcats are going to be able to be one-dimensional against this defense, which is going to put some pressure on Nate Dahle to make some plays downfield.
Since taking over the starting job, Dahle has only completed 50% of his passes for 1,591 passing yards, 16 passing TDs, and 10 INTs. The turnovers are another worry, because the Bobcats will make Dahle pay if he forces the ball into dangerous situations. The Bobcats have 9 INTs this season, which ranks 2nd in the Big Sky.
Weber State has already exceeded all expectations this season, but there are levels to this. I think this is where the Wildcats run into a machine, which is a healthy Montana State team in Bozeman. I like the Bobcats to win this one by multiple scores at home.
No. 6 North Dakota at No. 8 South Dakota
This is a massive MVFC matchup that could have real postseason seeding implications for both teams. North Dakota is coming off a physical win over South Dakota State, but can they carry that momentum on the road into the DakotaDome, where the Coyotes have been dominant this season?
South Dakota sits at 6-2 overall and has won three consecutive games since its loss to Youngstown State. The Coyotes ranked 8th in the playoff committee's first rankings, but they are still searching for a real signature win for their resume. They have a big opportunity this weekend to do that, plus both teams are looking to remain in a tight MVFC race, which features five teams with one conference loss.
The biggest storyline is whether Charles Pierre Jr. will be fully healthy after sitting out last week's game against Murray State. He will reportedly return this weekend, but the Coyotes need him to be 100% in this game, especially since the run game will be an important key to the game if South Dakota is going to win. Pierre leads the Coyotes with 585 rushing yards and 7 rushing TDs.
This South Dakota offense works best when Austyn Modrzewski can work off the play action, where he averages 11.4 yards per attempt, compared to 7.9 on his other pass attempts. Modrzewski is one of the most talented freshman quarterbacks I've seen at this level, completing 66.5% of his passes for 1,734 yards, 22 passing TDs, and 6 INTs. This is a big test for the talented freshman, which is why the Coyotes will want to put him in good situations with a strong run game.
North Dakota's defense has been elite against the run this season, allowing only 2.9 yards per carry in conference play. South Dakota needs to avoid 2nd/3rd-and-long situations because this North Dakota defense can really get after the quarterback, posting 11 sacks in only 4 conference games. Lance Rucker is finally back and led this team with 7 pressures last week in his first start of the year.
The other thing I'll be watching is whether South Dakota's defense can keep North Dakota's offense behind the chains and off schedule. It's something SDSU was unable to do consistently last week, but the Coyotes are going to have to generate some negative plays and find creative ways to get after Jerry Kaminski.
Kaminski has only been pressured on 27% of his dropbacks, but has not played well when teams have been able to generate some pressure. He's only completing 47.5% of his passes for 5.2 yards per attempt when pressured. South Dakota has not been great at getting after opposing quarterbacks, which cost them against Youngstown State. The Coyotes need to find production out of Chris Dixon, Nathan Laperi, or Isaiah Wray.
The Fighting Hawks feature a solid 1-2 punch in the backfield with Charles Langama and Matt Lawson. The duo has combined for over 900 rushing yards and 9 TDs, led by Langama with over 7.9 yards per carry. The Coyotes are allowing 4.9 yards per carry in conference play, but have been solid outside of an outlier performance against Youngstown State.
I am concerned about how North Dakota is able to respond after an emotional, physical win over South Dakota State. Plus, the Coyotes have looked like a different team inside the DakotaDome, but I still can't overlook the matchup on the field. I'm going with North Dakota in another physical MVFC battle, trusting the Fighting Hawks to get some key defensive stops to secure the win on the road.
Prediction: North Dakota (24-21)
