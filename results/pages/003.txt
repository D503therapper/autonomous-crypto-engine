TITLE: No. 13/16 Harvard Set for Spooky Showdown Against Dartmouth On Friday | The Harvard Crimson
DATES SEEN: []

Since a sluggish career debut, sophomore quarterback Charlie Smith has been all gas and no brakes, averaging 276.4 yards per game with an 11-3 TD-INT ratio over the last five games. Across the entire season, the sophomore carries the second-best completion percentage of any signal caller in the country (72.3 percent), and his completion rate is almost 10 percentage points higher than the next best in the Ancient Eight.
On the ground, six different Harvard skill players have double-digit rushing attempts on the season, spreading the wealth for a team that averages 128.5 rushing yards per game. New to the already deep running back room is junior DJ Gordon, who played his first game of the season against Princeton last week after being the team’s second-leading rusher in 2025.
“No one has these flashy, super outstanding stats, but it’s everyone, and that’s what’s making us super successful right now,” said captain running back Xaviah Bascon. “We have so many playmakers, so many guys that can all make many kinds of plays, and that just makes us so dynamic.”
Protecting Smith and the running backs is an offensive line with two of the Crimson’s Midseason All-Americans: seniors Spencer Doan and Thomas O’Brien. The group has limited disruption in the backfield all season, allowing just one and a half sacks per game despite persistent injuries causing rotations along the line.
We came into the season knowing in every position that we had great depth, and the o-line obviously was one of those,” Bascon said. “They’ve been incredible communication-wise, new guys in different roles and stuff, and they’ve just worked super hard. And it starts in practice, starts in film, and all those things have come along, and guys have stepped up and been great.”
Dartmouth started its season by losing back-to-back games in which it led after three quarters. In its season opener, the Big Green fell 24-20 to then-No. 7 Lehigh. Despite making it inside the Mountain Hawks’ 30-yard line twice in the last three minutes of regulation, Dartmouth’s offense was unable to execute down the stretch.
After falling to Monmouth 31-28 in its second game, the Big Green picked up a tight conference win over Penn before losing to Yale 28-7 in a game that saw the Bulldogs gain 537 yards and 30 first downs. Dartmouth quickly bounced back in its final non-conference game with a 27-21 win over Merrimack in which it scored 13 unanswered fourth-quarter points.
