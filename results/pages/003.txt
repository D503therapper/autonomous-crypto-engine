When campus empties out for Thanksgiving break, not every student goes home. Roomie Pride Pantry is setting the table for those who stay behind.
Southeastern will be closed Nov. 25-27, including on-campus dining locations and stores. Some international and out-of-state students, student-athletes, students with complicated home lives, and those wanting to catch up on their studies remain on campus. Roomie’s Pride Pantry will offer meals, support and opportunities for students to spend time together throughout the break.
Before the break, on Monday, Nov. 23, the food pantry will host Lionsgiving with the Student Government Association to serve a Thanksgiving meal and share information about upcoming resources. On Tuesday, Nov. 24, students can go on a grocery trip to stock up on what they need for the break.
On Wednesday, Nov. 25, a hot meal will be provided at the Baptist Collegiate Ministry (501 W. Dakota St.) and coffee and bagels will be served at the Wesley Foundation (307 W. Dakota St.,) from 8 to 10 a.m. On Thursday, Nov. 26, a Thanksgiving meal will be served at the Wesley Foundation from noon to 4 p.m. In addition, emergency food bags are always available in partnership with the University Police Department, which is always on call and ready to go to the pantry if a student needs them.
Thais Lindemayer Gomes, the coordinator of international programs and student well-being, runs Roomie’s Pride Pantry. She said the meals give students a chance to meet people from the community and spend time with fellow students during the break.
“We also usually have a pool table and ping pong table and then hot meals. It is nice because they get to sit down and actually share a meal with other people without having to rush, without having to necessarily grab a to-go box,” Gomes said.
The pantry works with volunteers who serve the meals to accommodate students’ dietary restrictions.
“Sharing a table is almost like a common ground. Everyone comes together, we’re sharing the meal and it is also a way to put our differences away,” Gomes said.
Over time, as students visit the pantry and attend off-campus hot meal times, they build connections with staff and other students. Gomes said that beyond receiving food, students can create a supportive network through these interactions.
The university sends out email reminders to students so they are aware of the resources available.
“Food insecurity is invisible and it’s hard to know if a student is facing food insecurity because they can look perfectly fine, but maybe inside they’re not,” Gomes said.
Roomie’s Pride Pantry is always looking for student organizations to help serve meals and accepts donations like rice, pasta, bread, snacks and refrigerated items during office hours.
“Hammond is a great community and Southeastern does care about their students, so if you’re staying here, make sure you come to the meals,” Gomes said.

The Lions were tied for first in the SLC with Stephen F. Austin and defeated the Lumberjacks 3-1 (25-23, 20-25, 25-21, 25-15) at the UC Thursday night, putting them in prime position for the regular-season finale against Lamar.

Before the team faced off against Lamar, seniors Keira Beverly, Ainsley Driska, Kyra McKelvey, Mina Petrovic, Lindsey Wickersham and Carissa Young were honored with their families alongside them on the court.
