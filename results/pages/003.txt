This offseason, Baylor acquired a player with tremendous boom-or-bust potential in D.J. Lagway, a high five-star recruit out of high school who had endured notable consistency issues through his first few collegiate seasons.
Lagway, an elite tier recruit out of high school as the top quarterback prospect of the 2024 class and the No. 3 in overall recruit, began at Florida before moving on to Baylor this past offseason.
As a high school recruit, Lagway was rated higher than fellow 2024 prospects such as CJ Carr, Julian Sayin and Colin Simmons. With the Gators, Lagway made 19 starts across 24 game appearances, completing 62% of his passes for 4,179 yards with 28 touchdowns to 23 interceptions, with 237 net rushing yards and a touchdown.
Now with Baylor, Lagway threw for 333 yards and two touchdowns, with one interception, in the team’s season-opening loss to Auburn, then missed two games with injury before returning last week to pass for 221 yards and two touchdowns, with one interception, in a win over Colorado.
Lagway has struggled with accuracy in two games this season, as he’s completed just 49.5 percent of his 93 pass attempts.
In Lagway’s absence, Nate Bennett stepped in and statistically was more effective than Lagway — albeit against much lesser competition — as Bennett threw for 314 yards with three passing touchdowns, one rushing touchdown and two interceptions against Prairie View, and then he tossed for 253 passing yards with three passing touchdowns and one rushing touchdown in a win over Louisiana Tech.
At running back, Dawson Pendergrass has rushed 73 times for 287 yards with one touchdown along with five receptions for 29 yards. A fourth-year player in the Baylor program, Pendergrass rushed for 88 yards in the season opener against Auburn and 85 yards last weekend against Colorado. He missed all of last season due to injury but had three 100-yard rushing outings for Baylor across the 2023-24 seasons.
The Baylor wide receivers group, which lost all its top performers to graduation from last year, is guided by Gavin Freeman, with a massive gap in production between Freeman and the next leading receivers in Jayden McGowan and Louis Brown IV.
Through four games, Freeman has 35 receptions for 364 yards with two touchdowns. Freeman, who transferred to Baylor this offseason after spending the 2022-23 seasons with Oklahoma and 2024-25 at Oklahoma State, is currently tied for the national lead in total receptions and ranks 22nd in total receiving yards.
Freeman started the 2025 season with a pair of double-digit reception totals as he caught 12 passes for 154 yards with a touchdown against Auburn and then had 10 grabs for 104 yards against Prairie View, followed by seven receptions against Louisiana Tech and six against Colorado.
Make no mistake about it, though there has been quite a bit of personnel turnover from last season to this year, Baylor will attack the football, as the Bears currently are tied for fourth nationally in team sacks (15.0) and rank fourth in team tackles for loss (36.0), while also ranking 28

ASU’s offensive line has had notable performance and injury issues through the season’s first three games, and this weekend does not appear to provide much relief as the Devils undoubtedly will be heavily tested by the Baylor defensive attack.
Baylor Special Teams Preview
Rhett Armstrong handles placekicking duties and kickoffs and has connected on 8-of-10 field goals so far this year with a long of 54 yards.
Baylor boasts arguably the nation’s best punter in veteran Palmer Williams, who enters this game with an incredible career average of 47.0 yards on 121 punts.
Overall Summary
Though this game matches up a pair of teams with winning records and each with just one non-conference loss on the season, some less-than-thrilling play for both squads truly makes this a greatly important contest with some tangible pressure for both teams.
In this contest, Baylor likely will lean upon its attacking defense to try to stifle the Sun Devil offense, just as Kansas was able to do two weeks ago despite ASU leaving London with the victory in that game.
In multiple respects, Saturday’s game could have similarities to that international exchange between the Sun Devils and the Jayhawks, as ASU likely will again seek to claim control of the game by forcing turnovers and other inefficiencies out of the Baylor offense, while the Devils will have to find new ways to protect the quarterback and allow the offense to flow at its own pace and not have such limited offensive options as was the case against Kansas.
ASU’s defense should have several advantages over the Baylor offense, but the game could be decided by how the Sun Devils have been able to use the bye week to find new ways to create opportunities for its offense.
