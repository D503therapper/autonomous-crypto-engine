San Diego State injury report: 4 players trending in opposite directions ahead of Oregon State
The Aztecs get encouraging updates at two positions, but two wide receivers are trending in the wrong direction
Bernie Wilson
|
San Diego State Aztecs
There were some significant moves on the
San Diego State Aztecs’
Pac-12 availability report on Thursday evening, two involving players who were upgraded and two involving players who were downgraded.
The upgraded players were tight end Jackson Ford, who moved from questionable to probable, and right guard Mikey Watkins, who also moved from questionable to probable.
Watkins missed the last two games after starting the second and third games.
Ford missed the last game, a thrilling comeback win against Texas State in the Pac-12 opener, after starting three of the first four games.
Two wide receivers are trending downward: Donovan Brown was moved from probable to questionable, while Nate Acevedo was moved from questionable to doubtful.
Brown started the first two and last two games while missing the third game.
Acevedo has started all five games at slot receiver.
Injuries and illness continue to test SDSU's depth
The Aztecs have been hard hit by injuries and a mumps outbreak over the last several weeks. This week’s availability report lists 35 players, up from 32 last week.
The availability reports are posted on the Pac-12 website starting three days before game day and updated the following two days and then again two hours before kickoff.
While the reports list the players, they do not indicate the injury or illness affecting them.
The mumps outbreak has affected more than a dozen players. It appears to have hit the tight end room the hardest, so Ford being upgraded is good news. There are still four tight ends listed as questionable, doubtful or out.
Other notable players
The two big names on the report, of course, are running backs Lucky Sutton and Javion “Chop” Kinnard.
They remain listed as questionable after missing the Texas State game with injuries. They both had to be helped off the field.
Sutton aggravated a high ankle sprain suffered in a loss at UCLA on Sept. 12 that forced him to miss the following week’s home loss to James Madison.
Kinnard replaced Sutton in the JMU game and went off for a remarkable 251 all-purpose yards, including 181 yards rushing and 40 yards receiving.
With both players out last Saturday, true freshman Josiah Lucas rushed for 111 yards and two touchdowns in the win over Texas State.
