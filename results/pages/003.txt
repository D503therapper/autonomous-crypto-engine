TITLE: Penn Football Preview: Quakers Host Ivy League Favorite Yale | Philadelphia Sports Now
DATES SEEN: ['October 22, 2026']

The University of Pennsylvania Football team will return to action at 7 p.m. on Friday, Oct. 23, hosting the Yale Bulldogs in a bid to pick up their first Ivy League win. The Quakers (2-3, 0-2 Ivy League) dropped a razor-thin decision to Columbia last week after
sideline miscommunication heading into a last-second field goal try
; their road won’t get any easier against Yale (3-2, 1-1 Ivy League), especially if uncertainty remains at quarterback.
Karson Siqueiros-Lasky took snaps for Penn last week with freshman starter Miles Teodecki out due to a knee injury. Siqueiros-Lasky opened a new dimension to the Quakers’ passing game but also threw four back-breaking interceptions; Teodecki was listed as a game-time decision last week, indicating he’s close to returning to game speed, but head coach Rick Santos said after the win that he wants to protect the young quarterback rather than rushing him back into the line of fire.
Penn enters play as a +4.5 underdog, their money line odds of +140 giving a roughly 40 percent chance of winning in this home matchup on ESPNU.
Scouting the Bulldogs
Junior quarterback Dante Reno is the son of former Yale head coach Tony Reno, who died on Oct. 2 of Creutzfeldt-Jakob disease. The Renos led the Bulldogs to an Ivy League championship in 2025 and entered the season as the favorites to go back-to-back; Dante Reno has maintained his high level of play, leading the Bulldogs in passing with 1,182 yards, 13 touchdowns and just two interceptions on 95-of-157 passing. Despite coming close to matching the Quakers’ total passing output, Reno has played just four games this season after dealing with an elbow injury early on.
His top targets are Jaxon Santiago (38 catches for 584 yards and eight touchdowns) and Lucius Anderson (34 catches for 417 yards and two scores); the Bulldogs also have a prolific rushing offense behind sophomore running back Hugo Djeumeni Kemeni, who is averaging 183 yards on the ground over his past three games, a testament to how dangerous Yale is with Reno under center.
Yale’s defense boasts 32 tackles for loss — a bad sign for Penn’s shaky running game — but struggles to get home to the quarterback, averaging just 1.2 sacks per game. They’ve forced four turnovers through five games, so the Quakers have a chance to get back to playing clean football after a number of brutal throws against Columbia.
