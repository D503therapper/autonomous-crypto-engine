Notebook: Injuries, MSU’s bad fumble luck, WMU tight ends shining, EMU visits Ohio’s fortress
Lots of injury news to stay on top of, plus notes on the Broncos tight ends, EMU visiting a difficult play to win and how MSU is hunting fumble recoveries.
Andrew Graham
22 Oct 2026
—
Of six times that Michigan State’s opponent has put the ball on the deck, the Spartans have recovered just one, against Eastern Michigan early in the second half. The 16.7 percent recovery rate from MSU’s defense is better than just four teams, three of which haven’t recovered a fumble all year.
“I'm not going to say that because I don't want to, like, say things that are coming down to luck,” Rossi said. “But, yeah, like, I think sometimes those things come in bunches, where like you're close, close, close, and then all of a sudden, boom, all of a sudden, you got — one's bouncing to your area.”
And while six opponent fumbles is not a particularly large denominator, the Spartans feel like it’s been a fair share of missed opportunities in not scooping up fumbles by the opposing offense.
Take what happened when MSU did recover one against EMU. After Harold Mack fumbled across midfield on what was going to be a third down conversion and MSU recovered, the Spartans offense marched for a touchdown. That helped pile on and put MSU up 28-7 after an opening drive touchdown out of halftime from the offense.
The Spartans drill fumble recoveries and punch outs in a number of ways, but some of it does come down to good bounces and the cliches about success being contagious — one fumble recovery could open the floodgates.
“I think, you know, you just gotta get one,” defensive lineman Eli Coenen said.

Levicki is currently third amongst Bronco receivers with 145 yards and 12 catches, with the recent touchdown and Parks isn’t far behind with 10 catches for 98 yards and the two scores. Toss in Ryan Brotherton with three catches for 40 yards and a score, too.
While those stats might be relatively uninspiring on their face, it’s worth considering the WMU offense has been one of the lower-volume passing offenses in the country and that Parks, Levicki and Brotherton have accounted for roughly a quarter of WMU’s passing yards and half of Lowry’s passing touchdowns have gone to the tight ends.
But on top of their prowess to catch a pass or two, the group has been a definitive plus in the run game, particularly the tight ends who haven’t caught a pass: Enese Tonga and Brennan Wooten, who have delivered relentlessly for the Broncos in the run game.
“Wooten, Brotherton, Enese, all three of those guys who we don't talk about a ton played really well,” Taylor said. “I thought Wooten probably played his best game as a Bronco. He was really physical, got a ton of movement. And I just thought, one of the things that stood out to me about Wooten was just the strain to finish down field. I showed several clips yesterday, from our game, to the team in our team meeting and he was on a couple of those and it was just really him blocking his man all the way to the whistle. The strain and effort to finish, that his man wasn't going to make the play.”
WMU even got into some 14 personnel (four tight ends) to run the ball vs. Central Michigan, and while there were quips about 15 personnel, the Broncos wouldn’t be wrong to want to build the plane out of tight ends with the way the group has played.
