November 19, 2026
At this point in the season, NCAA FCS teams are in a win-or-go-home situation. For FIU’s Willie Simmons, who was previously a coach at Florida A&M, he knows all about postseason football and how one loss will end your season. Although the FBS Panthers are set to play one more game this weekend, regardless of the outcome, another defeat would mean the team will not go bowling in 2026 and will finish below .500, a step back from the previous season.
“How will this team respond with our backs against the wall, as far as bowl eligibility, knowing that we haven’t won a road game this season and many guys not having the seasons that they had thought they would have going into it? How will we respond?” Simmons asked during his Tuesday media availability. “What type of team will we take to Atlanta? That’s going to say a lot about this current 2026 team and the foundation that we’ve laid moving forward, so I’m excited.”

INJURY UPDATES
Shamir Sterlin, the Panthers safety, is “highly doubtful” for Saturday’s game, Simmons said. In the fourth quarter of FIU’s loss to Delaware last week, Sterlin suffered a “significant” quad strain chasing down Blue Hens quarterback Nick Minicucci on the big fourth-down conversion.
Sterlin has played in nine games this season, recording 30 total tackles (19 solo/11 assisted) and two interceptions. The Panthers redshirt sophomore has dealt with injuries this season, even trying to play through them, as he did for the LIU and Jacksonville State games, but wasn’t able to and failed to provide much help in those contests. Sterlin could play in the season-finale against Sam Houston State, but it seems unlikely at this point.
Another injury worth noting is wide receiver Greg Gaines III, who is likely to miss the rest of the season with a shoulder injury he suffered against Liberty. If he does not play another game this season, Gaines will finish his FIU tenure with 10 receptions for 132 yards and no touchdowns.
Expectations were high for the Rhode Island transfer, who was coming off a 1,000-yard receiving season, but just wasn’t able to match that production at the FBS level.
FRESHMAN EMERGENCE
Two names to keep an eye on in these final two games are running back Jayden “Mook” Ford and safety Jaden McMillan.
Ford, a running back who came out of Miami Central, got a late start to the season due to an injury that he suffered over the offseason. He didn’t make his collegiate debut until the fourth game of the season against LIU, where he ran for 11 yards on one carry. He has since played in seven games, rushing for 70 yards on three carries and catching four passes for 57 yards.
“We all see Mook growing up in front of our eyes,” Simmons said. “He’s a lightning in a bottle. When he touches the ball, good things typically happen. For us, it’s about finding more ways to get him the ball. It’s about being able to get him out in space and to let him grow and develop as a football player. … His role will continue to increase because he’s a player who has an extremely bright future here.”
As for McMillan, he has played in every single game this season, but Saturday marked the first time he started at safety for the Panthers and recorded five total tackles. McMillan has also been starting to get more action at that safety spot, whether because of injury or just practicing well enough to earn it. FIU defensive coordinator Jovan Dewitt even said that he hasn’t noticed a freshman out there.
“He’s gotten in at some critical times, and I’m going to be honest with you, I didn’t know if he was in or not,” Dewitt said. “I think for a freshman, that’s a really good thing. I really think that’s a positive thing because what you worry about if there’s a freshman that goes in and you’re like, ‘oh hell, like what’s he going to do?’ And he operated just as if he was a vet. For me, that’s one of the most encouraging things I can say.”
With Sterlin out, McMillan will play a key role in the Panthers’ defense against a team that averages 228.8 passing yards per game, the third-best marker in Conference USA.
TWO-QUARTERBACK DILEMMA
To start the season, Kennesaw State had Rickie Collins starting. He completed 165 passes for 1,687 yards and seven touchdowns. He also ran for 321 yards and six touchdowns on 87 carries. Collins has played in nine games, but an injury limited him in the win over Delaware, and he missed the Owls ‘ most recent game against Sam Houston State.
Backing Collins up has been Mississippi Gulf Coast transfer Landon Varnes. In five games this season, he has completed 56 passes for 589 yards and two touchdowns. Varnes started against Sam Houston State, completing 22 passes for 181 yards and a touchdown.
“They got the guy that played most of the year, number one, who’s really athletic,” Dewitt said. “They have a whole bunch of different quarterback run games. There’s stretch action going one way and a quarterback counter going back the other. We call those wrong-way runs. There’s a little bit more of that as opposed to the guy who played last week. Right now, that run game is a little bit more limited.
“It’s a little bit more of a passing-game scenario. It’s not similar, but it’s the same thought process when we went to play USF. We didn’t know if they were going to be the 11-personnel spread team or the 12-personnel team. We had to have almost two game plans ready to go.”
