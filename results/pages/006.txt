Tony Tellez takes a long look at UConn traveling all the way to Laramie to face Wyoming in an early kickoff. The Huskies are 6-5 and favored by a field goal on the road, while the 7-4 Cowboys sit at home as a short underdog with the total set at 45.5. Between the altitude and the body-clock start, Tony likes the home dog. His free college football picks lean to Wyoming plus the points.
The spot screams home dog
Start with the situation, because it’s a big one. UConn is an East Coast team flying all the way to Laramie for what amounts to a 10 a.m. local kickoff, and Wyoming plays at the highest elevation of any of the 138 FBS sites. That combination of a long trip, an early body-clock start, and thin mountain air has tripped up a lot of visitors over the years. Asking a travel-weary team to also lay points in that environment is a lot.
Wyoming has earned respect at home, too. The Cowboys are 4-1 in Laramie and holding opponents to just 12 points a game on their own field. When a home team defends like that and the visitor has to deal with altitude and an early whistle, I want the points with the team that lives there, not the one getting off a plane.
History backs the spot, too. Teams making the long western trip into altitude for an early kickoff routinely come out sluggish in the first half, and by the time they adjust they’re often chasing. Wyoming is built to take advantage, leaning on the run and shortening the game, which is exactly how a home dog hangs around and reaches the fourth quarter within a field goal.
UConn’s pass defense is the soft spot
The Huskies can play; they put up 485 yards on Old Dominion and ran it for 246. But defensively there are real cracks. UConn ranks 121st in EPA per play and a shaky 109th against the pass, allowing a 67% completion rate and seven yards per attempt. That’s the kind of secondary that gives up chunk plays, and in a close game those swing the cover.
Wyoming, meanwhile, defends the pass well and runs for five yards a carry while averaging 7.2 yards per pass attempt of its own. The Cowboys don’t need to light up the scoreboard; they just need to keep it close and physical, and their style plus the altitude points toward exactly that kind of game. With a total of only 45.5, there isn’t much margin for a road favorite to pull away.
Game Snapshot
UConn (6-5) makes a cross-country trip to face Wyoming (7-4) on Saturday in an early Mountain Time kickoff, roughly a 10 a.m. local start. The Huskies are laying 3 points as road favorites and the total sits at 45.5 in what projects as a grind.
UConn arrives off a 29-21 win over Old Dominion in which the Huskies rolled up 485 yards, throwing for 239 and rushing for 246 while giving up just 53 yards on the ground. They converted four of ten on third down and have been a balanced, physical team when things go well.
Wyoming is the better home team by record at 7-4 overall and 4-1 in Laramie, even though it’s coming off a 24-17 loss to New Mexico in which the Cowboys managed just 188 yards. Wyoming plays at the highest elevation of any of the 138 FBS locations, a real factor for an East Coast team making its first trip out west this late in the year.
The matchup numbers: UConn surrenders big plays on defense, ranking 121st in EPA, and is especially vulnerable through the air at 109th in passing-defense EPA while allowing a 67% completion rate and seven yards per attempt. Wyoming defends the pass well, runs for five yards a carry, and averages 7.2 yards per pass attempt. At home the Cowboys hold opponents to just 12 points a game.
