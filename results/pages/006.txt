The source of the optimism surrounding this team? First on that list was quarterback Micah Alejado. Alejado completed 66% of his passes as a freshman, along with 3,106 passing yards and 24 touchdown passes despite missing time. He registered multiple wins over Power Four programs. Promising to say the least. Fast forward to now, Alejado ranks 118th-nationally in quarterback rating. What happened? Surely Alejado has to bear some of the blame, but I think it's a collective effort. Alejado was sacked 25 times in 2025, he's already at 14 sacks through five games this season. The protection hasn't been good and Hawaii calls plays like they don't trust the pass blocking. The injury to wide receiver Pofele Ashlock has proven to be devastating, as the transfer receivers haven't hit yet (more on that later). I'm sure Alejado will point the finger at himself, maybe justly so, but this offense is a collective mess. Can he play hero in Tempe? Or should I say, will his supporting cast enable him to be a hero?
Arizona State is a missed opportunity
When the 2026 schedule was completed, in tandem with the growing hype surrounding this Hawaii team, Arizona State appeared to be an opportunity. A respectable Power Four opponent, but not a juggernaut beyond the Warriors. Back when resume mattered. That's all history. Arizona State is coming fresh off an embarrassing home loss to Baylor, losing 55-19. At 2-2, it's mixed results thus far, featuring two blowout losses to Texas A&M and Baylor, and two wins against Morgan State and Kansas. In an alternate timeline, this might've been a chance to bolster Hawaii's Group of Six bid case. Instead, the Warriors are rightfully 20.5-point underdogs with little hope for victory. Neither team will be in a good mood Saturday night.
