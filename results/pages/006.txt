Partner
Oct 15, 2026 6:00 PM
Published Oct 15, 2026 6:00 PM
Northwestern could benefit from Michigan State's lengthy injury report, with the Spartans missing several key defensive starters ahead of Saturday's Big Ten matchup.
Northwestern
football has dealt with its fair share of injuries throughout the 2026 season, but heading into Saturday’s Big Ten matchup against
Michigan State
, the Wildcats might be catching a much-needed break.
The Spartans are dealing with a significant number of injuries themselves, including several key defensive starters.
According to Michigan State’s initial availability report, the Spartans will be without their top four cornerbacks, three key linebackers, starting left tackle Ben Murawski and fullback Jay Coyne, among others.
That is on top of the season-ending injury to running back Cam Edwards, who had been one of Michigan State’s most important offensive weapons.

And with Michigan State missing so many important defensive contributors, this could be another opportunity for Chiles and the Wildcats to put together an impressive offensive performance.
The Spartans’ secondary will be particularly interesting to watch.
With cornerbacks Tre Bell, Charles Brantley, Keshawn Williams and Tyran Chappell all ruled out, Michigan State will need several less-experienced players to step into larger roles.
That could create opportunities for Northwestern’s receiving corps, especially Griffin Wilde, who has quickly become one of Chiles’ favorite targets.
Wilde is coming off an impressive performance against Ball State, recording eight receptions for 144 yards and a touchdown.
The Wildcats have also received important contributions from Drew Wagner, Alex Honig and several other playmakers throughout the season.
If Northwestern can establish its passing attack early, Michigan State’s depleted secondary could face a difficult afternoon.
But the injuries do not stop there.
Michigan State will also be without linebackers Jordan Hall, Brady Pretzlaff and Caleb Wheatland, creating additional challenges in the middle of its defense.
That could be significant against a Northwestern rushing attack that has already demonstrated its ability to dominate games on the ground.
With running backs Caleb Komolafe and Joseph Himon II leading the way, Northwestern has shown it can create explosive plays without relying entirely on its passing game.
The combination of Chiles’ mobility, Kelly’s offensive creativity and Northwestern’s productive rushing attack could present serious challenges for a Michigan State defense missing several regular contributors.
However, the Wildcats are certainly not entering Saturday at full strength themselves.
Northwestern has 10 players listed as out, including offensive lineman Ezomo Oratokhai, who suffered a season-ending injury earlier this year.
Tight end Luke Dehnicke is listed as doubtful, while defensive backs Robert Fitzgerald and Damon Walters, along with defensive lineman Migo Jackson, are questionable.
Fitzgerald’s
status
is particularly important considering his leadership and production in Northwestern’s secondary.
The veteran defensive back leads the Wildcats with 49 tackles and has recorded two interceptions this season, including a pick-six against Ball State.
His availability will be something to monitor closely as kickoff approaches. Of course, injuries are never something a team wants to see, regardless of the opponent.
Ideally, both programs would enter Saturday’s matchup at full strength, allowing their best players to compete against one another.
But injuries are an unavoidable part of football, and successful teams must find ways to adjust when important contributors are unavailable.
That is where the next-man-up mentality becomes so important.
For Michigan State head coach Pat Fitzgerald, Saturday will be a major test of his team’s depth and ability to make adjustments.
Several players who might not have expected significant playing time earlier in the season could now find themselves in important roles against a ranked Big Ten opponent.
And for Northwestern head coach David Braun, the challenge will be making sure his team remains focused despite the Spartans’ injury concerns.

Michigan State might enter the game with a 2-4 record and a depleted roster, but the Spartans are still capable of competing.
They nearly defeated Illinois last weekend before falling 38-37 in overtime, showing that they can remain competitive even while dealing with adversity.
Northwestern cannot afford to overlook that.
The Wildcats have already shown they can compete against strong opponents and dominate teams when everything is clicking.
Now they have an opportunity to earn their first road victory of the season and continue building momentum in the Big Ten.
Michigan State’s injuries could certainly work in Northwestern’s favor, particularly with the Wildcats’ offense playing at such a high level.
But ultimately, the game will still be decided by execution, discipline and which team does the better job adjusting to its available personnel.
Both programs will be relying on their depth Saturday.
And while Northwestern might have an opportunity to take advantage of Michigan State’s depleted roster, the Wildcats will still need to prove they can get the job done on the road.
