Strong Opinion – (452) LOS ANGELES RAMS (+2.5) over Buffalo
Lean – Over 52.5
·
We have the Bills rated as the best team in the NFL this year, but this price is too high versus the Super Bowl Champs in a good spot at home and this game should be a pick ’em.
·
West coast teams are 38-17 straight up and 32-22-1 ATS against east coast teams on Sunday/Monday night football since 1989 due to the timezone benefit. It is much better for a player’s internal clock in the second half to be at 7pm than 10pm for peak performance. We saw Baltimore blow a 14-point lead down the stretch in Las Vegas last year to open the season.
·
Wide Receiver Allen Robinson should be a solid addition to the Los Angeles offense after a tough season last year in Chicago averaging just 6.2 yards per target. Matt Stafford has consistently shown the ability to elevate WRs throughout his career and Robinson certainly has the ability to be Pro Bowl-caliber as he averaged 2.06 yards per route run in 2020 (12th) despite the Bears ranking 26th in yppp.
·
The Rams offensive line will likely take a step back after the retirement of LT Andrew Whitworth (5th in pass blocking efficiency last year).
·
Los Angeles lost edge defender Von Miller (16th in pass rushing efficiency) this offseason, but added another perennial All-Pro to the defense with LB Bobby Wagner joining DT Aaron Donald and CB Jalen Ramsey.
·
Nickelback Troy Hill is back with the Rams after spending last year in Cleveland. Hill conceded only 1.03 yards per cover snap in the slot (5th) during his last season with LA in 2020 and he should replace the production of CB Darious Williams (now in Jacksonville).
·
I expect the Bills to finish near the top of the league in scoring, but they did lose offensive coordinator Brian Daboll and there’s a risk with first-time play callers like Ken Dorsey.
·
WR Gabriel Davis led last year’s Bills with 2.03 yards per route run and he will likely see a huge uptick in targets after the departure of Emmanuel Sanders.
·
Von Miller coming into Buffalo should be able to replace the 80 pressures going out the door with edge rushers Jerry Hughes and Mario Addison.
·
The Bills allowed a league-low 18.3 points per game in 2021 but our metrics show that was a bit flattering due to weather. The scoring conditions in Buffalo’s games last season were 1.2 points worse than average.
·
Bills CB Tre’Davious White made back to back Pro Bowls in 2019 and 2020 before tearing his ACL last year and he will start the season on the PUP list.
·
Our model favors the Bills by 0.1 with a predicted total of 54.1.
Rams are a Strong Opinion at +2 or more
.

Baltimore (-7) vs NY JETS
·
The Ravens came into 2021 with a market win expectation of 10.4 after losing starting CB Marcus Peters and top three running backs in the preseason. Baltimore’s win expectation is 10.2 this year with an easier projected strength of schedule so I think they are undervalued. The Ravens are slightly worse on offense than a year ago at this time, but clearly healthier and better on defense and I think they are the favorite to win the AFC North.
·
The Ravens lose WR Marquise Brown on offense but he might be a touch overrated after averaging only 1.61 yards per route run last season which ranked 30th out of 33 wide receivers with at least 100 targets.
·
Baltimore’s defense adds Marcus Williams, who led all safeties with a 0.38 forced incompletions per target last year.
·
Baltimore’s offense might be in a bit of trouble with their high run rate in this specific matchup. The Jets were terrible in just about every facet of the game last season except rush defense where they ranked 8th by our numbers.
·
Edge defender Carl Lawson will be a welcome new piece after he didn’t get to play a game for New York last year due to torn Achilles, but ranked 9th in pass rushing efficiency his last full season in 2020.
·
As a defensive-minded head coach, I think Robert Saleh was embarrassed by the league-high 29.6 points per game New York surrendered in 2021 and I think he’ll have more conservative tendencies to make that number more reasonable this year.
·
Joe Flacco will get the start and I certainly don’t plan on seeing any fireworks from the Jets offensively. Flacco has averaged just 5.6 yppp the last five seasons.
Ravens LT Stanley is out. The model now favors the Ravens by 7.0 with a predicted total of 43.3 points.

New Orleans (-5.5) vs ATLANTA
Lean – Over (42.5)
·
The market rates New Orleans around a league-average team, but I think they’ll be better with one of the most talented defenses in the NFL once again. The Saints conceded only 19.7 points per game in 2021 (4th) and they should be near the top of the league this year.
·
The New Orleans secondary loses Marcus Williams, Malcolm Jenkins, and CJ Gardner-Johnson. However, they added Pro Bowl safety Tyrann Mathieu and safety Marcus Maye, who forced 7 incompletions his last healthy season in 2020 (7th).
·
The Saints were missing DE Marcus Davenport and DT David Onyemata for the first two months of last season, but will have both to start the year in 2022. Davenport ranked 12th in pass rushing efficiency down the stretch and Onyemata ranked 11th.
·
The New Orleans offense loses Sean Payton as a play-caller and three-time Pro Bowl LT Terron Armstead, but they will have Jameis Winston returning under center rather than that combo of Trevor Siemian, Taysom Hill, and Ian Book they were rolling out there for the back half of last year.
·
We haven’t seen WR Michael Thomas healthy in awhile but he did lead the NFL with 2.88 yards per route run his last full season in 2019. The Saints added Jarvis Landry and drafted Chris Olave in the first round so ostensibly they are upgrading at all three starting WR spots.
·
The Falcons somehow finished 7-10 last season despite a -146 point differential (28th) and it’s likely we see Atlanta fall off into the league’s cellar this year.
·
The Falcons will take a step back offensively with Marcus Mariota replacing QB Matt Ryan. Mariota averaged just 5.6 yppp in 2019 when now head coach Arthur Smith was his offensive coordinator before getting benched for Ryan Tannehill.
·
I think Atlanta’s cornerback tandem with new addition Casey Hayward alongside AJ Terrell will be amongst the most underrated in the NFL as they finished 1st and 2nd last season in receptions conceded per cover snap.
·
However, the Falcons won’t have starting LB Deion Jones and nickelback Isaiah Oliver to begin the season. Those two are worth 0.7 points combined.
·
Our model favors the Saints by 8.0 with a predicted total of 45.1.

Lean – Cleveland (+1.5) over CAROLINA
·
Home field advantage is worth less than 2.5 points this season (we’re going with 1.4 HFA this year) and I think the Browns should be lined around pick because they are better than the Panthers.
·
The Colts offense finished 16th and scoring and 19th in our metrics during the 2019 season with Jacoby Brissett at the helm. I believe Kevin Stefanski is on the same level as Frank Reich in terms of play calling ability so the market rating Cleveland’s offense at the bottom of the NFL is way too low to me.
·
The market is down on Brissett because he gained only 4.7 yppp last season (32nd) but much of that can be attributed to Miami’s terrible offensive line (last in pass blocking efficiency).
·
Amari Cooper will provide an upgrade to the Browns receiving room this year as he has averaged 1.85 yards per route run during his career and Cleveland’s WRs combined to average just 1.33 yards per route run in 2021.
·
The Browns defense finished 14th in scoring last year but their success on high-leverage downs should improve in 2022. Cleveland’s defense ranked 3rd in success rate on early downs but 21st on 3rd down and their opponents converted 64% of 4th downs (27th).
·
The Browns only recovered 41% of their opponent’s fumbles last season (26th) which should also turn around.
·
Cleveland’s starting cornerback trio conceded only 0.95 yards per cover snap in 2021 and with an average age of 24 are likely to get even better in this campaign.
·
Edge defender Myles Garrett ranked 5th in pass rushing efficiency last year and he should dominate across from rookie LT Ikem Ekwonu making his first NFL start.
·
This Panthers offense is slightly better than the Browns, but Cleveland’s coaching staff will have high familiarity with concepts to exploit Baker Mayfield’s weaknesses.
·
The Panthers defense ranked 20th in scoring last season and 22nd in our metrics. I don’t think the additions of DT Matthew Ioannidis, safety Xavier Woods, and LB Damien Wilson will get this unit rated above average like the market seems to be implying. None of those three players has ever even made a Pro Bowl. Carolina will have last year’s first round pick CB Jaycee Horn back from a broken foot, but Pro Bowl CB Stephon Gilmore and edge defender Haason Reddick left this offseason.
·
Our model favors the Browns by 0.3 with a predicted total of 42.1.

WASHINGTON (-2.5) vs Jacksonville
Lean – Over (44)
·
The Jaguars finished 2021 last in scoring averaging less than 15 points per game but there’s reason for some optimism this season namely a possible year 2 jump for Trevor Lawrence and bringing in Super Bowl Champion head coach Doug Pederson.
·
Jacksonville’s offensive line was a minor bright spot for the team last season ranking 9th in pass blocking efficiency and should be solid again replacing guard Andrew Norwell with Pro Bowler Brandon Scherff, who did not concede a sack in 11 games last year.
·
The Jaguars receiver room is beefed up with Christian Kirk (led Cardinals in yards per route run last season).
·
Jacksonville’s defense has finished in the bottom 5 of points allowed each of the last two seasons and they’ll likely be around there again in 2022.
·
Defensive coordinator Mike Caldwell comes over from Tampa Bay where they blitzed at the highest rate in the NFL last year (41%), but the Jaguars really don’t have the safety talent to put a roof over those blitzes and I expect them to give up a fair number of explosive plays.
·
There’s not a ton of buzz surrounding Washington’s offense this season, but they finished 23rd in scoring last season with Taylor Heinicke and Carson Wentz is at least a minor upgrade at the QB position otherwise they wouldn’t have made the change.
·
The Commanders drafted Jahan Dotson in the first round and Curtis Samuel is healthy now after battling a groin injury during last year. Samuel gained 1.94 yards per route run in 2020 (19th).
·
Washington is pretty much rolling the same defense out there again that allowed 25.5 points per game last year (25th). However, that number is skewed by one 56-point performance by the Cowboys when the Commanders were missing starters at every level of the field. Washington’s defense would’ve ranked 20th taking out the Dallas game which is where we have them going into 2022.
·
Edge rusher Chase Young will be out to start the season and our numbers have him valued at 0.5 points.
·
Our model favors the Commanders by 1.9 with a predicted total of 46.5.

Kansas City (-6.5) vs ARIZONA
Lean – Over 53.5
·
The Chiefs traded Tyreek Hill this offseason but I still think they have the best offense in the NFL. Although, the gap has shrunk from last year when I had their offense 5.7 points better than average in the prior to 4.8 points better than average for 2022.
·
People seem to have forgotten WR JuJu Smith-Schuster averaged 2.12 yards per route run his first two seasons in the NFL before Ben Roethlisberger’s performance fell off a cliff following his elbow injury in 2019. Smith-Schuster should be able to replace the majority of Hill’s production while the addition of WR Marquez Valdes-Scantling keeps a deep threat on the field.
·
Much was made about Patrick Mahomes’s struggles against 2-high safety looks during the middle part of last season, but the Chiefs solved these issues down the stretch and averaged 33.9 points in 7 games from mid-december onwards.
·
Mahomes has gained over 9 yards per attempt against the blitz in every season as a starter. Last year, teams also gave up on sending extra pass rushers against Mahomes as he faced an NFL-low 15% blitz rate (league average was 27%). However, Cardinals defensive coordinator Vance Joseph is live-or-die by the blitz (4th-highest rate in 2021) and Mahomes should dice them up on Sunday.
·
Arizona’s defense got worse this offseason losing Pro Bowl edge defender Chandler Jones and their top CB Robert Alford. We have them rated slightly below average.
·
We’re going to be right around market ranking the Arizona’s offense borderline top 10 for 2022. DeAndre Hopkins is suspended to start the year and there’s been a lot of discussion about Kyler Murray’s contract situation over the summer, but the Cardinals have finished 7th and 8th in our offensive ratings the last two seasons and I doubt Arizona’s offense will drop below league average.
·
Kansas City’s defense got worse to me this offseason with a clear downgrade from Pro Bowl safety Tyrann Mathieu to Justin Reid. The Chiefs will be relying quite a bit on two rookies (DE George Karlaftis and CB Trent McDuffie) which isn’t usually a recipe for success.
·
Arizona has some injury issues with JJ Watt, Rondale Moore, and Travon Mullen all out. Those injuries have been adjusted for in the model.
·
Our model favors the Chiefs by 4.3 with a predicted total of 54.9.

Lean – LA CHARGERS (-3.5) over Las Vegas
·
In my opinion, the Chargers had the best defensive offseason additions in the NFL after Brandon Staley went from conceding 19.3 points per game in 2020 with the Rams (2nd) to 27.0 points per game in his first season with the Chargers last year (29th).
·
Los Angeles had a rush defense about a point worse than average by our metrics last year and brought in DT Sebastian Joseph-Day, who led all interior defenders with a 14.5% run stop rate his last full season in 2020 with Staley. The Chargers also brought in edge defender Khalil Mack, who missed more than half of last year but his 19 run stops in 2020 ranked 14th.
·
Mack’s 57 pressures ranked 7th in 2020 and he will also be a force getting after opposing QBs opposite Joey Bosa (9th in pass rushing efficiency last season).
·
Los Angeles added Pro Bowl cornerback JC Jackson but he is out on Sunday with an ankle injury.
·
The Chargers signed nickelback Bryce Callahan and he has conceded only 0.87 yards per cover snap in the slot during the previous two seasons.
·
Justin Herbert’s offense should finish near the top of the league in scoring again having not lost any major pieces from last year when they averaged 27.9 points (5th).
·
This Raiders season could go a lot of different directions with the new coaching staff.
·
Head coach Josh McDaniels inherited an offense that averaged 5.7 yppl (11th) and added one of the best WRs in the league, Davante Adams (2nd in yards per route run last year).
·
The offense was Derek Carr’s after Jon Gruden stepped down and he called his own number quite a bit. Las Vegas finished 2021 with a 63% pass rate (4th) which I doubt will be the case under McDaniels.
·
The Raiders ranked 27th in pass blocking efficiency last season and didn’t make any notable improvements.
·
Las Vegas has a high ceiling if everything goes right and their floor is around league-average as long as everyone is healthy.
·
The Raiders best defensive acquisition was edge rusher Chandler Jones, but his 10.6% pressure rate last season was lower than Yannick Ngakoue’s 12.0% (departed for IND).
·
New defensive coordinator Patrick Graham might have his work cut out for him as the Raiders also lost CB Casey Hayward, who conceded just 22.8 yards per game last season.
·
Our model favors the Chargers by 5.2 with a predicted total of 51.7.

Green Bay (-1.5) vs MINNESOTA
·
The Packers are 7-0 without Davante Adams under Matt LaFleur but I think there is a lot of smoke and mirrors involved there.
·
A perfect example is last year’s game in Arizona. Green Bay won 24-21 but only because Kyler Murray threw a strange interception from the 5-yard line with 15 seconds remaining when AJ Green didn’t see the ball coming. The Packers labored on offense all game averaging just 4.7 yppl.
·
Green Bay’s offense also lost WR Marquez Valdes-Scantling, who finished 3rd on the Packers in receiving yards.
·
It is very difficult to succeed in the NFL without any above average receiving threats even if you have the MVP at quarterback in Aaron Rodgers. We still think Green Bay’s offense can finish in the top 10 of scoring, but it will be a lot more dink and dunk getting RBs Aaron Jones and AJ Dillon involved in the passing game.
·
Defensively, the Packers are going to be very stout bringing CB Jaire Alexander back into the fold after he missed most of last year with an AC joint injury in his right shoulder. Alexander ranked 3rd in forced incompletion rate during the 2020 season.
·
Green Bay’s defense ranked 6th in our metrics last year and we have them 3rd going into this season.
·
The Vikings did not make any massive changes on either side of the ball with new GM Kwesi Adofo-Mensah and this season will be an experiment in the contrast between losing continuity from the old coaching staff and the fresh ideas from first-time head coach Kevin O’Connell. Minnesota finished last season rated slightly above average by our metrics and we’ll start them there again in 2022.
·

Cousins might take a drop in efficiency because defenses won’t be playing the run as much but the offense should improve overall after finishing 17th in yppl last year.

Lean – NY Giants (+5.5) over TENNESSEE
Lean – Over (44)
·
The market is too low on New York’s offense implying they will finish 29th in scoring and too high on the Giants defense ranking them around league average.
·
New head coach Brian Daboll has finished 1st and 6th in our play calling metrics the last two seasons with the Bills. Our metrics suggest New York’s offense has room to get around 2 points better just in the play calling improvement from Jason Garrett to Daboll and new offensive coordinator Mike Kafka.

New York’s defense is setting up to be one of the worst in the NFL after cutting CB James Bradberry (4th in forced incompletions) and safety Logan Ryan. New defensive coordinator Wink Martindale is going to try bringing plenty of pressure, but the Giants don’t have enough talent on the back end to hold up when they blitz.
·
Ryan Tannehill has been one of the most efficient passers in the league since joining the Titans averaging 6.9 yppp the last three seasons. However, Tannehill averaged just 5.4 yppp in the games without top wide receiver AJ Brown (now in Philadelphia).
·
Tennessee also lost two starters on the offensive line and I expect them to finish near the bottom of the NFL in pass blocking efficiency once again.
·
I doubt we see the Titans finish the season above average in scoring, but this game in particular could be one of their high points of the season given that Giants defense.
·
Tennessee’s defense took a huge blow losing edge defender Harold Landry to a torn ACL in the preseason. Landry ranked 17th in pass rushing efficiency last year and is worth about a half point.
·
