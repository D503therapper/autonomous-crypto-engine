Setting the scene
I went into the game notes looking for a reason to believe this one would be close and came out with a one-score tally courtesy of Navy.
According to research from Navy’s Scott Strasemeier, which made its way into ECU’s notes, both teams have played six one-score games. The Pirates are 4-2 in those games. Army is 3-3. Somewhere in Annapolis, somebody may already have the next row ready.
East Carolina (6-4, 4-2 American) squeezed past Charlotte 17-16 last week to become bowl-eligible for a third straight season, something it hadn’t done since 2012-14. Army (5-4, 4-2 American) can secure its own third straight bowl trip on Senior Day after a bye and back-to-back losses to Memphis and Air Force. Both enter the weekend a game behind Memphis, UTSA, and North Texas in the loss column, so a third league loss would make the path to the American title game awfully narrow.
The series history gives Pirates fans plenty to work with, though Army’s lone win was a 45-28 victory when ECU last came to West Point in 2024. The teams have split their two meetings as American opponents.
ECU averages an FBS-best 9.5 tackles for loss and an American-best 2.9 sacks per game. The Pirates have reached double digits in tackles for loss in six of 10 games, so this production goes well beyond the 18 they piled up against NC Central. At Charlotte, they allowed 22 yards and two first downs after halftime.
Against Army, the first job for Carr and the front is to muddy the mesh point and stay sound on the edges. A tackle for loss on first down can force Army to show more of its playbook. A defender chasing the first back through the hole can give Cale Hellums all the room he needs to keep it himself. And if the Pirates leave Army with fourth-and-1, they should plan on playing another snap.
On offense, Ashton Gray has put together consecutive games with 100-plus yards from scrimmage. He carried 24 times for 88 yards and both ECU touchdowns at Charlotte, then added four catches for 20 yards. Army gives up 164.3 rushing yards a game, so Gray has a chance to keep ECU on schedule and give Emory Williams manageable downs. There’s room for a shot to Landon Sides, too. The pair hooked up for two touchdowns against South Florida, and Army is allowing 226.3 passing yards per game.
The Pirates also gave the ball away four times against a one-win Charlotte team and still got out with a win. Doing it again against a team that averages 33:35 of possession is a good way to spend most of the afternoon watching Gray stand next to Blake Harrell.
