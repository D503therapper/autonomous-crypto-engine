Air Force vs Army Odds
Air Force has taken possession of the Commander-in-Chief Trophy exactly one time over the last 10 years, and I'm guessing many within the walls of the Falcon football offices have never even laid eyes on the trophy.
The fact that Air Force can clinch the CIC Trophy this weekend provides added motivation for a bunch that doesn't need much to draw up reasons to want to play Army.
The Falcons have overcome a decent amount of adversity this season, but along with bowl eligibility and a conference championship possibility, winning this game will allow the Flyboys to accomplish one of their most cherished goals of the season.
The ground game has been humming along despite starting quarterback Liam Szarka being ruled out for the season after suffering a leg injury against Navy.
Backup quarterback Evan Medders has also missed time with injury, but Kemper Hodges, who is a converted fullback, has flourished under center and is running the ball really well.
Hodges has run for 401 yards and six touchdowns with a bruising style that wears down defenses due to his physical size. At six feet tall and weighing 245 pounds this dude is a lot to handle.
Owen Allen has carried the rock effectively out of the fullback position, and Rocco Conti provides a bit of pop along the outside.
The world's finest Air Force is equipped with the finest rushing attack in the college leagues, as the Falcons are averaging 341 yards per game on the ground.
Twice this season, the Flyboys have won a game without completing a pass, but there are three receivers who have at least five catches this season.
The defense has been strong against the run a good majority of the season, with only Wyoming and Nevada exceeding 150 rushing yards against this unit.
Competent quarterbacks can throw on Air Force, but Army will line up and run the ball. So, the key for Air Force will be defending the power run game, which features a heavy dose of QB power.
Army has been involved in high-pressure games all season long, with the Knights sitting at 3-2 in one-possession games.
The team is vastly improved from last season, and there has been more consistent play on offense with the emergence of reliable playmakers.
Godspower Nwawuihe has run for 455 yards and four touchdowns this season. Samari Howard and Zach Mundell are also reliable options that provide some explosiveness. Jake Rendina can be used out of the traditional flexbone "B" back position.
But the straw that stirs the drink is the quarterback.
Cale Hellums has run for 874 yards with 17 rushing touchdowns this season, and he has more carries than any other three players for the Army offense.
Hellums doesn't provide a ton of explosiveness on the ground, and despite Army leading the country in 20-plus yard rushes, only one of every 15 rushes is deemed explosive.
