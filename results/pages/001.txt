Notre Dame football’s latest injury news sets the Irish up for something we haven’t seen in the Marcus Freeman era
Notre Dame football received a wave of encouraging injury updates on Monday. What does the timing mean for the Irish heading into the College Football Playoff?
Ryan Roberts
—
National College Football Writer
Nov 18, 2026 12:49 PM EST
Sep 19, 2026; South Bend, Indiana, USA; Notre Dame Fighting Irish head coach Marcus Freeman points to the crowd after beating the Michigan State Spartans half at Notre Dame Stadium.
Mandatory Credit: Michael Caterina-Imagn Images
Notre Dame football has dealt with its fair share of bad injury news over the years.
Monday brought a different kind of update. Several key Fighting Irish players are trending toward a return, and for the first time in years, it feels like this team could get healthy at the most opportune time to make a legitimate national championship run.
That timing matters. If the recoveries go as hoped, this could be the healthiest Notre Dame team we have seen heading into the postseason during the
Marcus Freeman
era. That alone is a reason to buy into this group.
Notre Dame’s injury outlook on defense
Safety
Brauntae Johnson
is listed as questionable for the matchup against SMU. It’s the first time he has carried that designation since his Week 1 injury against Wisconsin. I still have my doubts that we will see Johnson against SMU.
Independent · CFP No. 6
Notre Dame
Fighting Irish
Jason Onye
and
Francis Brewu
, are also still listed as questionable. Based on the conversations I’ve had, I still have my doubts about seeing them against SMU.
I’ll be honest. When the initial prognosis came back, I was worried both could be season-ending injuries, and I didn’t feel great about the Brewu one. The fact that they are listed as questionable rather than doubtful or out gives you a reasonable timeline to expect both back for the College Football Playoff.
Outside of losing
Drayk Bowen
, Notre Dame is in good shape. I also expect star cornerback DJ McKinney to potentially be back for the playoffs. Get McKinney, Johnson, and the defensive tackles back, and this group looks a lot different.
The rest of the Irish roster is healthy
Offensive lineman
Charles Jagusah
is back on the practice field after suffering a humerus injury, along with an infection and nerve issue, before the 2025 season. I don’t know exactly what to make of his return.
Perhaps he eventually gets reinstated into the lineup, even if that is a 2027 return. At the very least, it helps depth in practice. It gives other players a chance to rest and others a chance to get more acclimated.
The offensive line is healthy.
Matty Augustine
CJ Carr
has remained unscathed.
Why the timing matters for a title run
Think back to Notre Dame’s run to the national championship game against Ohio State following the 2024 season. Jeremiyah Love was compromised. Rylie Mills did not play, and Howard Cross III was banged up. Kicker Mitch Jeter was also dealing with an injury going into the postseason. Honestly, it was a bit of a miracle that the Irish made that run at all.
That has been a recurring theme, and it was true at times in the Brian Kelly era, too. Every time the conversation turned to a potential title run, it was dampened by the same thought. If only they were healthier.
This season looks different. Notre Dame took its injury hits early in 2026, and now the team appears to be getting healthy right when you want it to peak. There is finally some light at the end of the tunnel.
If this team is healthy and plays its best football at the right time, it can win a national championship. The Irish still have to actualize that potential, but I am buying into Notre Dame and the timing of these returns.
