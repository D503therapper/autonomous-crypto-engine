This is news that came as
no surprise
, and it's crazy to see it take as long as it did.
Brown had only suited up for four games this season, but he continued to appear on injury reports leading up to Florida's 34-27 win against Vanderbilt.
The 7-4 Gators certainly could've used Brown in that brutal one-win stretch from Oct. 3 to Nov. 7, but at the end of the day they bounced back terrifically from Nov. 14 onward.
Brown was a crucial part of this team's 4-0 start as he recorded 17 receptions for 271 yards with a pair of touchdowns.
For reference, he's still third on the team in receiving yards, proving just how valuable he was during the few games when he was around.
For the Gators, it's business as usual going into the final game of the year, a rivalry showdown at Florida State.
Florida's offense is business as usual
Finally, Florida's offense knows it can move on once and for all without Brown. That's not a sentence they wanted to hear back on Sept. 26, but it's been a long time coming as his status had been in doubt for the better part of two months.
Brown is only a sophomore, so the Gators risking further injury by putting him back on the field was never going to be the right answer.
The Florida native made an immediate impact as a freshman with his 40 receptions for 512 yards. Obviously, this season took quite a turn with those losses to Missouri and South Carolina, and it doesn't feel like a coincidence that the offense scored fewer than 20 points in those games without Brown.
Running back Jadan Baugh was not a factor in either of those games either, so that also didn't help the Gators chances when they lost their offensive superstar.
When Brown and Baugh were both on the field, this team looked absolutely unstoppable. Now, it'll once again be up to Aaron Philo to keep this run-game going, though he looked much better through the air against the Commodores.
Florida State enters this non-conference game at 5-6 overall, fighting for bowl eligibility against a Brown-less Gators offense.
