South Florida, 1 (2025)
Setting the scene
The Alamodome is where dreams go to die in the American Conference.
Jeff Traylor has been head coach at UTSA since 2020, and he’s 35-5 in the Alamodome and 25-1 against conference opponents. Last season, the Roadrunners ran College Football Playoff qualifier Tulane out of the building by 22 and ended East Carolina’s contention hopes in a 34-point victory.
First-year South Florida head coach Brian Hartline is aware of the Alamodome magic, but he is skeptical of the crowd noise. In his game week press conference, he stated, “I heard that it’s really loud there. I’m trying to figure out what the population size is. I don’t know how it gets so loud if there’s not a lot of people there.”
UTSA has used Hartline’s comments to rally the fanbase, even offering a “
Brian Hartline Special
” of $9 tickets (a reference to Hartline’s college jersey number) in hopes of packing the Alamodome.
The offense stepped up against Pac-12 competition, defeating Texas State 31-26 and Colorado State 59-45. However, UTSA has competed in more defensive-driven bouts. It lost 30-6 at No. 1 Texas and last weekend, it overcame a trap game against Rice, emerging 16-14 on the road without notching a single second half point.
But UTSA can always trust the Alamodome to recapture its magic, especially on offense. Jeff Traylor’s team averages 52 points per game this season in San Antonio, compared to 17.7 outside its beloved venue.
Coming off an offensive struggle, Owen McCown and the UTSA offense look for a bounceback. McCown is still displaying the greatest efficiency in his three years as a starter, connecting on 68.2 percent of passes with seven touchdowns against two interceptions — six touchdowns and zero interceptions at the Alamodome.
The Roadrunners support their longtime starting quarterback with a wealth of skill position talent including a running back trio of Will Henderson III, Brandon High Jr., and A’Marion Peterson, who each average more than five yards per carry and combine for all nine of the team’s rushing touchdowns. Henderson has seamlessly taken on the No. 1 role in the backfield, totaling 80 rushing yards in all four wins this season. Getting Henderson on track for another 80 will be key, as the inability to run the ball doomed UTSA in its 32-point loss at South Florida last November. The Roadrunners may need to overcome some adversity to accomplish this as one of their top linemen — guard Ben Rios — is a game-time decision.
UTSA also features a deep receiving corps where the top four targets all range between 16 and 20 receptions. Mekhi Anderson, who returned a kickoff for a touchdown two weeks ago, leads the group in catches, while DJ Allen has been the premier deep threat of the bunch, contributing a team-best 269 yards.
The Roadrunners’ defense has played better on the road than it has in the Alamodome. Last week, UTSA finally jumpstarted its pass rush, securing three sacks (of its six total on the season) against Rice. Outside linebacker Darion White was the conference’s defensive player of the week for producing seven tackles and a clutch four quarter sack. White ranks second on UTSA in tackles, and the top three on the leaderboard are all linebackers — with Brandon Tucker Jr. checking in at first and Owen Pewee third.
The defense has forced enough havoc for the Roadunners to be a plus-4 in the turnover battle, and it’s a concerted effort. Seven different Roadrunners have either intercepted a pass or recovered a fumble through five weeks. One UTSA standout that has yet to force a turnover, but has been instrumental to the defense’s improvement, is cornerback Asaad Chapman. He has three pass breakups on the year and operates as a standout cover corner on a passing defense limiting opponents to a 55.6 completion rate.
Prediction
The history of the Alamodome in the Jeff Traylor era implies a UTSA victory Thursday night. But it’s more than just the setting of the game favoring the Roadrunners in this matchup.
Although inconsistent, UTSA’s offense has shown a gear that South Florida hasn’t reached — or come close to reaching — in 2026. The Will Henderson-led run game is consistently effective, while Owen McCown has been unstoppable as a gunslinger at home. South Florida’s defense will certainly provide more resistance than Colorado State did in that 59-45 shootout, but the Roadrunners already have experience eking out wins when the offense isn’t clicking on all cylinders.
The Roadrunners’ ability to adapt will be key in this ESPN primetime matchup, and Traylor will improve to 24-1 against conference opponents in San Antonio.
Prediction: UTSA 27, South Florida 16
