You’ve probably seen how Penn football’s Friday night lights showdown against Columbia ended last week. The Quakers inexplicably chose to attempt a field goal instead of spiking the ball to stop the clock. The clip has since gone viral on social media, including an Instagram reel from CBS Sports that got 1.6 million views in less than a week.
“That is a spike situation 100 times out of 100,” coach Rick Santos said. “There was a miscommunication between the staff and the players.”
From what we know, someone from Penn’s sideline yelled “Bazooka!” -- a call for the special teams unit to rush onto the field for a field goal attempt. The unit quickly lined up, but the rushed execution may have caused sophomore kicker Mason Walters to miss the 51-yard attempt despite nailing a 52-yarder earlier in the game. Had junior quarterback Karson Siqueiros-Lasky spiked the ball, Penn would have had more time to set up the kick. Instead, a costly miscommunication handed the Quakers another conference loss.
“Moments like that are not what we want to be defined for, and we will not be defined as that going forward,” Santos said.
With the team yet to win its first conference game, the entire Penn community seems to be asking who’s starting at quarterback on Friday. For now, the answer remains unclear.

“We're gonna find out when we get to game day,” Santos said. “It's gonna be a kind of game-time decision to see who we're gonna play.”
In the past five games, freshman quarterback Miles Teodecki started under center for four of them. Unfortunately, Teodecki went down with a knee injury when the Quakers played Howard, giving Siqueiros-Lasky his first snaps of the season. Siqueiros-Lasky played admirably and overcame a 10-point deficit in the fourth quarter to secure the win. And last week, he threw for 374 yards and two touchdowns in his first collegiate start, but also had four intercepted passes, including one in the final two minutes of the game.
