Rice (1-3, 0-0 American) rides a three-game losing streak heading into the conference opener. The Owls finished 3-1 in non-conference play during Scott Abell’s first season, but this year offered a difficult slate featuring Notre Dame, Western Michigan, and Fresno State — three teams that combined for 29 wins in 2025.
The Owls are in their second year of running a shotgun option offense under Abell. Despite a worse showing in non-conference play, the offense has clearly taken strides forward. Rice’s rushing average has skyrocketed from 4.1 yards per carry to 5.6, while their per game output has increased from 205 to 269. Quarterback Jacurri Brown has been the perfect centerpiece of the option attack, recording his second consecutive 120+ yard rushing performance last Saturday at Fresno State — highlighted by an impressive 50-yard fourth down scramble in the fourth quarter.
Brown has been excellent, but Rice demands more production from the running backs and slot receivers, especially since star running back Quinton Jackson suffered an injury in Week 1. D’Andre Hardeman Jr. and Tyvonn Byars are the power backs looking to contribute more explosive runs. Meanwhile, slot receiver Ben Grice had one last week, going 88 yards in the first quarter to hand Rice an early lead. It was a quiet season from the slots until that moment, but getting this position involved could make this option attack even more lethal.
Rice is third-to-last in aerial offense this year, and the team has only attempted 58 passes. The Owls must be wary of fumbles, but the expected rainy conditions in Houston could favor a team that prefers to keep things grounded and consume time of possession.
While the Owls’ offense is executing at a higher level than the 2025 team, the defense must do the same. Rice is 100th in total defense and tied for 116th in scoring defense, and one issue is the lack of game-changing plays. The Owls have only forced two takeaways all season — one against FCS opponent Houston Christian and the other one special teams against Western Michigan. After shutting Fresno State out in the first quarter last week, the defense surrendered 38 points while the Bulldogs waltzed to an efficient 459 yards.
Losing defensive end and top pass rusher Chibby Nwajuaku to injury was a brutal hit for the defense. The Owl defense only has three sacks outside of Nwajuaku and was unable to sack Fresno State in 34 passing attempts last weekend. The run defense, led by linebackers Austin Harnetiaux and Cooper King, also looks for improvement against a Roadrunner offense capable of attacking in a multitude of ways.
Prediction
Outside of the Notre Dame game, Rice remained competitive with Western Michigan and Fresno State — remaining within one touchdown of both opponents in the fourth quarter. The Owls benefit from homefield advantage and the likely sloppy conditions at First Community Stadium on Saturday night.
UTSA’s offense will be a bear to overcome, and Rice fans know that. The Roadrunners have eclipsed 40 points in three of the last five matchups against the Owls, including last October’s 61-point outburst. This one remains on the lower-scoring side, but the dynamic UTSA offense, combined with a potentially improved defense, still emerges in Houston by a couple touchdowns.
