UTSA is shuffling at offensive tackle while preseason injuries heal, the left guard spot has been thrown into flux by an injury to Ben Rios, and UTSA is "still searching for clarity" in the receivers group, Traylor said.
Entering a matchup against South Florida at 6:30 p.m. Thursday in the Alamodome, UTSA's offense is still coming together.
"Our offense has not been near what it's capable of being," Traylor said. "We're just surviving with a really good defense, and we've ran the ball well, and our special teams have been very good. So, surviving would be a good way to describe what we've been doing so far."

After years of being led by standout targets like Devin McCuin, Zakhari Franklin and Joshua Cephus, no Roadrunners' pass-catcher has reached 300 total receiving yards through five games.
UTSA leaned on A'Marion Breland at left tackle in the first half against Rice before Jonah Miller took over to start the second half, and MJ Senegal earned his first start at left guard but quickly gave way to Davion Hurth, who played the bulk of the game.
Traylor said he was disappointed the Roadrunners' offensive line "was not as excited to play the game of football on that day as Rice's D-line and linebackers were," but he suggested that better performances are within reach.
"It is out there, there's no doubt, but I'm afraid there's some really good teams coming before we're going to be maxed out how good we are," Traylor said. "Can we keep surviving as we're getting healthier and better? That's going to be the question."
