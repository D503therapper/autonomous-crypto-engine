TITLE: Brawl of the Wild puts rivalry, Big Sky title, playoff position on the line
DATES SEEN: ['November 17, 2026']

Montana brings one of the country’s most explosive offenses to Bozeman, led by quarterback Keali’i Ah Yat, the nation’s top receiver Brooks Davis and Eli Gillman, the Big Sky’s all-time touchdown leader.
Vigen said Montana’s offense presents a challenge, especially if Ah Yat has time to throw.
“The trifecta of Ah Yat, Davis and Gillman is very much known,” Vigen said. “If we’re not getting pressure and he gets to sit back there, he can be an elite dude.”
Montana State has dealt with key injuries throughout the season and nearly entered rivalry week unbeaten. The Bobcats still have a chance to claim at least a share of the Big Sky title, giving Saturday’s game stakes beyond the rivalry trophy.
“I don’t think it says ‘share the Big Sky title’ on anything that we’re after,” Vigen said. “I know that.”
Last season showed how much home-field advantage can matter in the postseason. Saturday’s winner could improve its position for a playoff run, but players on both sides say the rivalry means more than trophies and standings.
“There’s great passions that run deep on both sides, so it’s a special rivalry,” Kennedy said.
