Oklahoma State enters Jack Trice Stadium as the No. 24 team in the nation. Twice in the last 15 years Iowa State has defeated top-10 Cowboys teams at home, most memorably when the Cyclones turned the college football world upside down with a 37-31 win in double overtime against the No. 2 Pokes as 27-point underdogs. The result, which helped send Alabama to the 2011 national championship game, is said by many to be the final nail in the coffin for the BCS system.

ISU vs. RANKED FOES
Iowa State has faced four teams that were ranked in the Top-25 of the AP Poll at kickoff. Saturday's game will be the fifth against a Top-25 opponent.
The Cyclones are playing their third-straight nationally-ranked opponent in the same season for the first time since 2013. In the Big 12 era (since 1996) this is just the ninth time ISU has played five ranked foes in a season.
BIT BY THE INJURY BUG
Iowa State has been bitten by the injury bug among players they expected to be starters or key contributors. Through seven games, ISU has lost double-digit players for the season. The Cyclones are down four of their expected top six receivers heading into fall camp.
Due to those injuries, ISU has 16 first-time college starters this season, the fourth-most nationally.
OTHER NOTABLES
Last time out, ISU struggled to stop a high-powered Arizona offense and the Cyclones had a hard time getting anything going as well as they lost to the No. 25 Wildcats, 39-3, on the road. Arizona's 36-point margin of victory was the largest by a Cyclone opponent since TCU beat ISU 62-14 in 2022.
