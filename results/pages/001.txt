TITLE: FCS Week 12 Game of the Week: Montana Grizzlies @ Montana State Bobcats: How to Watch, Preview, Predictions | Underdog Dynasty
DATES SEEN: ['December 20, 2025', 'Nov 19 2026', 'Nov 19, 2026', 'November 20, 2026']

Montana has made it clear all season long that it butters its bread on offense. The Griz offense can score points almost at will, averaging 39.9 points per contest. At the heart of it is star running back Eli Gillman. Gillman, who now holds the Big Sky record for career touchdowns with 80, is the best breakaway threat in the entire country. Idaho State did a good job last week of containing him but that’s no easy feat and no one knows it better than this Bobcats defense. Last year between their two matchups, Gillman rumbled for 238 yards and two scores against MSU. Stopping #10 has to be top priority for Vigen’s team.
Doing that, though, will leave quarterback Keali’i Ah Yat a little more free to sling it around and that’s something he does extremely well. Ah Yat has thrown 3,195 yards this year (third in FCS) along with 30 touchdowns. With receivers like Brooks Davis at his disposal that comes as no surprise. Davis is the best pass catcher in the Big Sky right now with 1,314 yards and 12 touchdowns. If Ah Yat places the ball anywhere in his vicinity, there’s a good chance he’s coming down with it. Between Gillman, Davis and Ah Yat, the Montana State defense is going to have its hands full.
One thing the Bobcats have going for them on that side of the ball, though, is a very stout defensive line. Led by defensive ends Zac Crews and Seth Brock, the trenches have been a game-wrecking part of the equation of MSU this year. Crews and Brock have a combined 11 sacks between them this year along with 15 tackles for loss, five QB hits and two forced fumbles. That pair will look to rough up a Montana O line that has only allowed ten sacks this year. Getting pressure on Ah Yat will not be easy but guys like Crews and Brock are more equip to do it than most that the Griz have seen up to this point.
It’s the back end of the defense that’s been suspect for the ‘Cats at times this year. Allowing nearly 230 yards per game through the air this year, Montana State will have to shore things up against Davis and company. The good news for them? Last year’s Big Sky Defensive Player of the Year is back there. Safety Caden Dowler, who authored a pick six against these Grizzlies last November, will be patrolling the secondary and seldom is he out of position. Dowler will make his plays. It’s guys like Takhari Carr, Tayden Gray and J.J. Dolan that will have to step up and solidify what has been the shakiest part of MSU’s game.
Prediction
This has the chance to be one of the higher scoring Brawls there’s been in a while. Both defenses have struggled at times this season to keep opposing offenses off the field and off of the scoreboard. It could be a track meet of a game but it may very well also turn into a game of keep-away. The best defense for both units might end up being long, sustained offensive possessions. If that is indeed the case, that style of play favors MSU more than it does UM. Guys like Lamson, Jones and White will be able to keep the chains moving and the Griz offense on the sideline. Montana will be able to strike quickly when they do have the ball and that will result in some fast points but in the long run it may end up actually hurting their defense more. Last season Montana State was able to salt the game away at the end with a 14-play, seven-minute drive. That run game paired with homefield advantage will serve strong for MSU in the late stages and the Bobcats will take a close one.
