TITLE: The picks are in for Penn football’s Friday night showdown against Yale - The Daily Pennsylvanian
DATES SEEN: ['Oct. 17, 2026', 'Oct. 18, 2026', 'Oct. 22, 2026']

Daniel Obstgarten, Sports associate
The Quakers have a tough test ahead against the Bulldogs, but senior wide receiver and defensive back Jayden Drayton has that
dog in him
. Drayton stood out for Penn last week against Columbia, exploding out for a 66-yard kickoff return and pacing the Quaker defense with five pass breakups. The rest of the defense held their own, led by senior defensive back Josh Narcisse’s seven tackles and junior defensive back Ty Cortes’ five-tackle, one-interception performance.
Even though the defense gave up four touchdowns last week, a few things work in their favor heading into this game. Yale doesn’t have the best receiver in the FCS, and the Quakers’ offense surely won’t throw four interceptions again this week. Even with that, the defense still stood strong when they needed to, holding Columbia to one touchdown the entire second half. The defense doesn’t need to reinvent itself: it just needs an offense to back it up.
Next, of course, is the question of which Penn offense we will see — the high-flying, the disastrous, or the stagnant. Place your bets on the first. After the interceptions started pouring in, it looked like the offense quickly lost focus and came out in the second half uninspired. Still, junior quarterback Karson Siqueiros-Lasky threw for nearly 400 yards, with top targets freshman wide receiver Mason Page and sophomore wide receiver Isaac Lee each notching more than 100 receiving yards. After a full week of rest, recovery, and rethinking, coach Rick Santos will have that offense ready to bat against one of the tougher defenses Penn has faced this season.
And then give me sophomore kicker Mason Walters to walk it off for the Quakers. This season of Penn football really does need something special, and that would be a fitting end to this three-game home stand.
Penn 35, Yale 60

Grant Han, Sports reporter
After a meltdown of epic proportions last week at home against Columbia, the Red and Blue desperately need a spark of some kind to get their season back on track. Unfortunately for the Quaker faithful, that spark will not be happening this week.
The Yale team that Penn will face this Saturday looks, by far, to be the best team that the Quakers have faced thus far. Penn’s previous opponents have all had a glaring weakness of some sort, whether that be Lehigh’s pass game or Dartmouth’s defense. The Bulldogs, however, are truly a complete team.
Yale quarterback Dante Reno is having a very efficient season, posting 1,182 yards through the air and 13 passing touchdowns with only two turnovers thus far. Backing him up is an extremely diverse and potent arsenal of weapons. Bulldog receivers Jaxton Santiago, Lucius Anderson, and tight end Graham Smith are all having extremely productive seasons. If the pass game fizzles out, Yale’s offense can always turn to running back Hugo Djeumeni Kemeni, who is averaging 5.5 yards per carry on 659 rushing yards.
And that’s not even mentioning the Bulldogs’ defense, which held Dartmouth’s attack to just seven points in their matchup earlier this season.
However, that’s not to say the Bulldogs are invincible. No team is after all. Yale has looked streaky this season, losing to both Rhode Island and Cornell. It’s safe to say that this does not look like the Yale team so used to dominating the Ivy League. What I’m trying to say is that there exists a parallel universe out there where the Quakers pull off the upset, but sadly it is not this one.
Penn 10, Yale 30
