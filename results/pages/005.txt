TAMPA BAY (-8) vs Dallas
Lean – Over 51.5
Tampa Bay started last season a bit slowly but the defending Super Bowl champs will likely pick up where they left off with continuity on their side this time around. Tom Brady had to deal with a covid offseason his first year in Tampa Bay and the Buccaneers started 7-5 but went on an eight-game winning streak after their bye in week 13. Tampa Bay’s full season offensive numbers should improve in 2021 and our model ranks them 2
nd
on that side of the ball.
The Buccaneers return every key piece on defense from the unit that held the Chiefs to 9 points in the Super Bowl. There’s reason to believe they could improve with the consistency in personnel and another year of experience for their young secondary. Safety Antoine Winfield, along with CBs Carlton Davis, Jamel Dean, and Sean Murphy-Bunting, have all been drafted since 2018.
Dallas quarterback Dak Prescott averaged 7.7 yards per pass play last season before going down with a broken ankle. Prescott also averaged 7.7 yppp in 2019 (3rd) when the Cowboys finished 6th in scoring. I’d be hard pressed to see Dallas finishing outside the top 10 in scoring this year if Prescott is healthy. The Cowboys offense will once again have one of the league’s best offensive lines with LT Tyron Smith and RT La’el Collins coming back from injuries. However, RG Zack Martin (4x All-Pro) will likely miss the opener due to covid.
Dan Quinn is probably one of the league’s worst defensive coordinators and Atlanta’s defense drastically improved when he gave up play calling in 2019. The Dallas defense could be one of the worst in the NFL with Quinn game-planning and calling the defensive plays. Linebacker is the most replaceable position in football and the Cowboys have used two first round picks in the last four years on Leighton Vander Esch and Micah Parsons as well as spending $9.8 million in cap space on Jaylon Smith. That’s just a waste of money and foolish team management.
Our model favors the Buccaneers by 8.3 points with a predicted total of 51.9 points. The matchups favor the over enough to lean that way at 52 or less.

Lean – Arizona (+3) over TENNESSEE
The Titans won the AFC South last year, but they will likely take a noticeable step back offensively despite the addition of Julio Jones, who is worth a point more than a replacement receiver. Jones is unlikely to meaningfully improve upon the production of Corey Davis, who averaged 2.58 yards per route run (4th) for Tennessee in 2020. Also, the drop off in play calling from new Falcons head coach Arthur Smith to new Titans offensive coordinator Todd Downing will be significant.
Ryan Tannehill was averaging 5.9 yards per pass play through his first six seasons with offensive play callers Mike Sherman, Bill Lazor, Zac Taylor, and Adam Gase. Tannehill has averaged 7.5 yppp the past two years with Arthur Smith. Tannehill is due to regress with the new OC, as I wouldn’t say Downing is notably better than Tannehill’s coaching in Miami. The 2017 Raiders were a league-average offense by our metrics in Downing’s only season calling plays after finishing 7th the previous year when they went 12-4. Tennessee’s offense finished 5th in scoring last season and they will probably take a step back similar to the offense that Downing led in Oakland.
The Titans did not do anything of note to address their defensive issues in the offseason. The only major change was swapping edge rusher Jadeveon Clowney for Bud Dupree (a slight downgrade) and we have Tennessee’s defense ranked 28th going into the season.
Arizona’s third-year quarterback Kyler Murray injured his shoulder in week 9 last season and landed on it in the first drive of the week 11 game against the Seahawks, which might’ve been a turning point in his year. Murray averaged 7.1 yppp and 67 rushing yards per game in the first 10 weeks of 2020 and only managed 5.7 yppp and 31 rushing yards per game down the stretch. The Cardinals finished the season 11th in our offensive metrics, and we think they will be above average in scoring again this year with a healthy Murray.
Arizona’s defense ranks around the league average according to our numbers since the start of 2019 with Vance Joseph in charge of that unit and we have them 16th going into 2021. The Cardinals blitzed on 43% of their opponent’s passing plays last season and the pass rush should improve with a healthy Chandler Jones, who was All-Pro in 2019 but played only five games in 2020 before his season-ending biceps surgery. JJ Watt and a full season of Markus Golden should adequately replace the production of edge rusher Haason Reddick.
Our model favors the Titans by just 0.6 points, with a predicted total of 49.4 points (the matchups favor the over though).

WASHINGTON (pk) vs LA Chargers
Lean – Over (44.5)
Last season, the Rams conceded a league-low 4.7 yards per play while highlighting two elite talents (Jalen Ramsey and Aaron Donald) with defensive coordinator Brandon Staley at the helm. Staley is now the head coach of the Chargers and I expect him to forge a defense in the same mold this year featuring edge rusher Joey Bosa (three Pro Bowls in five seasons) and safety Derwin James, who was All-Pro as a rookie before missing all of 2020 with a torn meniscus.
Justin Herbert was one of the best quarterbacks under pressure last season and many are pointing out he is unlikely to sustain that performance in 2021. I disagree. In my research I did find having a high ratio of passing touchdowns coming under pressure is a negative signal because many of those are broken plays with inherent randomness. However, having a high average yards per attempt under pressure is a positive signal because it shows the ability to diagnose the blitz and still push the ball downfield. The highest ratios of pressure yards per attempt to non-pressure yards per attempt in the last two seasons were Herbert in 2020 and Patrick Mahomes in 2019.
Washington’s defense benefitted from five games against backup QBs last year and a sixth after Joe Burrow was injured in their week 11 game. Washington ultimately allowed the 6th-fewest points in the NFL, but I think they will regress back towards average this season with a tougher slate of opposing quarterbacks.
Washington should improve on offense, however, as Ryan Fitzpatrick showed his value in comparison with Tua Tagovailoa last year (7.2 yppp to 5.4 yppp) and will give Washington’s offense a different vertical dimension they didn’t have last season with Dwayne Haskins, Kyle Allen, and Alex Smith.
Our model makes Washington a 1.6-point favorite with a predicted total of 47.2 points.

Lean – ATLANTA (-3) vs Philadelphia
The Falcons should improve this year due to coaching upgrades on both sides of the ball. Ryan Tannehill was averaging 5.9 yards per pass play through his first six seasons with offensive play callers Mike Sherman, Bill Lazor, Zac Taylor, and Adam Gase. Tannehill has averaged 7.5 yppp the past two years with Arthur Smith as OC at Tennessee. Smith is now the head coach of the Falcons and veteran Matt Ryan has averaged 6.7 yppp the last four years since Kyle Shanahan left (averaged 8.3 yppp in 2016). I think it’s fair to say Smith will be the second-best play caller Ryan has had in his career behind Shanahan, and Atanta’s offense should be firmly above average in 2021.
The Falcons might surprise on defense this season despite their lower-level talent. The Titans also had poor talent on defense in 2019 but allowed 5.5 yppl (16th) due to play calling from defensive coordinator Dean Pees. Tennessee’s defense went on to surrender 5.9 yppl in 2020 without Pees. I’m not expecting much from Atlanta’s defense this year, but they could improve after allowing 6.2 yppl last season.
We seem to be right on market with the Eagles. Philadelphia has an above average offensive line, below average receivers, and Jalen Hurts will probably be one of the worst quarterbacks in the NFL. The Eagles have some talent on defense, but their top players are aging with DT Fletcher Cox, CB Darius Slay, DE Brandon Graham now all on the wrong side of 30.
Our model favors the Falcons by 4.4 points with a predicted total of 47.9 points. Lean with Atlanta at -3 or less.

Seattle (-2.5) vs INDIANAPOLIS
The quarterback change from Philip Rivers to Carson Wentz will define the Colts’ season. Wentz was one of the worst quarterbacks in the league last year by nearly every measure and he had foot surgery earlier this month that could affect his mobility throughout September. Wentz averaged 6.7 yppp (11th) in his last season with Frank Reich coaching him (2017) and I think he will be somewhere between that and his 4.7 yppp in 2020. The Indianapolis offense finished 16th in scoring and 24th in yards per play with Jacoby Brissett at the helm in 2019. Our model thinks that’s a good baseline, as we have the Colts offense ranked 19th heading into this season.
However, Indianapolis will be without two key starters on offense to start the year. Left Tackle Eric Fisher was just activated off the covid list but is still recovering from tearing his Achilles last January. Backup LT Sam Tevi was on track to bridge the gap until Fisher was ready, but he suffered a torn ACL and will miss the 2021 season. TY Hilton led the team in targets (93) and yards per route run (1.76) last season, but he had surgery to relieve a neck injury and will start the year on injured reserve. The Colts are 1.3 points worse on offense without Fisher, Tevi, and Hilton according to our metrics.
Seattle’s defense ranked league average last year in points allowed and likely will take a small step backwards in 2021 after losing some key contributors in coverage. The Seahawks will no longer have the services of top cornerback Shaquill Griffin and linebacker KJ Wright, both of whom conceded less than one yard per cover snap last season.
The Seahawks averaged 34.3 points per game in the first half of last year before scoring just 22.8 points per game from week 10 onwards. Brian Schottenheimer was fired and Seattle hired Shane Waldron to be the offensive coordinator. Pete Carroll has indicated the team will run the ball more often this season under Waldron, which takes the ball out of Russell Wilson’s hands. In general, I’m bearish on the new run-heavy offense with the new play caller but the floor for an offense with Wilson under center is still pretty high and we have the Seahawks rated 10th in our priors.
The Indianapolis defense conceded 22.9 points per game (11th) last season, but they are due for some regression after ranking 5th in takeaways, which is mostly due to variance. Our model has the Colts defense rated near the league average.
Our model favors the Seahawks by 0.8 points with a predicted total of 51.4 points.
I’d lean with Indy if the line gets up to +3

Minnesota (-3/-3.5) vs CINCINNATI
Lean with Cincy at +3.5
Minnesota’s defense had finished in the top third of the NFL in every season under Mike Zimmer prior to last year by our metrics and I expect the Vikings to significantly improve after surrendering 29.7 points per game (29th) in 2020. Edge rusher Danielle Hunter did not play in 2020 after making the Pro Bowl in both 2018 and 2019. DE Everson Griffen returns after finishing 23rd in pass rushing efficiency last year. The cornerbacks will be better bringing in solid veterans Bashaud Breeland and Patrick Peterson and Jeff Gladney and Cameron Danzler both should improve with another year of experience.
Minnesota’s offense probably has the lowest variance in the league, and I’d be surprised if they didn’t finish in the middle of the pack – as they have every season with Kirk Cousins under center.
Joe Burrow averaged 5.6 yppp last season (28th) but quarterbacks typically see the most improvement from year one to year two and Cincy’s offensive line will be much better. RT Riley Reiff ranked 10th in pass blocking efficiency last season and former first round pick LT Jonah Williams could make a jump after ranking 21st in pass block efficiency in his first year as a starter. Also, it would be hard for the Bengals to get worse production at left guard after Michael Jordan surrendered a 7.3% pressure rate in 2020.
Cincinnati’s defense doesn’t look noticeably better or worse than they were last season. The Bengals lost edge rusher Carl Lawson (14.6% pressure rate) and added edge rusher Trey Hendrickson (13.2% pressure rate). Cincinnati allowed 6.1 yards per play (28th) last year and our metrics have them ranked 27th to start 2021.

Lean – San Francisco (-8) over DETROIT
Lean – Over (46)
The 49ers had the season from hell in 2020 – losing 111 games from starters due to injury or covid. However, San Francisco was able to finish 14th in our end of season rankings due to excellent coaching by Kyle Shanahan. We’re putting the 49ers back to where they were in our priors before the start of last season, which is 4 points better than an average team. San Francisco’s offense was 8th, and their defense was 5th by our metrics during their NFC title winning season two years ago and those serve as solid benchmarks for my expectations going into 2021.
Detroit’s defense surrendered a league-high 32.4 points per game last year and their only addition is interior rusher Michael Brockers, who is worth a half point at most. The Lions will likely produce more than the 0.8 takeaways per game they had in 2020 and it would be difficult for new defensive coordinator Aaron Glenn to be worse than Matt Patricia, but our model has Detroit’s defense ranked firmly at 31st.
Detroit’s quarterback/offensive coordinator combo of Jared Goff and Anthony Lynn will likely be a disaster. Goff has benefitted from Sean McVay’s masterful scheme and play-calling for years. I don’t think Goff will go back to averaging 3.8 yards per pass play like he did the season before McVay, but I could certainly see less than 6 yppp this year. The Lions lost all three starting WRs (Kenny Golladay, Marvin Jones, Danny Amendola) and Goff’s only weapon above replacement level is tight end TJ Hockenson. Lynn will be one of the few play callers in the NFL that still thinks running the football is the key to offensive success.
Our model favors the 49ers by 9.3 points, with a predicted total of 48.9 points.

KANSAS CITY (-6) vs Cleveland
These teams met in the playoffs last year and the spread was Chiefs -8 with a total of 56.5. The home field shouldn’t be too much different with the extra fans (no longer limited capacity) making up for the non-playoff environment (when home advantage goes up). The scoring conditions will be about a point better than they were on January 17th in Kansas City. The market is currently suggesting the Browns have made up 2 points of ground on the Chiefs and the total should be 2-3 points lower.
Cleveland’s defense added safety John Johnson, edge rusher Jadeveon Clowney, cornerback Troy Hill, and linebacker Anthony Walker. Those four players are worth about 2 points compared to replacement level according to our metrics. CB Greedy Williams conceded less than 1 yard per cover snap as a rookie in 2019 but missed the entirety of last season.
All 11 offensive starters return for the Browns after averaging 26.3 points per game (13th) last year, but it’s easy to forget they had three consecutive rain games in the middle of season with average winds of 23 mph. Our metrics show Cleveland played in conditions more than 2 points worse than average in 2020 and now they get three-time Pro Bowl WR Odell Beckham back healthy. We expect Baker Mayfield and the Browns to rank inside the top 10 of scoring.
Should be same old, same old for Kansas City this season. Our model has the offense rated 2 points better than anyone else after addressing issues along the line. The defense will be propelled by playing with leads generated by the offense and will likely finish around 10th in points allowed again.
Our model favors the Chiefs by 6.7 with a predicted total of 53.3. I think there’s a chance the market and our model might be regressing these offenses down too much and the total should probably be at least 55.

New England (-3) vs MIAMI
The Patriots are starting rookie QB Mac Jones and his weapons will be at least a point better than Cam Newton’s were last season with the additions of TE Jonnu Smith, TE Hunter Henry, and WR Nelson Agholor. New England’s offensive line ranked 9th in pass blocking efficiency last year and could move into the top 5 after the signing of RT Trent Brown (Pro Bowl his last full season in 2019).
Bill Belichick will have added talent defensively as well. The Patriots get 2019 Pro Bowl linebacker Dont’a Hightower back after he opted out last year because of covid. The front seven also adds edge rushers Matthew Judon and Kyle Van Noy (combined 70 pressures last season). New England’s defense will start the year ranked 8th by our metrics and has the chance to be elite when CB Stephon Gilmore (PUP list) joins in the middle of the year.
It is very unlikely Tua Tagovailoa will be able to match the 7.2 yppp Miami got from Ryan Fitzpatrick last season (9th), as Tua averaged just 5.4 yppp. The Dolphins offense was slightly above league average in 2020 but our model has them dropping to 22nd this year even with Tagovailoa likely to improve in his second season.
Miami’s defensive personnel and scheme will be basically the same in 2021 but the Dolphins gained 2.5 wins due to takeaways last season, which is not repeatable, and the defense will go backwards.
Our model favors the Patriots by 2.3 points with a predicted total of 43.8 points.

Lean – NY GIANTS (+3) vs Denver
This game rightly has the lowest total of week 1 with two offenses that might finish in the bottom 10 of the NFL facing two of the league’s best defensive play callers in Vic Fangio and Patrick Graham.
Denver’s defense will start the season 2nd in our model. Edge rusher Bradley Chubb finished 12th in pass rushing efficiency last year and he should improve with Von Miller (back after missing the entirety of 2020) drawing attention on the other side. The Broncos add former Bears All-Pro CB Kyle Fuller, who conceded only 0.72 yards per cover snap last season. Denver’s defense also retains Pro Bowl safety Justin Simmons and Bryce Callahan, one of only four qualifying cornerbacks in the NFL to not allow a touchdown all of last season. The Broncos defense has similar talent to the 2018 Bears that Fangio conducted to conceding only 17.6 points per game (best in the NFL).
New York’s secondary is probably one of the league’s most underrated. Adoree’ Jackson fought through injuries last season (only 154 snaps) but the Titans were a half yard per pass play better in the 11 games Jackson played in 2019 compared to the five games he missed (not including week 17 versus Houston’s backups). Jabrill Peppers and Logan Ryan form one of the most versatile safety tandems in the NFL as their 19 combined pressures ranked 3rd. James Bradberry conceded just one reception every 13.8 cover snaps in 2020 (12th).
Teddy Bridgewater likely takes a step backwards with a significant drop in offensive coordinator from Joe Brady to Pat Shurmur. Bridgewater averaged less than 6 yppp before playing under Sean Payton (in New Orleans) and Brady (in Carolina) the past three seasons.
Saquon Barkley returns after missing most of 2020 but the most impactful upgrade to New York’s offense will likely be WR Kenny Golladay, who led the league with 16 receptions with 20+ air yards in 2019 before dealing with a hip-flexor injury last year. However, my confidence is low for the combination of offensive coordinator Jason Garrett and quarterback Daniel Jones. The Giants have around a 50/50 chance to finish the year as one of the worst five offenses in the NFL again.
Our model favors the Broncos by 1.4 points, with a predicted total of 42.0 points.

Lean – LAS VEGAS (+4.5) over Baltimore
The Raiders defense will improve with defensive coordinator Gus Bradley and with more turnovers. Vegas was one of three teams to average less than one takeaway per game but defensive turnovers tend to regress towards the mean from one season to the next. Bradley will bring structure to Las Vegas and his conservative zone scheme has a high floor. The Chargers ranked 15th, 11th, 15th, and 15th in yppl allowed during Bradley’s four seasons calling the plays. Las Vegas made some solid acquisitions with CB Casey Hayward and LB KJ Wright worth 1.2 points combined according to our numbers.
The Ravens finished last season with 12.0 pythagorean wins despite dealing with plenty of bad breaks including All-Pro LT Ronnie Stanley going on IR in week 6 and playing without a ton of starters due to covid in 12. Baltimore’s offense still finished 5th by our metrics despite the adversity, after rating 1st in 2019. I expect the group led by Lamar Jackson to be near the top of the league in 2021.
The Ravens allowed 5.1 yards per play (6th) last season and they will have one of the best defenses in the NFL again. The only major change is edge rusher Justin Houston replacing Matthew Judon, which is a wash by our metrics.
Las Vegas ranked 10th in scoring last year but I think they will take a step backwards in 2021. Jon Gruden’s scheme relies on winning up front, but the Raiders lost three starters on the line this offseason. Our priors have the Las Vegas offense rated 18
th
heading into the season.
West coast teams are 36-16 straight up and 31-20-1 ATS against east coast teams on Sunday/Monday night football since 1989 due to the timezone benefit. It is much better for a player’s internal clock in the second half to be at 7pm than 10pm for peak performance.
Our model favors the Ravens by just 2.5 points with a predicted total of 50.2 points after adjusting for Ravens’ star CB Marcus Peters being out for the season (tore his ACL in practice).
