Spread: UNM -6.5
Preview:
It's fitting that a game that will feel more like war than sport would end up involving one of the branches of our military. New Mexico and Air Force have two of the more dominant defenses in the conference (UNM has the best in the country), paired with run-heavy offenses that can pound their opponents into the ground at every given opportunity. The outcome of this game will contribute heavily to determining who ends up getting into the Mountain West Championship Game. If New Mexico wins, they are in and the Falcons are out. If the Falcons win, they and the Lobos will end up being lumped in with all other teams in the conference with two losses and go to computer metrics to determine who gets to duke it out for a title.
Air Force has the best rushing offense in the nation, averaging an absurd 351.7 YPG on the ground. New Mexico ranks second in the conference, though their 210.9 YPG is a far cry from that of their northern foes. The Lobos win games via their nation-leading defense forcing turnovers and three-and-outs, allowing their offense to have short fields nearly every time they come onto the field. Unfortunately, Toa Fa'avae has completely wasted their efforts for most of the season, but Luke Moga's performance as the starting QB last week against Wyoming (234 passing yards, no turnovers) leads me to believe the Lobos now have a man under center who can be relied on to take pressure off of their stout defense.
I believe the Lobos will come out on top in this contest. The Lobos' greatest strength is their run defense, which is the best in the conference and third-best in the country (73.5 YPGA on the ground). Yes, the Falcons have a run game unlike anything the Lobos have seen so far, but the Falcons genuinely struggled to run the ball for three quarters in their early-season contest against the Bison, who have another of the best run defenses in the conference. If Luke Moga can limit mistakes and lead the offense to consistent production on first and second down, the Lobos will find themselves in front for most of the game, in which case they will force the Falcons to throw the ball in an effort to keep up. This, of course, is a task foreign to Air Force, and one or two critical interceptions will doom them to fall in the end, leading to the Lobos clinching their very first berth in the Mountain West Championship Game.
Prediction: New Mexico 23, Air Force 13
UConn Huskies (6-5) at Wyoming Cowboys (7-4)
Date and Time:
Spread: CONN -3
Preview:
This week features two true nonconference showdowns along with the aforementioned conference clash with no stakes, the first of which will take place in Laramie as the return game of a 24-22 Cowboy win in East Hartford back in 2021. The Cowboys and Huskies have both clinched bowl invites if there are enough bids to go around, though the Cowboys have a bit more to play for here given they still have an outside chance to reach the MW Championship Game if they can get themselves high enough in the conference's all-important computer metrics. That pursuit makes this game a must-win for the Cowboys despite the fact that winning would not enhance their conference record.
UConn gives up an average of 25.7 PPG defensively, giving up an insane 256.6 YPG through the air, which ranks 131st in the FBS. This is an obvious weakness the Cowboys could theoretically take advantage of, but sadly, Tyler Hughes is looking set to be out once again in this contest. Don't get me wrong, Landon Sims has done a good job subbing for Hughes in the last couple of games, especially in the ground game. Unfortunately, that brings us to one of UConn's greatest strengths as a team: their run defense, which ranks 43rd in the country (130.1 YPG). Meanwhile, the Huskies are ranked inside the top 30 in the nation in rushing offense, which matches up well against a Wyoming defense which ranks in the bottom half of the Mountain West in rush yards allowed.
This game truly hinges on whether or not Tyler Hughes is ready to play. If he is, I think the Cowboys will win due to what he brings both passing and running the ball. If he isn't, which seems likelier given the nature of his injury, I think the Huskies will be able to limit Sims' impact throughout the game and emerge with their seventh win of the year. If UConn wins this game, that would likely eliminate the Cowboys from MW title contention, which would make this year a massive "what if" when reviewing a season that could have been truly special for Wyoming if Hughes had stayed healthy.
Prediction: UConn 23, Wyoming 17
UTEP Miners (1-10) at Northern Illinois Huskies (0-11)
Date and Time:
MW+
Spread: NIU -4.5
Preview:
Welcome, one and all, to the Toilet Bowl. In a week full of fantastic rivalry games the likes of Michigan-Ohio State, Auburn-Alabama and USC-UCLA, I implore you to ignore all of those contests while this one is going on. This is the game of the year in college football, don't let any of those useless naysayers tell you otherwise. UTEP and NIU are two of just four teams in the nation with 10 or more losses this season, being in the bottom three of nearly every 138-team CFB ranking, with some having them as the two worst teams in the country. This is the ultimate Sickos game, being played less for pride and more out of desperation. This fact is especially true for the Huskies, who are looking to avoid infamy of going 0-12 in their debut season in the MW.
For the first time all season, NIU finds themselves favored in a game. This is a statement less about recent performance, rather being about how dysfunctional UTEP has been this season. The Miners have a win due to playing a Texas Southern team that is currently 3-8 in a weak FCS conference. Against FBS opponents, the Miners have been outscored by an average of 28.4 PPG, including last week's 52-3 shellacking at the hands of Air Force. Meanwhile, the Huskies were outscored by 26.7 PPG in their first six FBS games of the season, but have turned their performance around drastically as of late. Over their last four games, they have been outscored by 11.3 PPG, including a victory robbed from them by terrible officiating against UNLV and coming just short of a 28-point comeback win last week against North Dakota State. NIU has just not been able to finish games when they have the chance, which surely has them frustrated going into a game that is by far their most winnable of the season.
Call it bold, but I think the misery finally ends for the Huskies in this week's contest. UTEP just fired HC Scotty Walden two weeks ago, looking like a completely lost outfit in their first game without him. The Miners also have the worst offense in the country, scoring just 13.2 PPG on average. Even that is misleading, as that total is heavily buoyed by their 51-point outburst against Texas Southern. In their other 10 games, the Miners have scored an abominable 9.4 PPG. Granted, the Huskies are hardly much better (16.5 PPG), but NIU has scored at least 19 points in five of their last six games, whereas the Miners have yet to do so in a single FBS game this season. This leads me to believe this game will be a low-scoring affair, which benefits the Huskies and their (slightly) superior defense. Either way, this game will be a post-Thanksgiving treat for anyone brave enough to tune in.
Prediction: Northern Illinois 20, UTEP 13
Preview:
Finally, we reach the last regular-season game of the 2026 season. Hawaii hosts Sacramento State in a nonconference showdown that will serve as the finale for a pair of teams missing a bowl game. While the Hornets absolutely expected this outcome given this is their first season as an FBS team, the Warriors entered this season with hopes of a Mountain West title run. Yet, both teams are playing in the same type of meaningless game in the final week of the season. This is the first meeting between these teams, and as such, there is no past precedent to go off of here.
Hawaii's three wins have all come against the dregs of their schedule. New Mexico State, UTEP and NIU have combined for a pitiful 4-29 record this season, and the lowly Hornets have just one FBS win on their resume this season (31-10 win over 3-9 Kent State in Week 9). Outside of that performance, the Hornets have had just one of their losses come by a single-digit margin, and only three losses that came by less than 20. The Hornets are a far cry from their 2026 debut brethren in Fargo, and will pose next to no threat to the 'Bows under normal circumstances.
Unfortunately, it was announced this week that the 'Bows will be without QB Micah Alejado for the rest of the season due to his devastating injury suffered in last week's loss to Nevada. This will put the 'Bows heavily behind the 8-ball here, and backup Bjorn Jurgensen will likely struggle a bit in his first start. Even still, I think the Warriors will take care of business at home and send their seniors off with a win.
Prediction: Hawaii 24, Sac State 14
