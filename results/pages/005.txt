He opened the season with three consecutive 300-yard passing performances. According to Sam Houston’s official game notes, he became the program’s first quarterback to begin a season that way since Eric Schmid opened the spring 2021 season with four straight 300-yard games.
The receiving production has come from multiple targets.
Reed leads the Bearkats with 220 receiving yards and three touchdown catches. Grady O’Neill has a team-leading 22 receptions for 216 yards, while Burns has turned seven catches into 189 yards and two touchdowns, averaging 27 yards per reception.
Longo said he likes the offense’s short, intermediate and deep passing components. He also explained that an efficient passing play depends on the available reads rather than a single yardage requirement.
If a defense covers deeper routes and leaves a shorter completion available, taking that throw can still put the offense in a favorable situation.
The next step is giving that passing game more support on the ground.
Liberty’s late-game defense creates a significant test
One of the matchup’s most notable early storylines is how Liberty has finished games.
For Longo, the conference opener offers a chance to establish a different direction after the nonconference portion of the schedule.
“You want to start it off the right way if you want to make a run in this conference,” he said.
The Liberty game also begins four consecutive October midweek contests for Sam Houston. The Bearkats return home to face Western Kentucky on Wednesday, Oct. 14, host New Mexico State on Wednesday, Oct. 21, and visit Missouri State on Tuesday, Oct. 27.
Longo said the staff has an established plan for managing that schedule, with adjustments available when recovery needs dictate.
The immediate focus, however, remains on the first conference opponent.
“We’re not looking ahead,” Longo said. “You can’t when you’re playing a team like Liberty.”
