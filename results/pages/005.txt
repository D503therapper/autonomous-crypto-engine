Can Ball State football avoid complacency after big win?
After a 1-5 start to the season, Ball State needed last week's win badly to keep things from spiraling. The thrilling fashion in which they snatched victory from the jaws of defeat also gave them an added dose of momentum and confidence. The Cardinals now get to return to Scheumann Stadium — where they are 5-2 under Uremovich — and try to get a winning streak going in front of a homecoming crowd.
On the other side of this week's matchup is a flat-out bad Sac State team. There's no sugarcoating the fact that the Hornets' first season at the FBS level has been miserable — they are averaging just 8.8 points per game against FBS competition and sit dead last in the MAC in both scoring offense (10 points per game) and scoring defense (37.3 points allowed per game) during conference play.

Luster isn't the only piece of the offense that is clicking. TJ Horton has returned to form after battling an injury that limited him at the start of conference play and had a strong day against Bowling Green, averaging over five yards per carry and scoring twice. Meanwhile, it seems like a different receiver serves as the primary target each week — Jabari Smith at Northwestern, Donovan Hamilton vs. Toledo, then CJ Nelson at Bowling Green — which is indicative of a deep and well-rounded stable of playmakers.
Sac State's defense does present its challenges, though, especially for those receivers. 6-foot-4 cornerback Dionte Thornton was called "probably the best corner in the conference" by Uremovich, while senior safety Boogsie Silvera is another one of the Hornets' top defenders. While they haven't been good statistically, the Hornets are a physical unit that could give Ball State some trouble if the Cardinals fail to execute properly.
Will Ball State defense show continued improvement?
After a sluggish start to the season, Ball State's defense finally showed signs of life at Bowling Green. The Cardinals forced the Falcons to punt on each of their first three drives, they sacked quarterback Jay Kastantin four times after recording zero sacks in their first two MAC games, and they came up with their first interception in MAC play with a crucial redzone pick by Will Yates. They did still get exposed in some moments — namely a 77-yard touchdown given up on a screen pass — but overall, they played their most complete game of the season.
