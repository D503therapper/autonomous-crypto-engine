The series shifts to Tucson this fall, where Cincinnati is riding the high of knocking off another Big 12 Wildcat — Kansas State — in an electrifying 31-26 atmosphere last Saturday. However, the Bearcats must leave the confines of their city for the first time and battle an Arizona team that is 8-1 in its last nine home games, standing a double-overtime loss to BYU away from perfection.
Cincinnati Bearcats outlook

Cincinnati (4-0, 1-0 Big 12) is perfect through four games for the first time since its 2021 College Football Playoff run. The path to 4-0 wasn't exactly seamless as the Bearcats overcame double-digits deficits in back-to-back weeks. They stormed back from 21 down to Miami (OH) and climbed out of a 10-0 hole against Kansas State.

The Wildcats only led FCS Northern Arizona 7-0 after the first quarter in Week 1. Then the past two weeks, they trailed Northern Illinois 10-0 and Washington State 14-0 before recollecting themselves on both sides of the ball. Arizona won all three of those games by double-digits, but the team cannot afford a similar start against 4-0 Cincinnati.
One luxury Arizona has in this matchup is Noah Fifita. Fifita became the program's all-time leading passer last November against the Bearcats, and the fourth-year starter is off to a phenomenal 2026, owning the third-highest completion percentage among quarterbacks with 100 attempts (75.6) to go along with 1,131 passing yards and 11 touchdowns to two interceptions. Arizona's passing game ranks 16th nationally, and the Wildcats boast quite the arsenal as the six different wide receivers have at least 100 yards on the year. Having a wide variety of options pays dividends on third down, and Arizona owns the sixth-highest conversion rate in the country at 56 percent.
Gio Richardson was on pace to be the top receiver after the first two weeks but hasn't played since Sept. 12 at BYU due to a leg injury. Tre Spivey has stepped up as the No. 1 option in his place, recording team-highs in receiving yards (278) and touchdowns (4) — producing 219 and three, respectively, since Richardson's injury. Chris Hunter and Isaiah Mizell are among the other weapons in an offense that spreads the wealth as well as anyone in the Big 12.
Arizona's running back room also appeared to have significant depth with the quartet of Ismail Mahdi, Kedrick Reescano, Antwan Roberts, and Quincy Craig. However, the Wildcats haven't established the run yet. They rank 108th nationally in ground offense, and the offensive line has regularly struggled to assist the unit. The offensive line issues translate to the passing game as well, and only 10 FBS teams surrender more sacks per game than the Wildcats.
