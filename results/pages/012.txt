Wake Forest Football vs. Stanford: Kickoff Time, TV Channel, Injuries & What to Know
Wake Forest and Stanford meet Saturday in Winston-Salem after both teams scored ACC upsets last week
By
Eric Lusk
Two resurgent quarterbacks and two teams aiming to play way above preseason expectations will meet in Winston-Salem this Saturday as Stanford visits Wake Forest in ACC action.
The game kicks off at noon at Allegacy Federal Credit Union Stadium and will be broadcast on the ACC Network.
Wake Forest features former South Alabama and UNC quarterback Gio Lopez, who is quietly having one of the best seasons of his college career.
Stanford counters with Davis Warren, a cancer survivor who transferred from Michigan after missing the 2025 season while recovering from an ACL injury. Warren likewise has started 2026 in prime form.
Here is everything you need to know about Stanford-Wake Forest, including kickoff time, TV and radio information, betting odds stat comparisons, injury updates, series history and more.

What to Know About Stanford-Wake Forest
Wake Forest is overachieving again
Wake Forest was picked near the bottom of the ACC in 2025, Jake Dickert’s first season as head coach. The Demon Deacons responded by winning nine games, upsetting a ranked SMU team at home and knocking off an SEC foe (Mississippi State) in the Duke’s Mayo Bowl.
Wake was picked 13th in this year’s preseason media poll, and so far the Demon Deacons are punching above their weight once again. Dickert’s team put a second-half scare into ACC favorite Miami and then upset 16th ranked Louisville (picked third) this past weekend to improve to 3-1. Since replacing Dave Clawson, Dickert is 12-5 at Wake Forest.
Stanford was picked to finish dead last in the ACC, 17th out of 17 teams. But ACC foes may not want to overlook the Cardinal either. Stanford upset Georgia Tech, picked fifth in the preseason, this past weekend.
Davis Warren’s remarkable road to Stanford
Stanford quarterback Davis Warren’s journey to Saturday’s game at Wake Forest has been anything but conventional. Diagnosed with leukemia at 17, Warren underwent chemotherapy before returning to football, eventually walking on at Michigan and working his way into the starting lineup. He helped the Wolverines finish the 2024 season with wins over Ohio State and Alabama, but tore his ACL in the bowl victory over the Crimson Tide and missed the entire 2025 season before transferring to Stanford for a fresh start.
So far, Warren has made the most of his new surroundings. He threw for a career-high 396 yards and three touchdowns in last week’s 34-27 win over Georgia Tech, earning ACC Quarterback of the Week honors. Warren has also attempted 143 consecutive passes without an interception, the longest active streak in FBS, giving a player who has already overcome plenty another impressive chapter to his story.
