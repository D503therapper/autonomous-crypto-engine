NY JETS (pick) vs Denver
Undrafted free agent Brett Rypien will get the start under center this week for the Broncos. This offense is gaining just 4.1 yards per play the last two weeks without starting QB Drew Lock and I’m not optimistic about Denver’s chances on Thursday night with a guy that couldn’t even make the initial 53-man roster.
The Jets have the 6th-highest quarterback hit percentage and rank 8th in blitz percentage by defensive backs. I expect Gregg Williams to continue aggressively blitzing players from the secondary this week just like Pittsburgh and Tampa Bay did in their games versus the Broncos. Denver surrendered 4 sacks to defensive backs over the past two weeks and this is one area the Jets could have success in.
The Jets are the only team in the NFL that has not run an offensive snap while leading in any game on the season. New York has trailed by double-digits nearly 50% more than the next closest team (Washington). However, I do believe the Jets will be able to put up more points on Thursday night. New York has been unlucky to score a touchdown on only a third of their trips to the Redzone, a number that should significantly improve given that they were 55% touchdowns in the Redzone last season.
Vic Fangio’s defenses have always been excellent at defending inside receivers and his nickelbacks are on pace for the fourth straight season ranked in top 10 in yards per cover snap allowed in the slot. Essang Bassey should neutralize Braxton Berrios in this matchup, but it looks like the Jets will have Jameison Crowder back this week to provide a secondary option for Sam Darnold.
Meanwhile, Denver’s injury situation is looking bleak. In addition to Lock, the Broncos look like they’ll also be without top WR Courtland Sutton and their top two right tackles on offense and DT Jurrell Casey, superstar DE Von Miller on the defensive line (13 combined Pro Bowls from those D-Linemen), and top CB AJ Bouye (injured reserve) on their defense.
Our model makes this game near pick, with a predicted total of 42.6 points, but the Jets do apply to a 70-24-1 ATS contrary situation based on how badly they’ve performed thus far.

Arizona (-3.5) vs CAROLINA
The Cardinals are playing fast and only getting faster, recording a season-high 53% no-huddle rate last week. Kyler Murray seems more comfortable in Kliff Kingbury’s offense this year and DeAndre Hopkins is proving to be a great offseason addition – leading the NFL in receiving yards. However, Hopkins will line up opposite Carolina’s best CB Donte Jackson on Sunday, so perhaps he’ll be slowed a bit.
Panthers’ QB Teddy Bridgewater still seems to be relying on passes to players coming out of the backfield even without Christian McCaffrey, as their backup running back Mike Davis had 9 receptions last week. The Cardinals are surrendering over 10 yards per target to opposing running backs so expect Davis to have even more success in the passing game this week.
Carolina’s top wide receiver, Robby Anderson, is gaining 2.76 yards per route run (10th) and has a difficult matchup against 8-time Pro Bowl cornerback Patrick Peterson this week. However, there may be space to get behind Peterson for the speedy WR as Arizona’s defense will be without safety Budda Baker due to thumb surgery. Baker is worth 0.7 points by our metrics.
Our model favors the Cardinals by 5.1 points, with a predicted total of 50.9 points but the matchup favors Carolina and the Panthers’ upset win last week should put some wind in their sails after starting the season with two losses.
Game 4 underdogs of more than 1 point are 53-21-3 ATS after winning their first game of the season in week 3

Lean – Under (56) – DALLAS (-4.5) vs Cleveland
The Cowboys have run just seven offensive snaps with the lead so far through three weeks, and being in catch-up mode is why they are playing at the fastest pace in the NFL and why Dak Prescott is on pace to throw for 6000 passing yards this season. In contrast, the Browns have run the ball at the highest rate in the NFL and lead the league in runs of 10 or more yards through three weeks with 19. The Dallas rush defense ranks 4th and Baker Mayfield will need to throw the ball to exploit the Cowboys on Sunday.
Cowboys CB Jourdan Lewis is conceding a league-low 0.30 yards per cover snap in the slot and should limit Jarvis Landry on the inside. However, if ever there was a game for Odell Beckham to get going, this would be it. Beckham is averaging a career-low 7.0 yards per target but the Dallas secondary, outside of their nickelback (Lewis) has been a mess. CB Trevon Diggs is surrendering 1.80 yards per cover snap (62nd out of 72 qualifiers) and Beckham should finally have a good game.
Both quarterbacks are likely to be under pressure in this matchup. Cowboys edge rusher Aldon Smith had 8 pressures and 3 sacks last Sunday, leading all defenders in week 3. Smith should cause problems for rookie LT Jedrick Wills, who has already surrendered 2 sacks in the first 3 weeks. Meanwhile, Dallas is still likely going to be without their two starting tackles (Smith and Collins) and Myles Garret ranks 5th in pass-rushing efficiency of the edge.
Ezekiel Elliott is not off to a great start in 2020. Elliott played 80 of 82 snaps last week but had just 58 yards and he had a team-high 12 targets but he only caught half of those for 24 yards. Cleveland’s rush defense ranks 2nd and it could be another rough outing for Elliott. However, Dak Prescott could catch a break with Denzel Ward (0.76 yards allowed per cover snap, 13th) nursing a groin injury (check status).
Our model favors the Cowboys by 4.3 with a predicted total of 54.9 and I’ll lean under based on some value and matchups that should keep this game lower scoring than expected.

Lean – LA Chargers (+7) over TAMPA BAY
The Chargers went from a 3% target share to running backs with Tyrod Taylor under center to 25% over the past two weeks with Justin Herbert. Lavonte David is still one of the best coverage linebackers in the NFL with 12.4 coverage snaps per reception allowed (8th) and he should limit Austin Ekler receiving out of the backfield.
When the rookie is not dumping the ball off to a back he’s focused on top receiver Keenan Allen, who has a 35.5% target share over the past two weeks with Herbert. Sean Murphy-Bunting’s status will be worth monitoring for Sunday, as the nickelback is surrendering just 0.87 yards per cover snap in the slot (5th) and should neutralize Allen if he can suit up. Otherwise, Allen could have another productive afternoon. Mike Williams is banged-up with a hamstring injury and likely will struggle versus CB Carlton Davis, allowing only 0.75 yards per cover snap (11th).
Todd Bowles’ defense did a good job pressuring Denver’s quarterbacks on 16 occasions last week. With C Mike Pouncey, RG Trai Turner, and RT Bryan Bulaga getting hurt, the Chargers’ offensive line is once again facing extreme injury issues. Both Vita Vea and Shaquil Barrett have pass-rush win rates over 20% and should feast on Sunday.
The Chargers will also be without Chris Harris, one of the best nickelbacks in the NFL, but the Buccaneers lost slot receiver Chris Godwin to a hamstring injury. Tom Brady will likely be looking downfield without Godwin as a security blanket underneath. Under Bruce Arians, 37% of Brady’s passing yards are from deep completions (20+ air yards).
Tampa’s rookie RT Tristan Wirfs is conceding only a 5.9% pressure rate on his true pass sets so far this season (21st) against difficult competition (Cam Jordan, Bradley Chubb, etc). It won’t get any easier for Wirfs this week across from the NFL leader in pass-rushing efficiency, Joey Bosa.
Our model makes Tampa Bay a 7.8-point favorite, with a predicted total of 44.1 points, but the Buccaneers apply to a 46-129-2 ATS big home favorite letdown situation based on their 2-game win streak while underdogs that are coming off a straight-up loss as favorites of more than 6 points the previous week are profitable 176-109-10 ATS in the NFL since 1980. While the Chargers consistently find ways to lose close games when favored they are also 50-22-4 ATS as a road underdog going back to 2004, including 9-5-1 ATS under head coach Anthony Lynn. I’ll lean with the Chargers to bounce-back from that upset loss to Carolina by keeping this one close.

Lean – NY Giants (+13) over LA RAMS
New York’s offense has been dreadful under Jason Garrett so far, ranking 31st in our year-to-date numbers. The Giants are failing to gain any yardage on 32% of their rushing attempts, the highest rate in the NFL (league average is 17%), and quarterback Daniel Jones has faced a higher pressure rate than any other QB (42%). The Giants’ offense likely won’t get any better on Sunday. Aaron Donald had 5 pressures including 2 sacks last week and the All-Pro interior defender is the only player in the league with 20 pressures thus far. Jones is coming off his least accurate game as a pro (by completion percentage versus expectation) and he will need to get the ball out quickly in this matchup.
Rams top CB Jalen Ramsey has logged 20 coverage snaps on the outside in man and has yet to see a single target. However, Ramsey has not been traveling into the slot this season and the Rams allowed Bills’ slot-receiver Cole Beasley to gain 100 receiving yards last week. The Giants’ Golden Tate could have some opportunities on the inside in this game.
Giants new CB James Bradberry has 8 combined pass breakups and interceptions in 2020, three more than any other cornerback. However, New York’s secondary is likely to struggle to defend Cooper Kupp on the inside, as nickelback Darnay Holmes is surrendering 1.66 yards per cover snap in the slot (6th-worst).
Our model favors the Rams by only 10.1 points, with a predicted total of 47.4 points and I’ll lean with the Giants based on the value even though I’m uncomfortable doing so.

GREEN BAY (-7/-7.5) vs Atlanta
Calvin Ridley is making the most of Julio Jones’ nagging hamstring injury as the third-year WR is averaging 2.75 yards per route run (11th). However, Matt Ryan will be hoping Julio is back at full strength on Monday night as Ridley is likely to struggle against CB Jaire Alexander, who had a forced incompletion percentage of 20.4% last season, ranking 2nd among qualifiers. Last week, Alexander played 38 coverage snaps conceding just 2 targets, 1 reception, and -2 yards.
Linebacker Christian Kirksey has been a bad signing for Green Bay thus far, as he’s allowing 1.81 yards per cover snap (2nd-worst), but Kirksey will miss this game with a shoulder injury and backup Oren Burks might be an upgrade for Green Bay’s pass defense.
Defensive coordinator Mike Pettine has done a good job providing different looks for opposing quarterbacks as the Packers are one of the only teams in the league to not run any of their coverage concepts on more than a fourth of their calls.
Aaron Rodgers has been in rhythm so far in 2020, getting the ball out early (2.59 second average time to throw) while also looking to exploit teams deep with nearly 10 air yards per attempt. Aaron Jones may be limited catching passes out of the backfield by Deoin Jones, who ranks 3rd among linebackers with 0.50 yards allowed to cover snap. However, Rodgers should have time to look downfield with interior defender Grady Jarrett (2.5 sacks) neutralized by guards Elgton Jenkins and Lucas Patrick, who have combined to concede only 4 pressures in three games. Inside receiver Allen Lazard already has 3 receptions on targets of 20 yards or more and the Falcons placed starting nickelback Darqueze Dennard (hamstring) on the Injured Reserve list.
Our model makes Green Bay a 6.0-point favorite, with a predicted total of 55.9 points. The model leans a bit with Atlanta but the Packers apply to a 44-10-1 ATS Monday night home team momentum situation.
