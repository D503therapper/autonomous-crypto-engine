"The longer you're together, the more chemistry you get, the more you grow,'' Hartline said. "So, we're trying the best we can to make up for lost time. That's always the goal in your first year. But we're grownups. We've got plenty of guys (good players). We just need to clean up the mistakes and do a better job of executing in certain areas.''
Hartline said he and his staff are analyzing why outstanding practice performances haven't necessarily translated into similar efforts during the games. He's acutely aware that the initial drives of each half produced points against Temple, but the offense mostly struggled during the game's other junctures.
And despite the defense's overall standout efforts (19th nationally in scoring defense at 14.6 points per game, 39th nationally in total defense at 312.4 yards allowed per game), Hartline didn't like the way Temple was let off the hook after being pinned at its 4-yard line (it then produced a 10-play, 96-yard fourth-quarter touchdown drive to win the game).
Hartline said his biggest areas of concern are too many penalties at inopportune moments in recent games, overall offensive execution and the defense giving up a rare (but costly) big play because someone is caught out of position.
He also said he has lamented some playcalling at times.
"We have good plans, don't get me wrong,'' Hartline said. "But (there are) three, four, five plays that maybe I'm really mad at myself for allowing that to be called or (that it) made it to the call sheet. Whatever that is, it's on us (as coaches). But I would say for the most part, we've been pretty happy with where we're at (managing the game). … We need to get better at execution, consistency and drive-out (of the play).''
