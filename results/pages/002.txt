The one place Tulane is vulnerable is its run defense, which coughs up 214 rushing yards a game. Charlotte can’t take advantage of it. The 49ers run for 93.5 yards a game against FBS teams, 129th in the country, and their line ranks near the bottom in creating yards for its backs. Harrell has to win this through the air, and Tulane knows it.
The history doesn’t help either. Tulane won 34-3 in this building in 2024 and 27-0 last season.
Why I could be wrong
Tulane is 0-3 on the road, scoring 13.3 points a game away from New Orleans, and 0-3 against the spread as a favorite. It’s playing a fourth straight week on a short turnaround, with about three real practice days. The offensive line is held together with tape. Right tackle Ryan Mickow was carted off against UTSA and reportedly isn’t expected to play, guard Elijah Baker has been out all season, and Hall said the Wave have “ended up down four O-linemen” in a tough injury year. Maurice Turner, the Wave’s most explosive back at 8.5 yards a carry, has missed two of the last three games with a hamstring problem.
Charlotte has had 13 days to prepare, and a rested team with a quarterback who just played clean football is how a big favorite gets dragged into a backdoor cover. Harrell has started the last two games, but Charlotte hasn’t announced a starter for Friday yet.
How it plays out
Tulane’s defense, which has forced 10 turnovers of its own, smothers an offense that can’t run, and the Wave’s passing game finally breathes against a secondary that rarely touches the ball. Tulane pulls away after halftime, and a team with its conference hopes on life support handles its business.
