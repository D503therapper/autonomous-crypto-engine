North Texas QB Tayven Jackson out for regular season; Chris Jimerson Jr. takes over
Jackson is out for the next two games with an “upper body” injury. Jimerson will make first career start vs. Tulane.
by
Steve Helwick
Nov 18, 2026, 4:30 AM UTC
North Texas lost every starter from the winningest team in program history. Yet, the Mean Green immediately reestablished themselves as one of the American Conference’s top contenders.
They entered Saturday night riding a six-game winning streak and stood as the last remaining undefeated team in the conference standings. But when North Texas fell 35-16 to UTSA in San Antonio, the Mean Green lost more than their unblemished conference record.
Starting quarterback Tayven Jackson exited the contest in the first minute of the second half after converting a third-and-long with an 18-yard scramble. Jackson took a hard fall on his right shoulder and did not return. He was later spotted wearing a sling on his right arm, with his shoulder wrapped.
Head coach Neal Brown confirmed his starting quarterback will miss the final two games of the regular season, although he didn’t rule out a return for a potential conference championship or bowl game.
“He’ll miss both of these games, and then we’ll know after that,” Brown said. “He’ll definitely miss Saturday and then the UAB game as well. I’m not ready to say he’s out for the season yet. I think after that will be to be determined.”
Jackson currently ranks sixth in the FBS with 3,005 passing yards and ninth with 23 passing touchdowns. The fifth-year senior, who previously started games for Indiana and UCF, captained an offense that ranked first in the country in yards per game prior to Saturday night’s loss at UTSA.

Brown didn’t specify if the injury was to the shoulder or collarbone, or if another part of the upper body was affected.
“It’s upper body,” Brown said. “I don’t believe it will inhibit his training for the NFL. I’m not ready to rule him out for the season. He will miss the final two regular season games. We will know more in the next week or so.”
North Texas was already dealing with a depleted quarterback room. Second-string quarterback Chaston Ditta, who transferred from East Carolina, suffered an injury in October and remains sidelined, although Brown did not rule him out for the season. That left sophomore Chris Jimerson Jr. as the next man up following Jackson’s exit against UTSA.
Upon checking in, Jimerson completed the touchdown drive that Jackson started. Then, he led a second scoring drive, cutting North Texas’ 21-3 deficit into 21-16. Trailing by five, the backup quarterback possessed the ball in UTSA territory with under five minutes remaining. However, the Roadrunner defense thwarted the Mean Green’s comeback effort with a critical fourth down sack, sealing North Texas’ first loss since Sept. 19.
“With limited reps, I thought he gave us a chance,” Brown said. “He came in, threw the ball downfield well. I don’t think we had any issues from an operations standpoint. He’s got a good vertical — he’s not a side-to-side runner, he’s a vertical runner. But he gave us a chance to win the game. I think what you want out of your game, you want an opportunity to win games. The moment wasn’t too big for him nor will Saturday be too big for him. The kid’s played in huge high school games. He won a 6A state championship. He’s a winner, and he did that on Saturday night with limited reps.”
In Jimerson’s most extensive action to date, he completed 7-of-12 passes for 70 yards and a touchdown. North Texas also ran some option-oriented packages with him, and he managed a team-high 33 rushing yards and a touchdown in the defeat. Brown had to make adjustments to the offense on the fly against the Roadrunners, relying on concepts the sophomore had practiced most frequently. Now, with a full week of preparation ahead of his first start, the coaching staff can build a more tailored game plan.
“He didn’t play a lot last year,” Brown said. “He got some good reps in spring, lot of good reps in fall camp. He was the backup for the last month, so his reps increased. The more reps he’s gotten, his decision making and confidence have grown. I thought on Saturday, when Tayven got hurt and I had a chance to collect my thoughts, my immediate thoughts on that game were, ‘What do we have a bunch of reps in? What did we rep this week in practice? What have we logged reps in where we can go out and he can operate?’ Now, all our gameplanning on Sunday and Monday was with him in mind. So now, he’s got all these reps on Monday through Friday, and the gameplan is specifically for him.”
As early as Sunday, Brown already started devising his first gameplan with Jimerson. The first-year North Texas head coach had the quarterback write down his favorite plays, explain why he preferred those plays, and then the two determined evaluated how those concepts would stack up against the Tulane defense, which they face next Saturday.
