Oct. 1—PULLMAN — Sometime on Sunday afternoon, Jirah Leaupepetele pulled out his phone and dialed his brother Jonah. In another week, sometimes the brothers would just be chatting. Or they'd be talking trash to each other, a theme as they grew up together, especially as Jirah has flourished on Washington State's defensive line and Jonah has landed on Fresno State's offensive line.
This week, though, Jirah wasn't calling just to razz his younger brother. He was trying to squeeze a gameplan clue or two out of him.

"But he wasn't really trying to tell me anything," Jirah laughed.
When WSU and Fresno State kick off on Saturday evening, it won't just mark the beginning of Pac-12 play for the Cougars and Bulldogs. It will also pit Jirah and Jonah against each other for the first time , a seminal moment in their lives, a full-circle moment for their parents and the ones who know them best.
The dynamics are interesting. Jirah has started three of the Cougars' first four games of the season, becoming a cornerstone on the interior of his team's defensive line. That would set him up to line up right across from Jonah, who started the Bulldogs' season opener at right guard — except there's one problem. Jonah, listed as questionable on the first Pac-12 availability report, isn't expected to be able to play.
After playing 15 snaps in Fresno State's season-opening loss to USC last month, Jonah went down with an injury. He wasn't cleared to play in time for Saturday's game in Pullman, according to his father, Tony, who called the experience exciting for the family since Jonah will still be at the game. Parents Tony and Rhonda will still be in attendance with their other five children, all the way from Apple Valley, California, which sits about 100 miles outside Los Angeles.

Only those aren't the only Leaupepeteles who will be in attendance at Gesa Field on Saturday. The list also includes an assortment of grandparents and nephews and nieces, Tony said. "They better be wearing 98 in crimson and gray," Jirah said with a smile. Zoom out and you start to understand what this means to the Leaupepeteles.
"It is surreal. It hasn't really hit us," Tony said. "It's a proud moment for me and my wife, and our kids that look up to their siblings, their older siblings. ... When this schedule came out, we were like, 'Oh, we have to come to this game. This is a game we must come to.' "
"I'm just very proud to see them live out their dreams and goals right now," Rhonda added. "It's exciting to hear all the smack talking. We tell them like, 'Hey, you guys are brothers still.' But it's all fun and games."
The Leaupepetele's have certainly earned the opportunity, even if Jonah can't play in Saturday's game. After spending the first three years of his career at FCS Eastern Washington, where he totaled 24 tackles (five solo) and one for loss, Jirah transferred to WSU, where he's done nothing short of prosper. In WSU's loss to Arizona last weekend, Jirah tallied his first sack as a Cougar, giving him eight tackles on the year.

He's also earned three quarterback pressures this fall, according to Pro Football Focus, underscoring the space that he takes up with his 6-foot-3, 349-pound frame. Jirah has become a key cog in defensive coordinator Trent Bray's unit, registering 133 snaps in his first four games at WSU.
For his part, Jonah redshirted last year, his true freshman season at Fresno State. In short order, this season he earned the Bulldogs' starting right guard job, jogging out for Fresno State's first snaps of the season in Week 1.
He may not be able to line up against his older brother on Saturday evening, but the moment still means something to the family, to the brothers who shared a bedroom growing up.
"That's my best friend right there," Jirah said. "I love him to death, but we're still on that field. He's across the line. He's wearing a different color of jersey. That's the type of timing this is."

Turns out, Jirah and Jonah were opposites as they grew up, their parents said. They may have shared a room growing up, but that was about as similar as they got in those days. Jirah is more strong-willed, his dad said, remembering times when Jirah made a decision and pursued it full-strength. Jonah is more reserved, Rhonda said, taking time to weigh all his options when mulling a decision.
Jirah is more into video games, while Jonah likes board games and puzzles. Back home in Apple Valley, there would be plenty of times when Jirah would stay up late playing video games, hooting and hollering at the screen. Then would come Jonah's voice: "Bro, I'm trying to sleep."
They aren't huge trash-talkers in general, their parents say, only to each other. They had lots of opportunities over the years, sharing the field in different capacities, from youth football all the way to the high school state championships. Jirah's favorite memory of playing with Jonah, he said, was when they helped take Apple Valley to the CIF in Jonah's freshman year, Jirah's junior season.
"That was kinda really his first year taking football seriously, and that's when he decided it was something that he really wanted to do," Jirah said. "Just seeing him grow into the player that he is, and the person that he has become now is just really great to see."
