Air Force Falcons
Air Force enters this game with a 6-2 record, having beaten Navy in the first leg of the CIC trophy 14-9. The Falcons won that game primarily because of two touchdown drives in the second quarter. Their other 7 full drives included 4 punts, 2 interceptions, and a turnover on downs. In those drives, they gained just 130 yards and only entered Navy’s side of the field twice – and never reached the red zone.
But not a lot will translate from that game. For starters, Air Force has a different quarterback. Additionally, Navy’s rush defense grades out much better than Army’s. Lastly, Navy threw 18 passes in that game – a mark that Army hasn’t hit in a CIC matchup since 2010.
Offense
Junior QB Liam Szarka was in his second season in the starting role before suffering a season-ending injury against Navy. Sophomore Evan Medders was the immediate backup for the Falcons, but left the Wyoming game two weeks ago with an injury and did not play against UConn. If Medders can’t suit up Saturday, it’s probably senior Kemper Hodges who will take snaps to start. He’s appeared as a FB this year, but has been listed as backup QB for two years. He’s listed at 6’0” and 245 pounds. If it’s Hodges that starts, we shouldn’t see much in the passing game – a sight for sore eyes when it comes to Army’s defense. If Air Force ends up needing a passing game, they’re likely to look to freshman Howard Fisher IV, who’s thrown 9 times over the last two games

Despite the carousel at QB, the Falcons continue to use the QB for about half of their rushes. Fullbacks take about 30%, while slotbacks take the last 20%. This hasn’t changed much in the weeks after Szarka went down. For comparison, Army’s QB/FB/SB rush distribution is 44%/36%/20%.
The Falcons’ FB room is again dominated by Owen Allen – the only player with triple digit carries so far. His production is down from 5.7 to 4.2 this year, though. And he averaged just 2 yards per carry in the Navy game. This is primarily indicative of an offensive line issue. The Falcons returned just one starter this year, Alec Falk, who missed last week’s game with an undisclosed injury. It’s unclear whether Falk will be available this week.

Defense
Usually, this section would start with a look at Air Force’s defense in the Navy game. But since Navy threw the ball three times as much as their FB ran the ball, it won’t offer much in comparison this year.
Senior LB Blake Fletcher leads the team in tackles with 79. He’s joined by fellow LB Dallas Daley with 49. Air Force is 103rd in yards per carry allowed and 134th in TFLs. How can a team with such bad metrics be 6-2, you ask? Well, the Falcons have a top 20 passing defense and a top 40 red zone defense.
The only notable injury for the Falcons’ rush defense is NG Chaz Barnett. Barnett began the year as a starter before getting injured against NDSU. He isn’t listed as out for the season, but has not seen action since.
Special Teams
Air Force K Carter Cremascoli – Italian for “creampipe” in case any future squadronmates need a callsign suggestion – has missed one PAT and one FG this season. The PAT was blocked and the FG was a 28 yarder from the left hash which missed to the right. His long is 52 yards.
Junior P Will Duncan – whose name does not translate from Italian – is probably the best service academy punter this year, despite being the only one that hasn’t been nominated for a weekly Ray’s Eight award. He averages 48 yards per punt with 9 over 50 and just 2 touchbacks.
