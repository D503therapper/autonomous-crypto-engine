The Nicholls Colonels will host rival Southeastern on Thursday at 6 p.m. for the 37th annual River Bell Classic. The Colonels look to make a statement to close out their season and capture the elusive trophy that they have not held since 2023.
In addition to the rivalry, the game will be Nicholls’ senior night, the final opportunity to honor the seniors who have helped lead the program throughout their careers. This matchup will carry a little extra weight for the Nicholls football program.
The Colonels come into the matchup at 4-7 overall and 3-5 in the Southland Conference/ The Lions’ record is not much better at 5-6 overall and 3-5 in the conference. A win would give the Colonels the opportunity to finish ahead of their rivals in the standings and make a strong statement to close out the year.
The River Bell Classic has become one of the most recognizable rivalries in the Southland Conference, with the schools competing for the trophy since 1972. The Lions hold the slight edge in the series with a record of 19-17, with wins in the last two contests.
The game gives the Colonels the opportunity to send their seniors off on a high note, build momentum leading into the off-season and bring the coveted River Bell trophy back to Thibodaux.

For many students, finals arrive during the first full week of December, following a week of reading and review. Unlike midterms, which offer a chance to regroup and course-correct, finals carry a distinct finality.
“With midterms, students still have time to course correct if they perform poorly. With finals, there is really no time to make adjustments,” said Adrienne Naquin Bolton, executive director of Student Wellness and Support.
That pressure can feel overwhelming, but students don’t have to navigate it alone. Whether you need to master a complex math theorem, polish a final research paper, or simply take a deep breath away from the books, campus has a wealth of resources ready to help you cross the finish line.
Before you sit down to tackle your exams, taking stock of your standing in each class and mapping out your remaining assignments is the best first step. If you need a little backup, Elkins Hall serves as your main campus hub for academic support, housing multiple resources under one roof.
If you are looking to master tricky course concepts, the Tutoring Center is located in room 227 of Elkins Hall. Available to both online and in-person students, it offers flexible 45-minute sessions with certified tutors specializing in math, science and business courses.
Tutoring comes with a $25 charge to your Banner account each semester, though scholarships and payment resources are available if you need assistance. Appointments with tutors can be scheduled through the Navigate app.
Right across the hall in room 212, the Testing Center provides a quiet environment specifically for students taking online courses. Exams can be scheduled Monday through Friday from 8:30 a.m. to 4:30 p.m. entirely free of charge, and you can secure your spot by making an appointment through Canvas using the Register Blast link.
Academic support goes beyond Elkins Hall. If writing is your main hurdle as deadlines approach, the English department’s Writing Center is ready to help you refine your drafts.
Additionally, don’t underestimate the power of faculty office hours, either. Office hours can provide dedicated, one-on-one time with your professor to clarify expectations and figure out exactly where you stand before finals week begins.
Managing academic stress is just as important as studying the material. To help students decompress, the campus is rolling out mental health and relaxation initiatives ahead of finals week.
The bi-annual Stress Less Fest is happening on Dec. 2 from 10:30 a.m. to 1:30 p.m. across campus to help students unwind before exams begin.
The event is designed to give students’ brains a well-deserved break through hands-on, creative activities like adult coloring, journaling, rock painting, expressive painting and DIY self-care body scrubs, alongside providing connections to on-campus and community resources.
If you need a quiet retreat outside of the festival, Bolton highlights the Serenity Space, located within the University Counseling Center. It serves as a sanctuary designed to quiet your mind when the overwhelm of finals may start to set in.

The NCAA adopted its new 5-for-5 rule in June of 2026. The rule states that Division I athletes now have five years to play five seasons: eliminating red-shirts (medically and otherwise), causing a world of lawsuits and controversy.
A major grievance with the rule came with the notable exclusion of the Class of 2022. Lawsuits came in bunches, athletes argued that the class before them got an extra year for covid and the class after and all subsequent classes have five years automatically.
The NCAA won the suit upon appeal which meant they did not have to allow the Class of 2022 athletes another year. However, there are still many state cases going on with people filing different grievances, with some states even allowing players from the 2022 class to return to college.
The true danger of the rule came to a head when LSU’s coach, Lane Kiffin, recruited former professional players to their roster. While it was technically legal during the Class of 2022 eligibility wave, the SEC passed a rule that banned former professional athletes from playing and threatened to kick LSU out of the conference.
I think that there needed to be a change in the way NCAA eligibility was structured and regardless of the change, some would have disagreed. The 5-for-5 is a good rule on its face but was always going to come with some scrutiny.
With this new rule, the days of “eighth year seniors” and having multiple red-shirts are over. I think that in conjunction with the NCAA’s transfer portal and NIL rules this will be beneficial for the college athletes, especially incoming freshmen whose opportunity would have been taken by a much older player before.
Additionally, it encourages athletes to go somewhere that they will be able to play early on because they can’t just red-shirt their freshman year. Along with that, players have an exact timeline of when they will be out of eligibility and there aren’t any loop holes (once all the current court cases are over and there are only athletes who would have been in high school at the time of the ruling.)
It lays out a consistent foundation for all college sports and does not allow athletes to abuse the new NIL policies and spend an egregious amount of time in college.
My major concern is for those players who do suffer injuries that keep them sidelined for a full season. It is hard on players to not get that year back but with the transfer portal of today, if a team does not trust a player after their injury they can transfer to a “worse school” and still be a day one starter.
I believe the implications that it has are going to be major for the NCAA and college sports moving forward. I think that most of those implications will be good and overall it will benefit athletes and the programs with the new consistency in eligibility.

Construction on Nicholls’ Starbucks on the corner of Audubon and Afton drives will conclude in December, with doors set to open in January for the Spring 2027 semester.
The $2.9 million project was fully funded by university foodservice provider Sodexo, who approached Nicholls.
Director of Facility Services and Project Management Danielle Breaux explained the origins of the project.
“We are constantly looking for ways to bring services to our students that they would enjoy and appreciate and love. Sodexo had reached out and our campus administration had reached out to our students to see if they would be interested in having a Starbucks on campus, and, of course, everyone said yes,” Breaux said.
“They [Sodexo] are a great support of the university, of course, and great partners. They want to do everything that they can to help out, as well as to bring students the services that they want.”
The new Starbucks will offer both a full service menu, as well as Nicholls’ first drive-thru dining option. Other amenities such as indoor seating and an outdoor patio will be available to both Nicholls students and the community as a whole.
Despite being an on campus dining option, the Starbucks will function like any other location when it comes to accepting coupons, according to Executive Director of Auxiliary Services Margo Badeaux.
“It will honor all the coupons [and] anything that any other Starbucks honors,” Badeaux said.
Badeaux said the Starbucks location should not cause any major traffic issues, which is one of the factors that allowed for a drive-through feature.
“Because of the location, it’s not just as much of a traffic problem as if we tried to have a drive-thru in Chick-fil-A. In Chick-fil-A, the program that they presented to us did not allow for a drive-thru. Starbucks, right off the bat, said that we would have a drive-thru. They wanted to be a full service location,” Badeaux said.
“I think it’s beneficial for commuter students, of course, and for the community. On the way to work or on the way to classes, just a quick way to swing through and get what you want.”
Some student employment opportunities will be available through Sodexo. Interested students can apply for positions at Sodexo’s office in the Galliano Dining Hall, or through Indeed.com.
Though nothing has been officially confirmed, university officials are discussing removing or relocating Ellender Memorial Library’s The Brew following the completion of Starbucks.
“My understanding is that we will likely only have one presence, and that’ll be Starbucks. But nothing has been officially decided upon just yet. So as of right now, [The Brew] will remain open,” Breaux said.
