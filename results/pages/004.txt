Gamecocks Passing Attack Holds the Edge
Dual-threat quarterback Caden Creel is the engine of the Jacksonville State offense. Creel has thrown for 1,637 yards with 12 touchdowns and six interceptions while adding 500 rushing yards and four scores on the ground. His versatility makes him the most dangerous player on the field in this matchup.
Wide receiver Deondre Johnson leads the Gamecocks’ receiving corps with 431 yards and three touchdowns, and he draws a favorable matchup against a New Mexico State defense allowing 224.1 passing yards per game.
That’s where Jacksonville State should find its biggest advantage. The ground game is a different story, as the Aggies have been stout against the run, allowing just 109.1 rushing yards per contest. That strength could slow Creel’s scrambling and limit the Gamecocks’ rushing attack, especially with two running backs listed as questionable.
NMSU Ground Game Behind James Jones
New Mexico State’s offense runs through running back James Jones, who has piled up 777 rushing yards and four touchdowns this season. Jones is coming off a big performance against Sam Houston, rushing for 178 yards and a touchdown in a 30-24 win. Quarterback Adam Damante complemented that ground attack with 160 passing yards and two touchdowns in the victory.
If the Aggies can establish Jones early, they’ll have a chance to keep this game competitive while also shortening it. A run-heavy approach keeps the clock moving and limits possessions for both sides.
Jacksonville State has the more productive passing attack and has shown greater offensive consistency throughout the season, but with the Aggies’ run defense taking away part of the Gamecocks’ identity and injuries thinning out skill positions on both rosters, this sets up as a grinding, methodical game.
