Syracuse Heads Into Ranked SMU Test With Growing Injury Concern
Syracuse faces lineup challenges against No. 19 SMU, with key players sidelined by injuries shaking up both the offense and defense.
Dan Waterstone
·
IMAGE: Syracuse Orange quarterback Steve Angeli (9) prepares to throw during the first half against North Carolina Tar Heels at Kenan Stadium. / Cory Knowlton / Imagn Images
Syracuse will have to move forward without one of its starting defensive backs when it faces No. 19 Southern Methodist on Saturday.
According to the ACC’s availability report, the Orange have ruled out Jai Eugene Jr. Eugene has been working at both nickel and safety, and he started Syracuse’s last three games.
With him out, B.J. Long is expected to slide back into the starting lineup alongside Chris Peal.
Eugene’s season has been rough. Pro Football Focus lists him as Syracuse’s second-worst defender among 39 players on the roster, with an overall defensive grade of 48.9. His run defense grade sits at 36.3, and his tackling grade is 27.4.
Syracuse also has some uncertainty on offense heading into the matchup. Quarterback Steve Angeli and center Kahlil Stewart are both listed as questionable.
Angeli was back under center last week in Syracuse’s overtime win at North Carolina after missing three games. Stewart made his first start at center in that game, which came as part of a reshuffled offensive line that moved Joe Cruz to left guard and sent UCLA transfer Oluwafunto Akinshilo to the bench.
That change left Syracuse with both of its Week 1 starting guards benched.
Behind Stewart, the Orange have listed Matthew Hawn as doubtful. The CBA grad began the season behind Cruz and Stewart at center, but he has picked up more work at guard in recent weeks.
SMU also has a notable injury situation of its own. Kicker Nick Reed is questionable. Reed has been sharing snaps with Brendan Baird this season, and he has hit all 14 of his extra points while going 2-for-3 on field goals.

Syracuse Just Added A Center Who Could Change Its Identity Inside
Syracuse bolstered its frontcourt with the addition of Luke Wilson, a 6-foot-9 transfer from Appalachian State who went through a crowded portal market before settling on the Orange. Wilson had multiple Power Five options on the table, but Syracuses coaching staff proved to be the draw, especially the chance to develop under big man coach Arinze Onuaku.
For a roster looking to define its interior identity, Wilson arrives with a skill set that stands out immediately. He is expected to compete for the starting center job, and his defensive profile is already established after leading the Sun Belt in blocks last season. If that edge carries over, Syracuse may have found a player who changes the way opponents attack the paint. [
