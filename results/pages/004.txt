Miami outlook
The RedHawks eeked out another victory in a hostile environment when they were last in action, managing to stifle a Central Michigan rally in the second half to seal a 28-27 victory over the Chippewas.
Several aspects of their game struggled in Mt. Pleasant, but they found the plays when they needed to, finishing 50 percent of their third/fourth-down conversions and leading a game-winning offensive drive down six with under four minutes remaining.
The RedHawks have had a penchant for persevering through adversity; they've had one-score results decided in the last moments against Cincinnati (loss), UConn (win), Bowling Green (loss), UMass (win)
and
Central Michigan (win) in the 2026 campaign.
QB Caleb Heavner (18-of-33, 221 yards, one TD, two interceptions) posted a gritty game vs. Central Michigan last time out, playing through multiple injuries to led the RedHawks to victory. The Miami passing offense has had to carry the attack in 2026, a departure from the typical look under head coach Chuck Martin; the RedHawks are second-best in the MAC with 217 passing yards per game on average (compared to eighth in rushing at 141.3 yards per game.)
Maleek Huggins had his second 100+ yard performance of 2026 vs. Central, and leads the team with 24 receptions for 431 yards and a touchdown. Damarion Witten (17 rec., 346 yards) has been the consistent scoring threat, leading Miami with four touchdown catches. They also want to get gadget player Snoop Smith (five-foot-five, 150 lbs.) involved on horizontal routes, whether on jet sweeps or on crossers; he sits on 18 catches for 189 yards and a receiving score.
The rushing attack, outside of games against Holy Cross and Akron, has been largely pedestrian when facing even average units. They face a tough loss along the line, as starting guard Greg Smith Jr. suffered a leg injury last week trying to block for a run. The RedHawks like to utilize a two-back rotation, with Rodney Nelson (78 carries, 391 yards, three TDs) and D'Shawntae Jones (55 carries, 173 yards, three TDs) carrying part of the load. Heavner also contributes positively here, with 217 yards and two TDs.
Speaking of tough losses, leading tackler Malcolm McCain might also be out ahead of this game. The upperclassman MIKE backer has been crucial to the defense with 59 stops (13 more than second-place Blayne Myrick), and was directly responsible for the win over UMass with a crucial two-point conversion stop. It'll likely be up to Myrick (46 tackles, four tackles-for-loss, INT) to step up into the role. Roosevelt Andrews III and grant Lyons have combined for nine TFLs and seven sacks on the defensive line, while Amariyun Knighten leads the backfield with 10 pass break-ups.
Special teams haven't been a guaranteed for Miami, but they've employed their placekicker and punter well after a shaky start to the season. Kellan McLaughlin is 10-of-14 placekicking with a long of 47 yards, while Pierse Stainton averages 44 yards per boot. Scoop Smith has a return touchdown this season and has been one of the MAC's best specialists through eight games, averaging 30 yards a run.
Buffalo outlook
Much like Miami, Buffalo also needed to pull some late heroics to take home a hard-fought victory last time out, taking down Bowling Green 31-24.
Both UB and BGSU were 2-1 entering the contest, making it a very important game for the MAC standings. The Bulls were able to find the advantage in the fourth quarter after a back-and-forth contest, shuttering the Falcons with two three-and-outs and an interception while the offense did enough to build a 10-point lead to claim enough space for a win.
Key to the effort was senior halfback Messiah Burch, who exploded for a career-best 184 rushing yards and three touchdowns, securing his fifth-straight game of 100+ rushing yards to continue his all-MAC caliber campaign.
Since the start of conference play, the Bulls offense has settled into a fierce, run-driven outfit, averaging 195.8 rushing yards per conference game (second in the MAC) and averaging 4.4 yards per rush. They've also been top five in league play in first downs, third-down conversions and fourth-down conversions despite being near the bottom in time of possession (12th in MAC), showing decent efficiency.
