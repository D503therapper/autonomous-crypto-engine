His Beavers started the year with a pair of ranked losses to Houston (33-20) and Texas Tech (35-24) before winning five straight games over Montana, UTEP, Colorado State, San Diego State and Washington State.
“I’ll tell you what, we kept receipts…people would say we would only go 1-11 this year, and you know what? That just keeps putting fuel to the fire,” Shephard told USA Sports.
Through seven games, Oregon State ranks among the top five teams in the country (out of 138 FBS schools) in several offensive categories: 42.7 PPG (4th), 384.9 passing YPG (1st), 26 passing TD (1st), 527 total YPG (3rd), 40 total TD (3rd) and 7.35 yards per play (5th).
It will be a big test for Fresno State and Defensive Coordinator Nick Benedetto.
“Their O-line is very physical. They get off the ball really well, and the quarterback does a great job diagnosing plays. I think that’s what stands out to me on film, is [Atkinson] is able to go with the ball very quickly, and you know you can see the receivers and the running back can hit it downhill too as well,” Benedetto said.
Braden Atkinson (Reef Diego/Oregon State Athletics).
While being known for the passing attack, the rushing attack is a weapon as well and has actually outperformed Fresno State this season (142.1 to 139 YPG).
Defensively, Oregon State plays heavy upfront and despite not blitzing a lot, has 18 sacks as a team this season (t-28th in FBS). They’ve forced nine turnovers this season as a unit (six interceptions, three fumble recoveries).
“He’s very competitive. You can tell he loves the game of football, and they are gonna find a way to get him the ball,” Benedetto said. “When [Jesse Legree] gets the ball in his hands, that’s when he becomes dynamic. Very good route runner. He can take the top off the defense.”
Legree, who announced during the bye he was signing an
NIL-contract extension
with the Beavers, struggled to generate college interest out of high school, before ultimately being identified as a player of interest by Oregon State General Manager Eron Hodges this last winter.
Legree joined the Beavers without a scholarship, but was rewarded with a big pay day and a huge investment from the only school that believed in him.
“You could never make up for people not wanting you. It’ll never be made up,” Shephard said. “All those schools across the country, nobody wanted him. And we did.”
Fresno State starting cornerback Kiontre Harris is excited about the opportunity to face off against the best of the best.
It’s quite a stark contrast to Fresno State which has gotten little production (18 receptions, 185 yards, TD) from its tight end room on the receiving side of things.
Takari Hickle
— The strength of the Beavers defense lies in the middle of the defense, namely senior edge rusher Takari Hickle (37 tackles, FF), who’s already up to six sacks this season (10 in 3 seasons).
“When he goes out there, it doesn’t matter what’s going on in the game, in his life, in the lives of others on the team or around the country. None of that matters. He is out there to try to wreak havoc on the individuals around him,” Shephard said.
Takari Hickle (Karl Maasdam/Oregon State Athletics).
The linebacker core including Hickle, Aiden Sullivan, Gyriece Goodman, Dylan Lane, Kord Shaw and Jabari Odoemenem are amongst the best in the Pac-12 Conference.
“They do some creative things in their blitz packages on third down that really challenge teams to be good. They’ll load it up and bring everybody. They’ll load it up and drop out and do a drop-eight coverage,” Fresno State Offensive Coordinator Josh Davis said. “They haven’t made many assignment errors on defense this year. We’ll have to be good.”
Dog-Factors
Kiontre Harris
– Fresno’s State’s ability to stay in the game on Saturday will (more than likely) come down to two factors: winning the turnover battle and limiting Legree.
Tackling
: Via Gabe Camarillo of KMPH Fox 26, the Bulldogs missed a combined 32 tackles in their recent pair of losses – more than they missed in the previous four weeks combined (29) per PFF.
“The fundamentals of tackling is always a thing we have to keep improving on, keep getting better at,” Benedetto said. “We always hammer on our hand placement, our eye placement, and then just our communication level.”
Injury Report
