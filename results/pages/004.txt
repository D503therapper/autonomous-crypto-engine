Penn State vs. Purdue Preview
Injuries, Preview & Prediction
This will be the 22nd all-time meeting between Penn State and Purdue. This series dates back to 1951, but 19 of the 21 previous matchups occurred after Penn State joined the Big Ten in 1990. The Nittany Lions have won the last 11 games in this series by an average margin of 19 points. Purdue’s last win against Penn State was in 2004.
Top Two WRs Listed as Questionable
Chase Sowell
and
Koby Howard
are each listed as questionable on the first iteration of the Big Ten Availability Report this week. Howard has
missed the last four games
, and Sowell has been battling injuries throughout the season.

Should Howard return, it could bring a much-needed boost to Penn State’s floundering offense. Howard was a
top receiver in the nation,
statistically, before his injury, but Sowell has stepped up in his absence.
Howard remains the Nittany Lions’ second-leading receiver with 12 catches for 344 yards and two touchdowns, even after missing time, but Sowell now leads the team with 34 catches for 510 yards and three touchdowns. Sowell had the game of his career against Michigan, catching 11 passes for 151 yards and a touchdown.
Depth defensive end Alex McPherson is also listed as questionable. The Colorado transfer has 12 tackles this season.
Safety Jeremiah Cooper, defensive end Yvan Kemajou and defensive tackle Armstrong Nnodim are listed as probable. Depth defensive tackle Alijah Carnell is listed as doubtful.
Good news for Penn State: LaVar Arrington II is no longer listed on the availability report. Arrington underwent cleanup knee surgery at the
end of fall camp
and has not been available all season.
The difference on Purdue’s side of the availability report is stark. The Boilermakers have only four players on their availability report, while Penn State has 19. Starting wideout De’Nylon Morrissette and starting safety Jaden Mangham are listed as questionable.
Scouting the Boilermakers
Purdue is similar to Penn State statistically. Both teams have struggled to run the ball, have similar scoring offenses, have the same turnover margin and don’t make many tackles in the backfield.
Penn State averages 130.7 rushing yards, while Purdue averages 128.4 yards; Penn State averages 28.7 points per game, while Purdue averages 31.3; Penn State and Purdue have a turnover margin of +4; and Penn State has nine sacks and 32 tackles for loss this season, while Purdue has eight sacks and 33 tackles for loss.
The biggest differences are Penn State’s better scoring and pass defense, and Purdue’s better passing and red zone offense.
Some key players for the Boilermakers are quarterback Ryan Browne, who has 1,822 yards, 10 touchdowns and three picks this season; leading receiver Asaad Waseem, who has 38 catches for 588 yards but only one touchdown; running back Jame Ijeboi, who has 130 carries for 568 yards and nine touchdowns; and linebacker Jojo Hayden, who leads Purdue with 48 tackles and two interceptions.
Purdue’s scoring defense is a concern, averaging 32.3 points per game, which is more points than its offense averages. Penn State’s scoring defense has been a strength, allowing an average of 18.9 points per game.
The scoring output of Penn State’s offense likely hinges on Howard’s availability. He probably adds another touchdown to Penn State’s total, assuming he’s healthy enough to play.
Prediction
Penn State 27, Purdue 17
