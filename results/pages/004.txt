The Alamodome — where UTSA plays its home games — has become a formidable environment for opposing teams to walk into, with the Roadrunners holding a 35-5 record at home under head coach Jeff Traylor since he took over the team on Dec. 9, 2019.
But despite that success, Hartline said he questions the legitimacy of the Roadrunners’ homefield advantage.
“I’ve heard that it’s really loud there. I’m trying to figure out what the population size is,” Hartline said. “I don’t know how it gets so loud if there’s not a lot of people there.”
Through its first two games, UTSA has averaged roughly 25,000 fans per home game, according to the
San Antonio Express News

Hartline would not be the first coach to question the legitimacy of UTSA’s crowd.
In 2025, then-Tulane head coach Jon Sumrall said he believes UTSA’s crowd noise may not be completely authentic.
“I think they may pipe some [noise] in through the speakers,” Sumrall said during a media availability prior to the game. “I don’t think it’s legal. I don’t think anybody’s investigating it.”
