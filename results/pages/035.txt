Lean – NY JETS (-6) over New England
New England’s pass rush has 6 sacks (10th) despite trading edge defender Matt Judon and sidelining interior defender Christian Barmore (49 pressures in 2023, 14th) due to blood clots. However, the sacks are a mirage as the Patriots have just a 28.9% pressure rate (26th). New England’s defense will eventually feel the loss of Judon and Barmore.
New England edge defender Keion White ranks 7th in pass rushing efficiency but he will be shut down in this game as Jets tackles Tyron Smith and Morgan Moses have not allowed a sack this season.
New York offensive coordinator Nathaniel Hackett’s offense is not well-schemed to give answers to the quarterback. Aaron Rodgers is averaging only 4.7 yards per attempt versus the blitz. The Patriots defense a 39.8% blitz rate (6th-highest) and could give Rodgers trouble.
New England cornerback Jonathan Jones is conceding 0.41 yards per cover snap (17th) and he will contain WR Garrett Wilson, who has a 60% success rate (12th).
Rodgers is targeting running backs on a league-high 32.8% of passes and he’s better off looking for RB Breece Hall against the blitz especially since Patriots starting linebacker Ja’Whaun Bentley is for the year after he tore his pec.
The Jets are also going to be without a starting linebacker after CJ Mosley was carted to the locker room due to a foot injury last week. Jacoby Brissett will attack this loss by featuring tight end Hunter Henry, who is averaging 2.44 yards per route run (4th).
New York’s defense will also be missing edge rusher Jermaine Johnson, who had 7 pressures this year (20th) before tearing his Achilles. The Jets were already shallow at edge defender because Haason Reddick has not yet reported. New York’s defense was counting on Reddick’s 66 pressures he got in 2023 (15th).
New England rookie RG Layden Robinson ranks 2nd-worst in pass blocking efficiency, and he will struggle across from Javon Kinlaw, whose 5 pressures ranks 18th.
Patriots rookie head coach Jerod Mayo hasn’t set the cadence in a short week yet. First year head coaches tend to be at a disadvantage preparing for their first Thursday game.
Our model favors the Jets by 6.1 points with a predicted total of 36.9 points, and teams coming off an overtime game (the Pats) are just 4-22 ATS on Thursdays (against a team not coming off an OT game the previous week). That’s probably worth a point.

Lean – Over (36) – PITTSBURGH (-1.5) vs LA Chargers
Mike Tomlin is historically conservative on the road and the Steelers are 2-0 while scoring only one touchdown and they lead the NFL with 38.5 rushes per game.
However, Pittsburgh’s ground game will be contained coming back home on Sunday as the Chargers are allowing a league-low -0.44 EPA/rush.
Justin Fields is one of three quarterbacks without a turnover-worthy throw, and he has retained the starting job. Although, I’m not sure how long his tenure will last. Fields had 41 turnovers in the last three seasons (7th-most).
Steelers WR George Pickens 0.78 EPA/target (7th) and he will battle across from CB Kristian Fulton, who is allowing a league-low 0.02 yards per cover snap.
The Los Angeles offensive line is responsible for their 2-0 start as 59% of the yardage gained by the Chargers through two weeks has been on the ground.
The Chargers rank 21st in adjusted pace by our numbers but the offense could speed up when they aren’t afforded free rushing lanes. Pittsburgh’s defense is allowing -0.29 EPA/rush (3rd) and the Chargers will have to throw.
The Los Angeles offensive line has been solid in pass protection – allowing pressure within 2.5 seconds on only 11% of dropbacks (2nd).
Chargers LT Rashawn Slater ranks 6th in pass blocking efficiency, and he will contain edge rusher Alex Highsmith, who had 69 pressures last season (13th).
Justin Herbert walked off the field last week and went into a medical tent with trainers, but he didn’t miss a snap. His banged-up lower right leg will likely affect his mobility.
The Chargers passing attack will come down to whether rookie right tackle Joe Alt can hold his own across from edge defender TJ Watt, who had a league-high 19.0 sacks last year.
Los Angeles wide receiver Quentin Johnston is thriving with the new coaching staff averaging 0.54 EPA/target (13th) but he will be limited by cornerback Joey Porter, who is conceding 0.50 yards per cover snap (20th).
Herbert will look inside to the battle of the rookies with WR Ladd McConkey lined up across from Beanie Bishop, who is allowing 1.44 yards per cover snap in the slot ranking 25th out of 30 qualifying nickelbacks.
Our model favors the Chargers by 0.8 points, with a predicted total of 38.1 points. Pittsburgh applies to an 88-24-1 ATS situation and I used the Steelers in my spread pool.

Lean – MINNESOTA (+2) over Houston
I want to go through the pricing for this game because I’m not sure Houston should be favored.
The betting market priors before the season had the Texans 2.5 points better than an average team and the Vikings 2 points worse than an average team. Factor in 2 points for home field advantage and I think we would’ve seen this line at Houston -2.5 if this game were in week 1.
Here we are in week 3 and I think it’s fair to have upgraded the Vikings significantly more than the Texans. Minnesota’s offense is averaging 6.8 yppl (2nd) and the defense is allowing -0.22 EPA/play (4th). We’ve upgraded the Vikings by 2.5 points since the preseason.
Meanwhile, Houston’s offense is averaging 5.3 yppl (18th) and the defense is allowing -0.09 EPA/play (8th). The Texans have been favored by 3 and won by 2 and favored by 6.5 and won by 6. I’m not sure Houston deserves much of an upgrade after the first two weeks.
Vikings head coach Kevin O’Connell is scheming it up for Darnold with only a 10% tight window throw rate (5th-lowest) and Darnold is averaging 8.5 yppp (2nd).
I think Darnold will have time to operate O’Connell’s offense because Minnesota’s best offensive linemen will neutralize Houston’s best defensive linemen. Texans edge rushers Will Anderson and Danielle Hunter have combined to 17 pressures, but RT Brian O’Neill ranks 16th in pass blocking efficiency and LT Christian Darrisaw ranked 17th in pass blocking efficiency last season.
Our model favors the Vikings by 0.7 points, with a predicted total of 44.8 points.

NEW ORLEANS (-2.5) vs Philadelphia
The Saints scored touchdowns on their first 6 drives last week in Dallas and all but one of them were at least 70 yards.
New Orleans offensive coordinator Klint Kubiak has really gotten this unit cooking with motion and the deep ball. The Saints lead the NFL with an 84% motion rate and are averaging 11.0 yppp on dropbacks with motion. Wide receiver Rashid Shaheed’s 70-yard touchdown against the Cowboys was his league-high 7th catch on passes with 50+ air yards since the start of last season.
Dallas blitzed Carr on 41% of dropbacks but the New Orleans offensive line continues to hold up and is conceding just an 18% pressure rate.
This Saints offensive line was considered to be one of the least talented in the NFL heading into the year so I’m crediting most of the protection to Kubiak’s scheme. Only 23% of Derek Carr’s dropbacks have been true pass sets. In other words, 77% of his dropbacks used play action, screens, time-to-throws under 2 seconds, short dropbacks or saw less than 4 rushers.
Carr has a 51.2% play action rate through two weeks and is averaging 0.53 EPA/play on those dropbacks. No quarterback was over a 32% play action rate last season.
New Orleans rookie LT Taliese Fuaga will be tested on Sunday by edge defender Josh Sweat, who has 10 pressures (8th).
Sweat might have the advantage in pass rush but he will likely struggle defending the ground game. Sweat averaged only 0.9 run stops per game last year and all 4 of Saints RB Alvin Kamara’s rushes of at least 10 yards were outside to the left side of the formation last week.
New Orleans is using heavy personnel to force opposing defenses to put more linebackers on the field. The Cowboys were in base defense on 88% of snaps last week. Philadelphia’s linebacker depth is lacking and they are surrendering a 44.9% rush success rate (27th). The Saints are averaging 0.17 EPA/rush (2nd).
Eagles’ defensive coordinator Vic Fangio’s defense surrendered 0.15 EPA/target to opposing running backs last season (31st) in Miami and Philadelphia’s linebackers will also struggle to contain Kamara as a receiver. Kamara leads running backs averaging 3.83 yards per route run.
New Orleans defense allowed -0.21 EPA/dropback last week despite missing top cornerback Marshon Lattimore. Saints’ rookie CB Kool-Aid McKinstry is preventing separation on just 72% of his opportunities (2nd).
New Orleans had a 46% press coverage rate last week and the Cowboys wide receivers earned just 3 targets when pressed. Eagles’ wide receiver AJ Brown would typically be the option for Jalen Hurt against press but he is likely out another week.
I do think Hurts will be given enough time in the pocket to find other Philadelphia receivers in this game. Eagles RT Lane Johnson has not conceded a pressure this season and LT Jordan Mailata ranks 18th in pass blocking efficiency. Saints’ edge defender Chase Young has 9 pressures (10th) and edge defender Carl Granderson ranks 12th in pass rushing efficiency but they will be contained by Philadelphia’s tackles.
The Eagles ground game ranks 6th by our metrics but New Orleans is allowing only a 27.0% rush success rate (3rd).
Our model favors the Saints by 2.0 points, with a predicted total of 49.2 points. I used Philly in my spread pool.

TENNESSEE (-3/-2.5) vs Green Bay
Note
: The analysis below assumes that GB QB Jordan Love will be out again this week, although there is a chance he could play. If he does play then I’ll update the analysis when that is confirmed.
Green Bay’s offense had a comically low 12% early down pass play rate last week in Malik Willis’ first start, which was the lowest we’ve seen from an offense since Mac Jones’ rookie year when the Patriots went into Buffalo on a Monday night with sideways rain.
The Packers had a 45% rush success rate in the first half before the Colts realized they had no interest in letting Willis throw the ball. Indianapolis started loading up the box and Green Bay’s run game had only a 6% success rate in the second half.
Tennessee’s defense will just load up the box right away and they have the talent to man-up on Green Bay’s receivers. WR Romeo Doubs is averaging 0.98 EPA/target (3rd) but he will be shut down on the outside by Titans All-Pro CB L’Jarius Sneed and CB Chidobe Awuzie, who has conceded 0.50 yards per cover snap (20th).
Tennessee nickelback Roger McCreary is allowing just 0.41 yards per cover snap in the slot (4th) and he will limit WR Jayden Reed, who is gaining 3.42 yards per route run (6th).
The Titans lead the NFL with 5 giveaways due to some maddening decisions by Will Levis, but he has just a 2.7% turnover worthy play rate and I don’t expect him to continue the bone headedness. If anything, Tennessee will become extra conservative to protect against Levis. I wouldn’t be surprised if the Titans did not turn the ball over once on Sunday.
Tennessee will also likely not get a punt blocked which has happened two straight weeks. The Titans have lost 9 EPA from those blocks.
Tennessee’s offense should take the ball out of Levis’s hands and pound the rock because the pass protection isn’t going to hold up. Packers edge defender Rashan Gary ranked 9th in pass rushing efficiency last season and he will wreak havoc across from RT Nicholas Petit-Frere, who has allowed a league-high 11 pressures.
Titans’ guards Peter Skoronski and Dillon Radunz rank 54th and 58th respectively in pass blocking efficiency out of 61 qualifying guards. Devonte Wyatt and Kenny Clark have combined for 13 pressures on the interior and should get at least one sack versus Tennessee’s guards.
Our model favors the Titans by 3.1 with a predicted total of 38.6.

SEATTLE (-4.5) vs Miami
Tua Tagovailoa will be out indefinitely after sustaining another concussion. Tagovailoa has averaged 0.17 EPA/play (8th) with head coach Mike McDaniel. Dolphins’ backup quarterback Skylar Thompson is averaging -0.27 EPA/play in the same offense, ranking 67th out of 72 qualifying quarterbacks with at least 100 dropbacks since 2022. Miami’s offense will drop 4.2 points according to our numbers.
Running back De’Von Achane led the Dolphins in both rushing (96 yards) and receiving (69 yards) last week versus the Bills while also forcing 0.31 missed tackles per touch. Miami’s offense will need a healthy dose of Achane again in this game with the backup quarterback. Seattle’s defense has allowed only 20 yards from 14 targets to running backs in the first two weeks but starting LB Jerome Baker suffered a hamstring injury last game and McDaniel can pick on Seahawks backup rookie linebacker Tyrice Knight.
I believe All-Pro WR Tyreek Hill will be contained on Sunday as Seattle nickelback Devon Witherspoon is conceding 0.42 yards per cover snap in the slot (5th) and CB Tariq Woolen ran an official 4.26-second 40-yard dash at the 2022 combine. Woolen is conceding 0.09 yards per cover snap (9th).
Thompson will instead look for Miami WR Jaylen Waddle, whose 61% success rate last year ranked 2nd in the NFL.
Miami LT Terron Armstead was not 100 percent last week and left the field a few times to get a shoulder harness worked on. Armstead ranks 11th in pass blocking efficiency and I’m counting him in at this moment. I’m also counting in Seahawks edge defender Boye Mafe, who will line up across from Armstead. Mafe suffered a minor knee injury late in the game against New England but should be in uniform this week and he ranks 4th in pass rushing efficiency.
Seattle’s interior defenders Leonard Williams and Byron Murphy have combined for 17 pressures, and they will collapse the pocket for Thompson. Dolphins starting guard Isaiah Wynn conceded only a 1.9% pressure rate last season, but he is out and backup Liam Eichenberg is allowing a 4.0% pressure rate since the start of 2023.
The Seahawks have protection issues of their own without starting RT Abe Lucas and backup George Fant. Seattle 3rd-string RT Stone Forsythe ranks 6th-worst in pass blocking efficiency.
Geno Smith was in empty formations on 32% of his dropbacks last week to make New England’s pass rush more predictable to cover for the offensive line.
Miami’s pass rush was suspect coming into the year after losing after losing 181 pressures from defensive linemen Andrew Van Ginkel, Bradley Chubb, and Christian Wilkins but the Dolphins have 41.3% pressure rate this season (4th).
However, Miami’s defense has a 37.0% blitz rate (7th-highest) and new Seattle offensive coordinator Ryan Grubb seems to have solved Smith’s problem versus the blitz.
Smith’s yards per attempt against the blitz was 0.7 lower than when opposing defenses didn’t send extra pass rushers last season. Smith is averaging 8.7 yards per attempt versus blitz this year (3rd).
Seahawks wide receivers DK Metcalf and Tyler Lockett will be limited on the outside by Pro Bowl CB Jalen Ramsey and CB Kendall Fuller, who has conceded 0.39 yards per cover snap (12th).
Dolphins nickelback Kader Kohou is surrendering 2.03 yards per cover snap in the slot (2nd-worst) and he will struggle across from WR Jaxon Smith-Njigba, who has a 72% success rate (4th).
Our model makes Seattle a 6.1-point favorite with a predicted total of 42.3.

Detroit (-3) vs ARIZONA
Kyler Murray averaged 11.7 yppp last week against the Rams by extending plays and throwing downfield. Murray averaged 18.2 yppp on dropbacks longer than 2.5 seconds.
We know Murray is one of the most elusive quarterbacks in the NFL but I’m more skeptical he can continue the deep passing success. Murray completed all 5 of his passes with at least 20 air yards last week but last season he went just 8 for 26 on such throws.
Murray is averaging 0.45 EPA/play (2nd) as Arizona’s offense has torched defenses with bad linebackers. Bills All-Pro LB Matt Milano is out, and the Rams traded top linebacker Ernest Jones before the season.
The Cardinals have used 13 personnel on 20% of snaps (2nd-highest) to force linebackers onto the field and they’ll do so again on Sunday as Lions top LB Alex Anzalone is likely out with a concussion.
Anzalone leads linebackers in preventing separation on 63% of his opportunities (6th) and he is worth 0.4 points to Detroit’s defense.
Lions CB Carlton Davis is not off to a great start in his new defense, as he’s surrendering a league-high 171 yards through two games. He could struggle again this week versus WR Marvin Harrison, who is averaging 0.77 EPA/target (8th). The rookie had 8 targets last week with Arizona’s next closest receiver only having 2.
The Cardinals are targeting tight ends on 34.6% of passes (3rd-most) but TE Trey McBride has not caught a pass versus man-coverage this season and Detroit’s defense has a 42.4% man-coverage rate (3rd-highest). The Lions conceded 0.07 EPA/target to TEs last season (8th).
Cardinals’ backup RT Kelvin Beachum ranks 52nd in pass blocking efficiency out of 59 qualifying tackles and he will get even worse after this week against edge defender Aidan Hutchinson, who leads NFL with 5.5 sacks.
The Lions are converting just 27% of their redzone drives into touchdowns through two weeks after having a 67% redzone touchdown rate last season. Detroit’s offense should start punching it in more often.
Amon-Ra St. Brown seemed to be in pain after suffering a hit on Detroit’s final drive and he might be banged-up this week versus Arizona nickelback Garrett Williams, who is conceding 0.48 yards per cover snap in the slot (6th).
Our model favors the Lions by 3.1 with a predicted total of 52.1.

Lean – DALLAS (+1) over Baltimore
Baltimore’s defense pass rush does not look the same this season and it’s becoming more and more clear they were getting by on scheme in 2023. The Ravens have a 28.3% pressure rate (27th) while the Seahawks have a 43.4% pressure rate (2nd) coached by former Baltimore defensive coordinator Mike Macdonald.

Dallas is allowing a league-high 0.15 EPA/rush and I expect Baltimore to feed Derrick Henry on Sunday. The Ravens are averaging 0.08 EPA/rush (3rd).
Baltimore’s right tackles Patrick Mekari and rookie Roger Rosengarten are combining to surrender a 9% pressure rate and they will struggle against edge defender Micah Parsons, who led the league in pass rushing efficiency last season.
Lamar Jackson will look downfield to WR Rashod Bateman when he extends plays evading the Cowboys pass rush. Bateman has 3 deep targets (10th) and he will line up across from Dallas rookie CB Caelen Carson, who is preventing separation on only 20% of his opportunities (3rd-worst).

Lean – ATLANTA (+3.5) over Kansas City
Atlanta had only a 1% chance of winning after Jalen Hurts converted a 3rd down with 2 minutes left last week, but the Falcons pulled off the comeback on Monday night.
However, Kirk Cousins is averaging just 5.8 yppp excluding the last drive against Philly, which was not Atlanta’s normal offense, and Cousins admitted himself “I don’t feel like I was sharp enough”. He wasn’t pushing off his back foot much due to the Achilles even on the last drive and he doesn’t look comfortable in the structure of his new scheme.
Atlanta offensive coordinator Zac Robinson’s scheme has also completely changed the dynamic of this offensive line. The Falcons ranked 2nd in pass blocking efficiency last season, and they rank 31st through two weeks.
Atlanta All-Pro RG Chris Lindstrom most exemplifies the regression surrendering a league-high 8 pressures this season after ranking 2nd in pass blocking efficiency in 2023. Chiefs DT Chris Jones leads the NFL with 13 pressures and will line up across from Lindstrom on Sunday.
Kansas City cornerback Trent McDuffie shadowed Cincinnati wide receiver Ja’Marr Chase on about half his routes last week and conceded only 1 catch for 4 yards. McDuffie will similarly shut down Falcons WR Drake London, who has a 64% success rate (10th).
Atlanta RB Bijan Robinson has 41 touches in two games and the Falcons will need to get him going on the ground and through the air in this game. Robinson leads running backs with a 70% pass success rate. Chiefs’ defensive coordinator Steve Spagnuolo’s defense is happy to concede swing passes to running backs and Kansas City’s defense ranked 28th in EPA/rush allowed last season.
Patrick Mahomes has not been able to get Travis Kelce going this year, averaging only 5.6 yards per target, and I do not expect them to turn it around this week as Kelce will be limited by Falcons All-Pro safety Jessie Bates, who is getting 34% of his snaps in the box through two games under new head coach Raheem Morris after never getting a 20% snap share in the box in his previous 6 seasons.
Our model favors the Chiefs by 5.6 points, with a predicted total of 46.9 points, but Atlanta applies to a 60-11-3 ATS situation and I’ll lean with the Falcons based on that.

Lean – Over (45.5) – BUFFALO (-5) vs Jacksonville

I’m calling a breakout game for Bills rookie wide receiver Keon Coleman. Although, it could also be WR Mack Hollins or WR Marquez Valdes-Scantling. Someone is getting loose in Jacksonville’s secondary.
Jaguars CB Tyson Campbell was preventing separation on just 64% of his opportunities (6th) but he is out and backup Montaric Brown is getting picked on. Brown saw 12 targets thrown into his coverage last week.
