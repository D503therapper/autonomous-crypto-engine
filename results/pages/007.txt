From doormat to the dominant force of college football, Indiana head coach Curt Cignetti changed the game forever after leading the Hoosiers to their first national championshipin January. Statistically noted as the worst Power Four football program in the country, Indiana has become a national power by going 32-2 in Cignetti's 2½ seasons, which includes a 5-0 start in 2026.
Perfecting the mix of his James Madison followers, transfer portal additions and homegrown recruits, Indiana tore through college football for a perfect 16-0 record. Cal transfer Fernando Mendoza thrived to win the program's first-ever Heisman Trophy and later becoming the No. 1 overall pick in the NFL Draft. The Hoosiers also lost several other key contributors, including all-conference receivers Omar Cooper Jr. (first round) and Elijah Sarratt (fourth round), starting running backs Roman Hemby and Kaelon Black (third round), All-American defensive backs D’Angelo Ponds (second round) and Louis Moore, Rose Bowl MVP center Pat Coogan (sixth round) and standout linebacker Aidan Fisher (seventh round).
Those departures forced Cignetti into the portal where he was able to flex the muscle of winning a national title. Josh Hoover always had one of the best arms in college football during his four seasons at TCU, but he could never take the next step, especially when it came to turnovers. Throwing 33 interceptions in his three seasons starting for the Horn Frogs, Hoover has been perfect nearly halfway through the 2026 campaign by throwing for over 1,100 yards with 15 touchdowns and zero picks.
A new dynamic duo leads the backfield for IU this season in Lee Beebe Jr. and Turbo Richards. Beebe Jr. was an all-conference running back for UAB in 2024, but he suffered a season-ending injury early in 2025 before returning this year to lead the Hoosiers in rushing. Beside him is Boston College transfer Turbo Richards where he's been equally as productive. The Huskers have history with Richards as the 5-foot-9 junior only record seven yards on four carries during NU's win over the Eagles in the 2024 Pinstripe Bowl.

Returner Charlie Becker is one of the best receivers in the Big Ten outside of Ohio State's Jeremiah Smith. He showed up time and time again for IU's run to the national title, which has continued in 2026 with a team-high 477 yards and eight touchdowns, which is tied for second in the conference. An All-Big Ten Honorable Mention last season at Michigan State, heralded transfer Nick Marsh has grabbed four touchdowns with 341 yards in his first five games as a Hoosier. 2025 Consensus All-American tackle Carter Smith leads a veteran offensive line that includes a pair of Wisconsin transfers in Drew Evans and Joe Brunner.
The Hoosiers boast a stout defense that ranks among the nation’s best against the run, which is led by nose tackle Tyrique Tucker. The former James Madison transfer comes off a 2025 that saw him earn First Team All-American and All-Big Ten accolades. Tobi Osunsanmi has been well worth the spend after bringing him over from Kansas State through the transfer portal. He leads Indiana and is tied for the conference lead in sacks (4.0) and is second with 8.5 tackles for loss. All-conference linebackers Rolijah Harrdy and Isaiah Jones round up a loaded front seven.
