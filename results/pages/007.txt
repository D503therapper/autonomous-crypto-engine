North Texas (5-2, 3-0 American) is one of two remaining undefeated teams in American play, sharing the designation with UTSA. The Mean Green have pieced together one of the most impressive reloads in college football this season under first-year head coach Neal Brown. They were one of two programs, along with Iowa State, to return zero starters from 2025, as many followed former head coach Eric Morris to Oklahoma State.
The nation’s No. 1 passer Drew Mestemaker, No. 5 rusher Caleb Hawkins, and No. 3 receiver Wyatt Young were among those who made the offseason trek to Stillwater. Despite the Mean Green losing a bulk of talent from the nation’s No. 1 offense, they remain just as lethal on that side of the ball with a new crop of talent. North Texas ranks second in scoring (43.1 points per game) and fourth in total offense (526 yards per game), and a new big three has emerged on offense.
In terms of yards per game, North Texas now fields the nation’s No. 3 passer Tayven Jackson, No. 6 rusher Jahiem White, and No. 14 receiver Justin Stevenson. These three Big 12 transfers headline an offense that has scored 44 points or greater in all five wins. Jackson has eclipsed 300 yards in every American matchup, firing 12 passing touchdowns across those three games. White has been among the most explosive rushers in the country, and his 7.4 yards per carry rank second in the FBS among ball carriers with at least 70 attempts. When White surpasses 100 yards, North Texas is 5-0, and he has managed a touchdown in all seven games.
Stevenson has emerged as a star more recently. His 209 yards against Navy were a career-high, and he has scored multiple touchdowns in three consecutive games. Stevenson’s rise has coincided with the injury of Corri Milliner, who will remain out for a third consecutive game. Other options in the receiving game include tight end Max Reese and receivers Kyle Koch and Barton Tipton. Additionally, White plays a pivotal role as a receiver. The star running back ranks second on the team with 27 receptions and third with 250 receiving yards.
North Texas’ defense oscillates in performance each game. In games one, three, five, and seven, the Mean Green surrendered at least 39 points each time. But in games two, four, and six, they held each of their opponents to 14 points or fewer. The result is a 116th-ranked scoring defense and 92nd-ranked total defense that has mainly struggled against the run game. North Texas has been stout at defending third downs, but there have been deficiencies near the goal line, as only 10 teams allow a higher percentage of red zone touchdowns than the Mean Green.
The Mean Green defense contributed to its 49 points last week against Navy, as inside linebacker Zakye Barker recovered a fumble for a touchdown. Barker is the leading tackler with 57 stops. Another wreaker of havoc on the unit is defensive end Davion Rhodes, who leads the team with 4.0 sacks and four forced fumbles.
Additionally, North Texas received a special teams contribution last week as Dalton Carnes returned a punt 76 yards for a touchdown. The teams in this matchup now combine for five special teams return touchdowns on the year.
