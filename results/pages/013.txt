The Chippewas found themselves in a 31-0 halftime hole last week at Ohio. And while CMU was much better in the second half, it was not nearly enough to overcome the deficit.
Clearly, the Chippewas have to be better, not only this week against the Broncos, but for the remainder of the season if they hope to be a factor in the MAC race.
"I think we came out ready to play," Drinkall said. "What we didn't do well was weather the first two big pieces of adversity that happened on the first two series."
The first was a pass interference penalty that wiped out a CMU interception; the second was a Chippewa failure to convert a fourth down.
Before long, the Chippewas found themselves in a 14-0 deficit and, on the road, were squarely on their heels.
"You can whittle your way back at 14-0 through playing the rest of the game," Drinkall said. "There's probably plenty of time left.
"We did a lot of uncharacteristic things from an assignment and discipline standpoint because we were just trying to do too much. We got punched in the face and never got settled again until the second half."
While the Chippewas would, obviously, prefer to be unbeaten in MAC play, they are far from out of the race for a spot in the title game. That said, they need a win over the Broncos to get back on the lead lap.
"I can handle losing," Drinkall said. "I can't handle losing with bad football. And we played really bad football for a half, and that part is on me, and that can't happen. We have got to figure out.
"You really need to focus on the stuff you can control, which is your own preparation, execution, and your own readiness."
The Broncos finished first in the MAC preseason poll. They stopped Buffalo on their 1-yard line with 2 seconds left to preserve a 20-17 win over the Bulls in their league opener, and then last week rolled past Kent State, 38-7.
