Brent Vigen, who recently became the winningest head coach in Montana State history, provided an injury update before the Bobcats’ regular season-ending game against rival Montana.

8-3, overall, 6-2 Big Sky
Brent Vigen says:
“A great opportunity in front of us: the rivalry, that being at stake; the conference title, the outright aspect there; playoff seeding. We want to put ourselves in this final game — regular-season game — to have all those things in play, and we do. Thankful for the efforts of our players and our staff to be able to do that. …
“Definitely a game where both teams have all the things to play for. That’s what you want. I told our guys that they better appreciate this. There are many college football players across the country that don’t have any idea what this is about, to play in this type of game with the stakes, the atmosphere, all those things. So appreciate it, have fun and prepare like crazy so we can go out and play as well as possible come Saturday.”
What’s a stake:
A win over the Grizzlies would give Montana State its third straight outright Big Sky title. The Bobcats also shared the conference championship with Sacramento State in 2022. The only other time they’ve won four league titles in five years was from 1964-68. They won three in row from 1966-68 and from 2010-12.

Notes:
The Bobcats “anticipate” starting defensive end Dominic Solano returning this week from an injury he suffered Sept. 12 at Nevada, Vigen said Monday.
As for injured backup nose tackle Josaiah Asuega, “I think we’ll have him back” against the Grizzlies, Vigen said.
If Asuega plays, he and Solano will be the only Bobcats “that we didn’t have last week that we will this week,” Vigen said. That means star wide receiver/punt returner Taco Dowler will miss his eighth straight game.
Backup running back Colson Coon, who left the UNC game in the first half with a leg injury, is “questionable” for the Brawl, Vigen said.
The Cats lead the Big Sky in scoring defense (19.7 points allowed per game), total defense (347.4 yards allowed per game) and rushing defense (117.5 yards allowed per game), while trailing only Montana in scoring offense (39.6 points per game) and total offense (444.9 yards per game). UM is averaging 39.9 points and 480 total yards per game.
