THURSDAY Ole Miss vs. Texas Injury Report: Colin Simmons Out, Kewan Lacy Off the Report
Posted by
Evelyn Van Pelt
|
OXFORD, Miss. –
Texas will be without its best defensive player on Saturday night. Edge rusher
Colin Simmons
, who leads the FBS in sacks, has been ruled out for the Longhorns’ game against No. 7 Ole Miss, according to the SEC’s Thursday availability report released at 7:10 p.m. CT. Simmons was listed as doubtful on Wednesday.
For Ole Miss, the news is mostly good.
Running back Kewan Lacy, right guard Patrick Kutas and wide receiver Caleb Cunningham
, all listed as probable on Wednesday, are off the report entirely.
Offensive linemen Terez Davis and Brycen Sanders
remain questionable, while cornerback
Pat Broomfield
has been downgraded from questionable to out.
Texas also downgraded running back
Hollywood Smothers
from questionable to doubtful.
Teams will release one more update Friday evening, followed by a final report on game day.

What changed since Wednesday
Ole Miss:
Off the report:
RB Kewan Lacy, OL Patrick Kutas, WR Caleb Cunningham, S Anthony Robinson III, S Craig Tutt, RB Ja’Michael Jones and TE Hayden Bradley were all listed as probable Wednesday and no longer appear on the report.
Downgraded:
CB Pat Broomfield, from questionable to out.
No change:
OL Terez Davis and OL Brycen Sanders remain questionable.
Texas:
Downgraded:
EDGE Colin Simmons, from doubtful to out, and RB Hollywood Smothers, from questionable to doubtful.
Added:
RB Derrek Cooper, listed as probable.
No change:
EDGE Richard Wesley remains out, and WR Kaliq Lockett and OL Jaydon Chatman remain questionable.
What it means for Ole Miss
The offensive line.
The status of Davis and Sanders is still the biggest question for the Rebels. Both left last week’s win over Missouri, and Ole Miss adjusted without missing a beat. Patrick Kutas slid from right guard to center, Troy Everett stepped in at guard and Connor Howes moved over to fill in for Davis. The Rebels still ran for a season-high 257 yards, and Pro Football Focus graded the line as the best in the FBS for the week.
If Sanders can’t play, Kutas, who is now off the injury report, could slide back to center. Kutas said this week that the toughest part of playing center is making the line calls, but that he already thinks “like a center” from his spot at guard. Read more in
Patrick Kutas on sliding to center
.
The backfield.
Lacy being off the report is the best news of the night for Ole Miss. He has been managing injuries for weeks and left the Missouri game for part of the first half before returning to finish with 122 yards. With Makhi Frazier, who ran for 101 yards against the Tigers, the Rebels have two backs ready for a physical game. Lacy, a Dallas native who grew up a Texas fan, will get to play in front of family and friends in his home state.
The secondary.
Ole Miss will be thin in the defensive backfield. Joseph, the starting nickel, will miss a second straight game after a tightrope procedure on his ankle, and Preston and Broomfield are also out. Nick Cull filled in at nickel against Missouri with three tackles and a pass breakup. At cornerback, the Rebels list Jaylon Braxton and Jalyn Crawford as co-starters on one side and Antonio Kite and Dorian Barney on the other. Robinson and Tutt being cleared adds some needed depth at safety.
What it means for Texas
Losing Simmons is a major blow for the Longhorns. He leads the FBS in sacks and has been the centerpiece of a pass rush that has 19 sacks in six games. With Wesley also out, Texas will be without two of its edge rushers against an Ole Miss line that allows just 0.83 sacks per game, the fewest in the SEC.
Pete Golding still expects plenty of pressure. He said Monday that the Texas front is “really explosive” and creates “a lot of pressure with four,” and he called the Longhorns “a really, really talented defense” even without Simmons.
On offense, Texas may be without half of its running back rotation. Smothers has 289 rushing yards this season, and if he can’t go, more of the load will fall on Raleek Brown, who has 265 yards and a team-high five rushing touchdowns, with Derrek Cooper also available. Golding said stopping the run on early downs is the first key for his defense, so the Texas backfield will be one to watch.
The matchup to watch
With Simmons out, the battle between the Ole Miss offensive line and the Texas front becomes far more balanced. The Rebels have allowed only 3.2 tackles for loss per game this season, 15th nationally, and they have scored on all 24 of their red zone trips. Texas still has one of the best defenses in the country, allowing just 17.8 points and 272.5 yards per game, but the Longhorns now have to generate pressure without the player who has been their most disruptive all season.
If Davis and Sanders are able to play, Ole Miss will have its full starting line back. If not, the Rebels have already shown they can shuffle and keep rolling.
When the next report comes out
The SEC will release Friday’s availability report at 7:10 p.m. CT, followed by a final report on game day before kickoff.

No. 7 Ole Miss (5-1, 3-1 SEC) visits No. 19 Texas (4-2, 1-2 SEC) at 6:30 p.m. CT Saturday at Darrell K Royal-Texas Memorial Stadium in Austin. The game will be broadcast on ABC.
