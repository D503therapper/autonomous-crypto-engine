Indianapolis (-3.5) vs Washington
The Colts’ offense struggled last week without big play WR Alec Pierce, who has led the NFL in each of the past two seasons in yards per reception while averaging an elite 11.9 yards per target (10.3 YPT on 7 targets this season before injury). Newly signed WR Darius Slayton did not play last week but he’s expected to play in this game.
Slayton is a good pickup, as he’s averaged 8.5 yards per target in his 7 seasons (mostly playing with Colts’ QB Daniel Jones), including 8.5 YPT last season. I only adjusted 1.1 points for Pierce being out to be conservative because Jones has played at a decent level in the 4.5 games that he’s been without Pierce and because he’s had better than average success throwing to Slayton.
Washington’s defense ranks near 5the bottom of the league in EPA per play allowed (#26) and success rate (#27) and I expect the Colts to move the ball at a pretty good rate.
The Commanders should also have success moving the ball, regardless of which quarterback plays. Jayden Daniels may play with his injured non-throwing shoulder but Marcus Mariota has been better than Daniels in EPA per play (which includes runs/scrambles) and success rate since the beginning of last season on a very similar number of plays. The Colts’ defense has been even worse than Washington’s defense in EPA per play (#31) and success rate (#29).
Washington’s 33-31 win last week over Seattle was deceiving given that they were outgained 258 yards at 4.0 yards per play to 437 yards at 6.8 yppl (they were +3 in turnovers, which is lucky) but I do think the Commanders are a bit underrated and I think the line should be Colts by 2 points (49.0 total points). However, Washington applies to a very negative 37-102-3 ATS letdown situation and favorites tend to cover the spread in these international games.
I’ll pass.

BUFFALO (-7 -105) vs New England
The Patriots continue to struggle offensively against better than average defensive teams, as they’ve now averaged only 14.7 points in their last 7 games – all against better than average defensive teams. Buffalo is a worse than average defensive team, but the right side of the Patriots’ offensive line is falling apart.
Starting RG Michael Onwenu was injured in the 1
st
quarter of week 2 and his backup Greg Van Roten was injured last week. That put rarely used Walter Rouse in the game last week and he’ll likely start this week. Onwenu is a solid guard with a 3.9% pressure rate and 0.5% sack rate allowed in recent years. Van Roten is a bit worse (4.5% pressure and 0.7% sack rate allowed since 2022) but Rouse has allowed 7 pressures and 2 sacks in 84 career pass block snaps (8.3% and 2.4%). Going from Onwenu to Van Roten hurt a bit but going to Rouse is a significant difference, as the Patriots have a -0.22 EPA per play average in 40 snaps with Rouse in the game this season (-0.11 EPA with Rouse not in the game), which is equivalent to 6.6 points over 60 snaps in a game. That’s a small sample, but Rouse also played 87 snaps for the Vikings last season and their EPA per play was -0.37 with Rouse in the game, compared to -0.07 EPA per play without Rouse in the game. That’s about 3 games worth of significantly worse EPA per play with Rouse on the field. I think a 1 point adjustment is warranted, which is very high for an offensive guard.
RT Morgan Moses has also not practiced this week (as of Thursday night), which makes him highly unlikely to play. Moses is not a star like Onwenu, and his backup isn’t as bad as Rouse, but having two backups on the same side of the offensive line is a problem.
The Patriots may also have a huge issue on defense this week if elite CB Christian Gonzalez can’t play. Gonzalez injured himself last week and missed the first two practices this week (through Thursday), which is a negative indicator for him playing in this game. DL Christian Barmore also has not participated in the mid-week practices and is unlikely to play.
Gonzalez is considered the be the best cornerback in the NFL and last season in 3 games without him the Patriots went 1-2 while giving up an average of 22.7 points and a +0.084 EPA per play to below average offensive teams the Raiders, Dolphins and Steelers. In their regular season games with Gonzalez the Pats were 13-1 (and 3-1 in the postseason) with an EPA per play allowed of -0.065.
Since the start of the 2024 season, the Patriots have played 342 defensive snaps without Gonzalez (excluding the 2024, week 18 game when starters for both the Pats and Bills sat) and they have been worse than average in those 6 full games worth of plays (+0.035 EPA/play). In 2,069 snaps with Gonzalez on the field the Patriots’ defense has an EPA/play allowed of -0.045. At an average of 56 snaps for Gonzalez per game that difference equates to more than 4 points per game. I’ll adjust 2.5 points if Gonzalez can’t play, but that may not be enough.
The Bills make up for their worse than average defense with an elite offense that’s averaged 34 points per game on over 400 yards per game while ranking 2
nd
in EPA per play and #6 in success rate (they’re 1
st
in explosive play percentage). That unit even managed to score 36 points on 409 yards against an elite Houston defense, so they should also be able to move the ball on a good Patriots’ defense (even if Gonzalez plays).
The Patriots do apply to a 152-65-6 ATS bounce-back situation and Mike Vrabel is 19-9 ATS as a head coach when getting 4 points or more. However, I’m not interested in New England if Gonzalez doesn’t play.
Check back on Sunday morning for an update and my predicted spread and total
(once the status of Gonzalez is clearer).
If Gonzalez plays then I’d lean with New England
. If not, then it’s a pass.

CHICAGO (-3.5) vs NY Jets
I would Lean with Chicago at -3 -115 or better
The Bears overcame the absence of quarterback Caleb Williams and prevailed 27-7 over a struggling Eagles team last Monday night with veteran 3
rd
-string quarterback Case Keenum at the helm of the offense. Williams’ value is solely in his running/scrambling, as his career 60.5% completion rate (58.7% under Ben Johnson) is well below average, as is his -2.7% completion percentage over expectation (CPOE). Keenum completed 71% of his passes against a good defense last week with a +2.8% CPOE and my adjustment for Williams is just the 2.0 points per game that his running provides (versus the negative run EPA of Keenum). It’s possible that #2 QB Tyson Bagent gets the start (Johnson is not tipping his hand) but how can you not start Keenum here after last week’s performance?
The Bears’ defense played well last week, and their advanced metrics are better than average (despite allowing 6.1 yards per play), as they rate at 1.8 points per game better than average. However, they’ve also faced weak offensive teams and had weather conditions that favor the defenses – two factors that combine to make it 4.1 points per game easier than average for their defense. Chicago has actually been 2.3 points per game worse than average defensively when taking weather and opposing offenses into account and I rate that unit at 1.5 points worse than average.
The Jets should move the ball at a decent rate, as Geno Smith is back to being one of the most accurate quarterbacks in the league after a disastrous season behind a terrible offensive line in Vegas. The Jets have posted average offense numbers, but they’ve faced a trio of below average defenses in Tennessee, Green Bay, and Detroit.
The Jets’ defense has been average so far this season, but I still rate the Bears as 1.0 points better than average offensively even without Williams.
The market is adjusting more for Williams being out than I am, which is why my ratings favor the Bears by 4.7 points (with 43.8 total points).
I’d Lean with Chicago if the line goes back down to -3

Lean – Arizona (-2.5) over NY GIANTS
The Giants’ offense struggled with Jameis Winston starting last week and Winston is just 15-35-3 ATS in his career starts when not getting more than 3 points. I do believe that Winston is better than he’s shown and I rate him just 3.3 points worse than Jaxson Dart. However, Winston and a worse than average rushing attack (just 3.9 ypr with lead back Cam Skattebo averaging just 3.5 ypr) puts the Giants near the bottom in my offensive ratings.
Arizona has a below average defense, but that unit is better than the Giants’ offense. The Cardinals’ offense has been efficient, ranking 13
th
in EPA per paly and #8 in success rate despite a schedule of better than average defensive teams (LAC, Sea, SF, although without Bosa). Jacoby Brissett is the epitome of a game manager, as he’s completed 70% of his passes while averaging only 8.0 yards per completion with just 1 interception (Brissett has the lowest interception percentage in NFL history).
The Giants have only allowed 18.3 points per game, but they rank #27 in EPA per play allowed and #25 in success rate.
Edge rusher Brian Burns is out for the rest of the season but I’m not adjusting for his loss. Burns had 16.5 sacks last season, which is 5 more than he should have had based on his number of pressures. Burns’ 14% pressure rate was still impressive, but he and fellow edge rush starter Abdul Carter (15.6% pressure rate last season) took advantage of All-Pro tackle Dexter Lawrence getting double-teamed more than any other interior linemen.
Lawrence is now anchoring the Bengals’ D-line and the pressure rate of all the Giants’ edge rushers has plummeted, with Burns dropping down to 5.1% (3 pressures and zero sacks), Carter dropping to 8.6% and Kayvon Thibadeaux going from 12% down to 9%.
As a team the Giants pressure rate has dropped 12% without Lawrence drawing those double-teams and New York ranks last in the league in pressures and sacks with just one.
Since the start of last season, the Giants have had a better EPA per play allowed with Burns off the field than on. In 926 plays with Burns on the field the Giants allowed a +0.11 EPA per play, which was significantly worse than the -0.04 EPA per play allowed in 297 plays without Burns on the field. The biggest difference is the EPA on run plays, which has a 0.24 EPA per play difference between Burns on the field and off (NYG was also better on pass plays with Burns off the field). I see no reason to assume the Giants will be worse without Burns and they may end up being better based on the 5 games worth of snaps the last two seasons without him on the field.
New York also have 5 defensive backs on injured reserve and CB Deonte Banks and S Tyler Nubin have been limited in practice this week (Banks has missed the last two games).
My ratings favor Arizona by just 2.0 points, and 43.5 total points, but I’ll lean with the Cardinals based on a 111-43-1 ATS situation.

HOUSTON (-3) vs Dallas
This is an interesting battle between a bad offense, good defense team and a good offense, bad defense team.
Houston ranks #24 and #25 in EPA per play and success rate on offense despite facing defenses that are collectively below average and playing all 3 games in dome stadiums. The Texans should be getting back their top WR Nico Collins this week (he’s missed the last 2 games), which will help, and they should move the ball well against a banged-up Cowboys’ defense that is last in the league in both EPA and success. Dallas traded for Steelers’ star CB Joey Porter Jr. and he may play this weekend.
The Dallas offense has been good (#5 in EPA/play and #7 in success rate) and they may have hit their stride last week in Brazil after struggling a bit the first two weeks. Houston’s defense is still elite despite allowing 25 points per game and ranking #15 in EPA per play allowed. More indicative of their quality is the 37.6% success rate allowed, which ranks 3
rd
best in the league and is better than last season’s dominating unit. Even more impressive is that Houston has faced teams (Buffalo, Cincy and Indy) and conditions (all 3 games in domes) that are more than 4 points tougher than average for a defense.
My ratings favor Houston by 4.1 points with 47.9 total points.
