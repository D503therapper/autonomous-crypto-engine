Oct. 1—PULLMAN — Washington State might not be at full strength at the running back spot on Saturday.
That's according to the first Pac-12 availability report, which listed tailback Kirby Vorhees as questionable, casting doubt on whether he'll be able to play in WSU's conference opener at home against Fresno State , set to kick off at 6:30 p.m. on USA Network.

Vorhees is dealing with an upper-body injury he sustained during practice this week, WSU coach Kirby Moore said, adding that Vorhees is "a little dinged up, but he's done a good job getting back into practice. So we're just working through that."
Also on the availability report is the news that tight end Trey Leckner is probable, which would mark his season debut. He's been out most of the year, missing all of spring ball and fall camp with a foot injury, which he reaggravated over the summer. It's big news for the Cougars (1-3) and their offense, which has yet to find its footing against FBS competition.
A redshirt junior from Snohomish, Leckner started nine of 13 games last season, catching 24 passes for 186 yards and three touchdowns, which tied for the third-most by a tight end in WSU single-season history. In 2024, he played in 12 games (two as a starter), reeling in three passes for 56 yards and one score.
Moore described Vorhees and Leckner as "gametime decisions," as he did with cornerback Jalil Tucker, who started the Sept. 6 Apple Cup but has been out since with an injury he sustained in that game.

In other news from the availability report, linebacker Nylan Brown is expected to miss "a significant amount of time" with a foot injury, Moore said. That happened during WSU's loss to Arizona last weekend, Moore said, when Brown played 12 snaps of special teams before going down with his injury.
Brown's injury could end his season, Moore said, but he indicated that "we're determining that right now."
Brown has taken a reserve role at linebacker, where the team's top options have emerged in starters Keith Brown and DJ Warner, plus key backup Isaiah Hung. Redshirt sophomore Gage Jones could see an expanded role as well, Moore said. Nylan Brown has played only 22 snaps of defense this season.
The rest of WSU's availability report looks like this: Linebacker Jack Ellison is doubtful and safety Kyle Peterson is out, as are defensive ends Malachi Wrice and Mike Hughes. Wrice and Hughes' absences could loom the largest for WSU, which has relied on starting pass rushers Matyus McLain and Linus Zunk for heavy snap counts — 184 each through four games.

In four games, Vorhees has totaled 36 carries for 210 yards and two touchdowns, the majority of that production coming from his 109-yard outing against FCS Duquesne, scoring a touchdown in that game. Most recently, in WSU's loss to Arizona last weekend, Vorhees posted 39 yards on nine carries.
If Vorhees can't play Saturday, expect more carries to go to WSU running back Maxwell Woods, who has looked sharp to open the season. He's picked up 173 yards on 28 carries, a large majority of those numbers also coming in his team's win over Duquesne, but he has looked shifty and elusive, no matter the opponent.
Fellow running back Leo Pulalasi could also play a key role if Vorhees can't go, though Pulalasi hasn't seen the same playing time that Woods has.
The rebuilt Pac-12 is in its first season of rolling out its availability report, following suit with the Power Four leagues of the SEC, Big Ten, Big 12 and ACC.
