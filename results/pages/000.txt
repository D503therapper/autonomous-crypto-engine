November 26, 2026
Notre Dame has one more regular-season game to strengthen its College Football Playoff position, and Syracuse must pull a major upset to reach six wins. The No. 4 CFP-ranked Fighting Irish arrive with a defense allowing 12.8 points per game, while the Orange have lost starting quarterback Steve Angeli to a shoulder injury. Malachi Nelson now faces an exceptionally difficult assignment against Marcus Freeman’s defense. Notre Dame Allows 12.8 Points a Game; Syracuse Needs a Sixth Win
The Irish have won ten of 11 games and own a 31-24 victory over Miami, now No. 7 in the CFP rankings. Their lone loss came at BYU, currently No. 10, making their strength of schedule more demanding than the win-loss column alone suggests. Notre Dame has won four consecutive games since that defeat, including 41-7 over Boston College and 24-3 over SMU. Those last two results particularly highlight the defense: the Mustangs were held out of the end zone, and Boston College scored only once. Notre Dame averages 38.1 points and 447.1 yards while allowing 12.8 and 247.5 respectively.
Syracuse’s record tells a less comfortable story, although its raw offensive production can be misleading. The Orange score 28.5 points and gain 437.5 yards per game, but they have needed 870 offensive plays to reach 4,812 yards, an average of 5.5 yards per play. Notre Dame has gained 4,918 yards in 742 plays, or 6.6 per snap. Syracuse has beaten North Carolina, SMU and NC State in ACC play but lost close games to California, Virginia and Clemson. The Orange average 26.1 points allowed and 358.7 defensive yards per contest. A sellout crowd for Senior Day helps Syracuse, yet its home record is only 2-3, compared with Notre Dame’s 2-1 mark in true road games.

Malachi Nelson Replaces Angeli Against an Irish Defense Allowing 247.5 Yards
Syracuse coach Fran Brown confirmed that quarterback Steve Angeli will miss the game with an AC-joint shoulder injury suffered against Boston College. Angeli, who previously played at Notre Dame, had completed 156 of 275 throws for 1,886 yards, ten touchdowns and six interceptions in eight appearances. Nelson is expected to start. His Syracuse season line stands at 54 completions in 96 attempts (56.3%) for 734 yards, four touchdowns and two interceptions over four appearances; he threw for 202 yards and a score after replacing Angeli against BC. Both of his previous starts, against Virginia and Louisville, ended in losses. That does not make an upset impossible, but it raises the level of execution Syracuse must achieve with an inexperienced replacement.
The most dangerous Orange playmaker is receiver Umari Hatcher, who has 77 catches for 1,257 yards and nine touchdowns, putting him among the national receiving leaders. His ability to turn an accurate intermediate throw into an explosive gain gives Syracuse a way to threaten a defense allowing only 158.5 passing yards per game. Running back Tylik Hill has 616 rushing yards, but Notre Dame’s front permits just 89.1 rushing yards per game and 3.0 per attempt. Three Factors: Nelson’s Protection, Red-Zone Scoring and Dome Possessions
Nelson against Notre Dame’s pressure:
Syracuse’s backup quarterback has completed 56.3% of his throws this season, and the Irish allow 12.8 points per game. A slow start or short-field interception could turn a competitive first quarter into a large deficit quickly.
Syracuse’s edge rush versus Carr’s protection:
Johnson and Adeleye have 15.5 combined sacks. They must prevent Carr from accessing an offense that gains 6.6 yards per play; a questionable Will Black makes the offensive tackle matchup worth monitoring.
Red-zone efficiency and possessions indoors:
Syracuse has only 25 touchdowns from 55 red-zone trips, compared with Notre Dame’s 32 from 46. The JMA Wireless Dome eliminates weather-driven passing concerns, but the Orange’s high play count and the Irish’s efficient rushing attack could still make possession management decisive.

Spread Lean: Notre Dame -25.5 Needs a Four-Touchdown Separation
Conditional spread lean: Notre Dame -25.5 (-110).
The favorite must win by at least 26 points. Carr’s efficient passing, the Irish’s 5.1-yard rushing average and Syracuse’s poor touchdown conversion in the red zone offer a path to that margin. Notre Dame’s defense allows only 247.5 yards and 12.8 points per game, and Nelson now has to replace an injured veteran against an opponent with a significantly stronger front than most of Syracuse’s schedule. The Irish have also held Boston College and SMU to a combined ten points in their last two outings. If Notre Dame opens an early two-touchdown lead, Nelson may be forced into more obvious passing downs and increased turnover risk.
The line has already moved considerably, so the value is not automatic. Syracuse is hosting a sold-out Senior Day game, Hatcher is a legitimate deep threat, and the Irish have significant defensive absences. The Orange have also beaten ranked visitors at the Dome under Fran Brown. A late Syracuse touchdown can spoil a large favorite’s cover even after an easy straight-up win. This is a small lean at -25.5 or a better number, not a recommendation to chase -27.5 or worse. Any further injury issues involving Carr, the running backs or the offensive line would require a fresh assessment.
Total Lean: Under 53 Depends on Holding Syracuse Near Ten Points

Syracuse’s 28.5-point average includes high-volume passing offense, but its red-zone touchdown rate is only 25 of 55 opportunities and it has just one game left to recover from its quarterback change. Notre Dame’s defense has allowed 12.8 points a contest and held both Boston College and SMU to single digits. The Irish can also drain possessions with James and Walton after building a lead. A game where Notre Dame scores in the high 30s or low 40s while Syracuse stays around ten points fits the Under without requiring snow or wind; this game is indoors.
There are substantial risks. The 2025 meeting finished 70-7, demonstrating Notre Dame’s ability to overwhelm this opponent when field position and turnovers break its way. Syracuse’s defense allows 26.1 points and can concede explosive runs, while Hatcher may exploit a depleted Irish secondary for a long touchdown. A defensive score or late points against reserves could push the result above 53 even if Nelson struggles. The Under is a marginal lean at 53 or higher, subject to offensive and defensive availability. A drop toward 51 would leave too little cushion to keep the same recommendation.
Final Prediction: Notre Dame 40, Syracuse 10 With the Irish Covering
Notre Dame is the clear straight-up choice because Carr has played efficiently, the rushing attack is balanced and the defense has conceded fewer than 13 points per game. Syracuse’s passing yardage and Hatcher’s explosiveness make the Orange more dangerous than a simple 5-6 record suggests, but Nelson’s limited starting experience and the mismatch in rushing efficiency are difficult to ignore.
