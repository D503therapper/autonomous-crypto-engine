For the first time in a long time, the
Tulane Green Wave
will hopefully look to have more strength offensively from an injury perspective
in a critical contest
against the North Texas Mean Green.
Every team is dealing with bumps and bruises, but it matters when injuries come in clusters to a position group.
And for as much as the Green Wave running back room was touted this offseason and has been strong for the team, it hasn't hit its full potential with the injuries that have hit that group.
Tulane was without running backs Johnnie Daniels and Jamauri McClure last weekend against the Rice Owls, and while Maurice Turner, Jaylin Lucas, and DJ Dugar were able to get it done, it's less effective without those two talented backs.
We've barely seen McClure much this year due the foot fracture he sustained in fall camp that took a while to heal, and that he unfortunately re-injured early against the UTSA Roadrunners.
Daniels got banged up against the Tulsa Golden Hurricane, and
without Daniels on the roster
, it's hard to see this team with a 5-5 record and a 3-3 slate in conference play.
McClure is trending in a good way as well. While not officially cleared this weekend like Daniels, McClure practiced this week and participated fully in team reps.
The only other time that the Wave have had everyone in the room healthy was against the Memphis Tigers, who they coincidentally beat.
Now, that was a game that was less defined by the ground game and more so by the six turnovers that the Tigers committed, but the point remains.
We heard quite a bit about the Superback role, a position designated for Lucas early in the year. Unfortunately, due to the injuries in that room, he's had to play more as a typical back – something that hasn't been entirely effective at his smaller stature but necessary to take the carries.
The return of Daniels and hopefully McClure should also allow them to use Lucas a bit more creatively. Lucas caught two grabs last weekend and should have had a long touchdown reception that was a bit overthrown.
Dugar also has been effective in limited reps, and impressively off the bench pretty cold given his rep count this year. He has a size advantage that can/t be taught.
If Tulane hopes to beat North Texas, they'll need to control the momentum and the clock. A full strength running back room is the best way to accomplish that.
